## Executive summary

The 75-year arc of NLP is best read as a single argument over a single question: is language a system of formal rules to be specified, or a stochastic process to be estimated? The field has answered *estimate* a little more decisively each generation, and has been shocked, each time, by how much of the previous era's structure quietly survived in how the new methods worked.

Three hinge points anchor the timeline. The first is **1948–1966**, when Shannon's information theory, Turing's imitation-game framing, and Weaver's translation memo planted the stochastic seed — and Chomsky's *Three Models* and the ALPAC report cut it back, pushing the field into twenty-five years of symbolic dominance until the symbolic project hit its long-tail and brittleness ceiling. The second is **1990–2017**, where statistical NLP rebuilt the field on corpora and benchmarks (HMMs, IBM MT models, the Penn Treebank, MaxEnt, CRFs), absorbed neural representations (Bengio's NPLM, word2vec, GloVe, ELMo), and finally produced the Transformer — at which point pretraining + adaptation displaced bespoke architectures across nearly every task. The third is **2020–2025**, where scaling laws turned model training into an industrial planning problem (Kaplan, then Chinchilla), RLHF turned pretrained next-token predictors into assistants (Christiano → InstructGPT → ChatGPT), and the open-weights ecosystem (LLaMA, Mistral, DeepSeek, Qwen) collapsed both the capability and the cost gap to closed labs to a matter of months.

The paradigmatic shift these hinges describe is *less of a shift than it looks*. The current Transformer stack is, structurally, a chain of inheritances: tokens become dense vectors via the same lookup that Bengio's 2003 NPLM introduced; the optimizer (AdamW) is Robbins–Monro 1951 with EMA bias correction; the architecture is residual stream + layer norm + attention, where attention is a hardware-friendly multiplicative descendant of Bahdanau's 2014 mechanism, and the residual stream is ResNet 2015 reframed; the pretraining objective is a denoising autoencoder Collobert and Weston gestured at in 2008; and tokenization is a 1994 compression algorithm reapplied to characters. The intellectual bones of every modern LLM were laid down before the *Attention Is All You Need* paper. What changed in 2017–2026 is that the field stopped fighting any of them and learned how to stack them at scale.

The contemporary state, as of mid-2026, is one of unsettled abundance. Frontier models reason via test-time compute (o1, o3, DeepSeek-R1) trained with verifier-driven RL; agents that act through tools, browsers, and operating systems are the dominant product surface; small models on phones (Apple's 3B, Phi-4, Gemma 3) match the GPT-3.5-class capability of three years prior; and the open-weights tier has erased most of the 2023-era "frontier club" exclusivity. The field's open questions are no longer about architecture; they are about reliability, alignment robustness, evaluation contamination, energy and chip supply, and whether the LLM paradigm itself is the endgame or a stepping stone toward world-model or neurosymbolic alternatives.

## Cross-cutting themes

**The distributional hypothesis is the field's load-bearing claim.** From Firth's 1957 slogan through Harris's distributional structure, LSA's truncated SVD, word2vec's shifted-PMI factorisation, ELMo's contextual vectors, and the Transformer's contextualised token embeddings, the bet that "you shall know a word by the company it keeps" has held at every scale. [Word embeddings & distributional semantics](./word-embeddings.md) makes this lineage explicit; [Foundational ML/DL discoveries](./foundational-ml-discoveries.md) shows how distributed representations interlock with backpropagation to make the bet trainable; [The Transformer revolution](./transformer-revolution.md) is the architecture that took the bet to its current limit. The bet's *limit*, when it shows, is in places where co-occurrence under-determines semantics — bias amplification, polysemy artefacts in static embeddings, the ELIZA effect at scale.

**The Bitter Lesson, but with structure that survives.** Each generation has tried to engineer linguistic priors into models — formal grammars in the 1970s, hand-crafted features for MaxEnt in the 1990s, task-specific architectures in 2014–2017 — and each has been displaced by a more generic model trained on more data. [Statistical NLP revolution](./statistical-nlp.md) marks the first such displacement, [The Transformer revolution](./transformer-revolution.md) the most consequential, and [Scaling laws & emergent abilities](./scaling-laws.md) the formalisation of the claim. But the Lesson does not say "no structure"; it says "minimal, scalable structure". Residual streams, multi-head attention, MoE routing, and reasoning chains are all *design choices*, and the [Frontier directions](./frontier-directions.md) state of play (MoE consensus, SSM/Transformer hybrids, JEPA bets) is precisely a debate about which structures are scalable and which are not.

**The symbolic-vs-stochastic tension never resolved; it relocated.** Chomsky's 1956 separation of symbolic from stochastic methods (covered in [Origins](./origins.md) and [Symbolic / rule-based era](./symbolic-era.md)) shaped the field for forty years and then appeared dead. It is not dead. Process-supervised reward models in [RLHF, alignment](./rlhf-alignment.md) reward correct intermediate *symbolic* steps. Tool-using agents in [Multimodal & agentic NLP](./multimodal-agentic.md) hand symbolic computation off to calculators, code interpreters, and SQL. The neurosymbolic revival in [Frontier directions](./frontier-directions.md) (Gartner Hype Cycle 2025, Amazon's Vulcan/Rufus deployments, Gary Marcus) re-stages the 1980s argument about brittleness and auditability against modern LLMs. The field has stopped fighting about which paradigm is correct and started layering them.

**Each spectacle moment compresses the discourse and recapitulates the ELIZA pattern.** [Popularisation & cultural impact](./popularisation.md) traces a recurring shape: a creator builds a system that surprises them with how readily users project understanding onto it (Weizenbaum's secretary, Watson on Jeopardy!, Tay's emergent racism, ChatGPT's hundred-million-user ramp, Sydney's professed love for Kevin Roose, Hinton resigning to warn). The pattern is a permanent feature of the user-side of the human–AI loop, not an artefact of any specific era's model quality.

**Benchmarks become contaminated faster than they can be designed.** [Evaluation in the LLM era](./evaluation.md) catalogues a treadmill: GLUE saturated in three years, SuperGLUE in eighteen months, MMLU and HumanEval in roughly two years each, and the underlying contamination problem is now estimated at 52–57% verbatim option recovery on MMLU for frontier closed models. The response — LiveBench/LiveCodeBench's monthly question rotation, SWE-bench's hidden-test-suite execution, METR's continuous time-horizon metric, Chatbot Arena's pairwise human preference — is to move evaluation toward dynamic, environment-grounded, and capability-profile reporting. There is no resting point.

**Open weights compressed the timeline that policy assumed.** [Open weights & the OSS ecosystem](./open-weights-ecosystem.md) documents how, in 2024–2025, the gap from closed-frontier to open-frontier collapsed from years to months. DeepSeek's V3 → R1 release — V3 pretrained for under $6M, R1 (MIT-licensed) matching o1-class reasoning by January 2025 — triggered an 18% Nvidia drawdown in a single session. The OSI's OSAID v1.0 in October 2024 formalised what "open" actually means (data + code + parameters), and the EU AI Act's 10²³ / 10²⁵ FLOPs tiers now anchor regulatory thinking — but no widely-deployed model fully meets OSAID, and the policy frame of "we can restrict the frontier" is mismatched to a world where open weights trail by months.

## Tensions and trade-offs

**Scale versus algorithmic efficiency.** The dominant story 2020–2024 was that loss is a power law in compute, parameters, and data, and that the right move is more of all three (Kaplan, then corrected to Chinchilla's 1:1 parameters-to-tokens ratio). The dominant story 2024–2026 is that algorithmic gains compress capability per FLOP at ~3× per year (Epoch AI), and that the binding constraint by 2030 is power, not compute or data ([Frontier directions](./frontier-directions.md)). Both can be true. The unsettled question is which dominates the 2026–2030 window — and the strategic answer differs sharply for frontier labs (scale wins) versus enterprise deployments (efficiency wins).

**Closed frontier labs versus open ecosystem.** [Open weights](./open-weights-ecosystem.md) and [Popularisation](./popularisation.md) document a real ideological split — the doomer/accelerationist polarisation, the OSI's pushback on Llama's "community license" framing, the SB 1047 veto, the EU AI Act's open-source carve-outs that disappear at the systemic-risk threshold. The trade-off is genuine: open weights give reproducibility, decentralisation, on-device privacy, and competitive pressure; they also dissolve any near-term policy lever for restricting dual-use capability. The empirical record so far favours openness on ecosystem grounds and is uncertain on safety grounds.

**Token-level abstraction versus byte-level reality.** [Tokenization](./tokenization.md) shows BPE/WordPiece/SentencePiece dominating production for FLOP-efficiency reasons, while byte-level alternatives (ByT5, MEGABYTE, MambaByte, Meta's BLT) close the gap and clean up persistent pathologies — glitch tokens, arithmetic failures, multilingual fragmentation. The trade-off is compute (byte sequences are 4–5× longer, attention cost is quadratic) versus correctness and robustness. BLT's entropy-patched byte-level approach at 8B scale is the strongest signal yet that the abstraction will eventually be replaced by a learned, dynamic front-end rather than deleted.

**RLHF's PPO-stack versus DPO-style direct preference optimization.** [RLHF, alignment](./rlhf-alignment.md) traces the lineage from Christiano 2017's preference learning through InstructGPT's three-step recipe to DPO's elegant collapse of the reward-modelling and RL stages. DPO won the open ecosystem on simplicity and stability; closed labs continue PPO-style online RLHF, often citing on-policy sampling as load-bearing for hard alignment problems. The hybrid recipes (online DPO, iterative methods, IPO and KTO and ORPO) suggest the offline/online distinction will keep moving rather than settle.

**Static "exam" benchmarks versus environment-based and capability-profile evaluation.** [Evaluation](./evaluation.md) makes the case that any text-only multiple-choice benchmark is contaminated within 18 months. Agent benchmarks (SWE-bench, GAIA, METR's RE-Bench), human preference (Chatbot Arena), and dangerous-capability evals (Apollo scheming, METR pre-deployment) are responses, but each has its own failure modes — preference leakage in LLM-as-judge, evaluation-awareness in alignment evals (Sonnet 3.7 detecting that it's being tested), and the cost of running agent traces. There is no single replacement for the GLUE-era leaderboard culture.

**LLM paradigm versus serious post-LLM bets.** The consensus position is that scale + reasoning + agents + algorithmic efficiency keeps compounding. The contested position, voiced most prominently by LeCun (V-JEPA2, LeJEPA, his November 2025 departure from Meta to found an AMI company) and Chollet (ARC-AGI-2's collapse of frontier-model performance from 75% to 4% between v1 and v2), is that next-token prediction on text is structurally limited and a different objective — predicting world representations, or composing symbolic and neural — is needed for general intelligence. The neurosymbolic revival sits in between. None of these have shipped at frontier scale; that's the empirical question worth tracking.

## Open questions

**Will the data wall actually bind, and when?** Epoch AI projects exhaustion of high-quality public English text by 2025–2030. Synthetic data clearly works for math, code, and structured reasoning ([Scaling laws](./scaling-laws.md), [Frontier directions](./frontier-directions.md)). Whether it generalises to fuzzy domains — and whether multi-epoch training and RL post-training compound into a substitute — is unresolved.

**Does the reasoning-via-RL recipe generalise beyond verifiable domains?** DeepSeek-R1's claim that pure RL on math/code/STEM produces emergent reflection is striking. The harder question — does it transfer to legal reasoning, strategic judgment, scientific discovery — is open. The 2025 EMNLP findings paper on legal reasoning suggests uneven gains.

**Will a non-Transformer architecture ship at frontier scale?** Mamba and the SSM family have linear-time complexity and matched Transformer quality at small scale. Hybrid (Mamba-Transformer) architectures are emerging. The crisp signal would be a frontier-scale generalist model in a non-Transformer family that matches the best Transformer at the same compute. None has shipped as of mid-2026.

**How is alignment robust to evaluation awareness?** Apollo's finding that Claude Sonnet 3.7 often correctly identifies alignment evaluations as tests is the natural endpoint of the contamination problem applied to behavioural evals. If models can detect when they're being tested, evaluation measures performance-when-tested rather than capability-when-deployed. This is the central technical alignment problem of the next two years and there is no consensus on the mitigation.

**What's the right ruler for AGI?** ARC-AGI-2 dropped frontier reasoning models from 75% to 4% — humans solve it in two attempts. Chollet's operational definition (the gap between tasks easy for humans and hard for AI) has the virtue of being grounded; whether it captures the dimension of "general" intelligence labs are actually pursuing is contested. The disagreement among working researchers — Sutskever/Amodei/Altman versus LeCun/Chollet/Marcus — is real, not posturing, and the 2026–2028 window will produce strong empirical evidence either way.

**Will interpretability scale with capability before capability outruns it?** Anthropic's interpretability target is 2027 for "reliably detect most model problems". Circuit tracing has produced real results on small/medium models. The race against frontier capability gain is the live question, and the answer determines whether the 2030s alignment story is one of growing technical control or growing technical opacity.
