# Marvel Crossover Events Timeline (1982–2026)

A chronological structural map of Marvel's line-wide crossovers from the format's invention through the events current as of 2026. The focus is on what each event *did* to continuity — institutions reshaped, cosmologies expanded, status quos overturned — rather than blow-by-blow plot. Events appear in publication-year order; lasting impact is judged by whether the result is still readable in the 2026 line.

## 1. The Format Is Invented (1982–1984)

Marvel's "company-wide event" was not a deliberate publishing strategy at first — it was a toy deal and a mis-shelved manuscript.

**Marvel Super Hero Contest of Champions (1982)** is the first Marvel limited series and a structural prototype for the crossover. Mark Gruenwald, John Romita Jr., and Pablo Marcos delivered a three-issue series originally written to tie into the 1980 Olympics; when those plans collapsed, Marvel re-tooled it into a Grandmaster-vs.-Death cosmic gambit and shipped it June–August 1982 [T3] Wikipedia, "Marvel Super Hero Contest of Champions," https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marvel_Super_Hero_Contest_of_Champions. Fan retrospectives now frame it as "the precursor to the concept of the Crisis Crossover" rather than a true line-wide event, because tie-ins did not propagate into the ongoing line [T3] TV Tropes, "Contest of Champions 1982," https://tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/ComicBook/ContestOfChampions1982.

**Marvel Super Heroes Secret Wars (1984–1985)** is the foundational line-wide event. Twelve issues, written by Jim Shooter, drawn by Mike Zeck and Bob Layton. The premise is famously thin: the Beyonder transports Marvel's premier heroes and villains to a constructed Battleworld and tells them to fight [T3] Marvel Database, "Secret Wars (1984 Event)," https://marvel.fandom.com/wiki/Secret_Wars_(1984_Event). The publishing context is more interesting than the plot: Mattel had asked Marvel for a story big enough to anchor a toyline, and what Marvel produced "sold more copies than any other comic in the previous 25 years" [T3] CBR, "Marvel's Secret Wars - How Comics' First Event Changed Everything," https://www.cbr.com/marvels-secret-wars-how-comics-first-event-changed-everything/. The structural fallout is what matters: Spider-Man returned in his black costume (the seed for Venom and the entire symbiote corner of the Marvel Universe), She-Hulk replaced the Thing in the Fantastic Four, and Magneto was repositioned as an X-Men ally rather than antagonist [T3] Wikipedia, "Secret Wars," https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secret_Wars. From this point, an annual summer crossover becomes a permanent feature of Marvel's publishing calendar.

## 2. The Late-80s Crossover Boom

With the format proven commercially, Marvel iterated through three structural patterns: the family-of-titles event (Inferno), the annuals-only event (Atlantis Attacks), and the villain-of-the-week brand event (Acts of Vengeance).

**Inferno (1988–89)** centers on the X-Men line — *Uncanny X-Men*, *X-Factor*, *X-Terminators*, *Excalibur*, *New Mutants* — but spreads to every Marvel title set in or near New York [T3] Wikipedia, "Inferno (Marvel Comics)," https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inferno_(Marvel_Comics). A demonic invasion of Manhattan literalizes hell on earth; Madelyne Pryor becomes the Goblin Queen and Illyana Rasputin completes her transformation into Darkchylde [T3] Marvel Database, "Inferno (Event)," https://marvel.fandom.com/wiki/Inferno_(Event). Inferno is structurally the first true X-crossover — multi-title, single coherent arc — and CBR's retrospective frames it as "the first and largest all-hands-on-deck Marvel Comics invasion, in what has now become an event staple, from Secret Invasion to Empyre to King in Black" [T3] Comic Book Herald, "X-Men: Inferno Reading Order," https://www.comicbookherald.com/the-complete-marvel-reading-order-guide/x-men-inferno-reading-order/.

**Atlantis Attacks (1989)** ran across the summer annuals only — a 14-part event published exclusively as Annual one-shots [T3] Wikipedia, "Atlantis Attacks," https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atlantis_Attacks. The Lemurian priest Ghaur and Llyra ally with Atlantean warlord Attuma to resurrect Set, the seven-headed Elder God [T3] Marvel Database, "Atlantis Attacks," https://marvel.fandom.com/wiki/Atlantis_Attacks. Structurally important for one reason: it moved the Elder Gods (Set, Chthon, et al.) from obscure backmatter into operational continuity, anchoring later mystical-cosmology stories. The Annual-only delivery format never became dominant but recurred (Evolutionary War 1988, Subterranean Wars 1991).

**Acts of Vengeance (1989–90)** is the cleanest high-concept event of the era: Loki, posing as an unnamed broker, convinces six villainous "Prime Movers" — Dr. Doom, Magneto, the Kingpin, the Wizard, the Mandarin, and the Red Skull — to swap antagonists with each other [T3] Wikipedia, "Acts of Vengeance," https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acts_of_Vengeance. The result is a giant matrix of mismatched fights (Spider-Man vs. Graviton, Daredevil vs. Ultron) [T3] TV Tropes, "Acts of Vengeance 1989," https://tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/ComicBook/ActsOfVengeance1989. Lasting impact is mostly atmospheric — it's the high-water mark of the "shared villain pool" model that the 90s would later replace with the singular-cosmic-threat model.

## 3. Cosmic Power and the Infinity Trilogy (1991–1993)

**The Infinity Gauntlet (1991)** by Jim Starlin, George Pérez, and Ron Lim is the structural template for cosmic Marvel for the next 30 years. The six-issue series picks up from *The Thanos Quest*: Thanos has assembled all six Infinity Gems into a single weapon and used it, at Death's behest, to wipe out half of all life in the universe [T3] Wikipedia, "The Infinity Gauntlet," https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Infinity_Gauntlet. The miniseries was a top seller and "spawned many sequels, most directly Infinity War and Infinity Crusade, in addition to many 'Infinity'-themed stories and events that continue to this day" [T3] Wikipedia, "The Infinity Gauntlet," https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Infinity_Gauntlet. It also fixed Thanos as Marvel's apex cosmic villain, a position he holds without serious challenge until Knull (2018+) and even then only partially. Its later structural significance is enormous: Infinity Gauntlet is the single comic with the largest influence on the Marvel Cinematic Universe's Infinity Saga (2008–2019) [T3] Inverse, "Oral History of the Infinity Gauntlet," https://www.inverse.com/entertainment/infinity-gauntlet-comic-oral-history.

The two follow-ups — *The Infinity War* (1992) and *The Infinity Crusade* (1993) — settle the cosmic-event cadence. From this point forward, Marvel runs a "cosmic" event approximately every 1–3 years, separately from its earthbound line-wide event.

## 4. The Mid-90s Reboot Mechanics: Age of Apocalypse and Onslaught

**Age of Apocalypse (1995)** is the first time a Marvel event temporarily *replaces* the main universe. Legion travels back to kill Magneto, kills Charles Xavier instead, and Apocalypse rises a decade early in the divergent timeline [T3] Wikipedia, "Age of Apocalypse," https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Age_of_Apocalypse. For four months, the entire X-Men line is replaced by AoA-branded miniseries (*Astonishing X-Men*, *Generation Next*, *Weapon X*, *Factor X*, etc.) bookended by *X-Men Alpha* and *X-Men Omega* [T3] Wikipedia, "Age of Apocalypse," https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Age_of_Apocalypse. The reality is later codified as Earth-295. AoA establishes two precedents Marvel uses repeatedly afterward: (a) line-wide title replacement as an event mechanism, and (b) the elevation of an alternate Earth into a permanent, recurring continuity node.

**Onslaught (1996)** is the structural pivot of late-90s Marvel. Onslaught is a psionic entity born from the merged subconscious darknesses of Charles Xavier and Magneto [T3] Wikipedia, "Onslaught (Marvel Comics)," https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Onslaught_(Marvel_Comics). His apparent victory kills the Avengers and the Fantastic Four; Franklin Richards saves them by exiling them to a pocket universe, and Marvel relaunches their books as the **Heroes Reborn** line — outsourced to Jim Lee and Rob Liefeld in an attempt to address declining sales on Avengers and FF titles while Spider-Man and X-Men remained healthy [T3] Wikipedia, "Heroes Reborn (1996 comic)," https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heroes_Reborn_(1996_comic). Onslaught is the first time an event is used as the *literal mechanism* for a line relaunch; it sets the template that Disassembled (2004), Civil War (2006), and Secret Wars (2015) will all follow.

## 5. The Bendis-Era Decade (2004–2012)

The 2004–2012 stretch is dominated by Brian Michael Bendis, Marvel's editorial center of gravity through the period. The events form a single rolling continuity: each one's resolution sets up the next.

**Avengers Disassembled (2004)** breaks the team. **House of M (2005)**, by Bendis and Olivier Coipel, is the consequence: Wanda Maximoff, mind broken, rewrites reality into a mutant-ruled world ruled by Magneto's House of M. When the heroes pierce the illusion, Wanda utters the line "No More Mutants," and reality snaps back — but with the global mutant population reduced from hundreds of thousands to roughly 200, "roughly 90% of the mutant population [stripped] of their powers" [T3] Wikipedia, "House of M," https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_of_M. CBR notes it "changed the dynamic of X-Men comics for nearly a decade" [T3] CBR, "House of M: How the Classic X-Men Event Still Affects the Marvel Universe," https://www.cbr.com/x-men-house-of-m-marvel-universe-affect/. House of M's "Decimation" is the load-bearing premise of every X-event from then until AvX in 2012.

**Annihilation (2006)** is run in parallel as a cosmic-only event. Annihilus' Annihilation Wave breaks out of the Negative Zone; "within 3 months of the fall of Chandilar, the Annihilation Wave had nearly eradicated the Shi'ar... killed Galactus and slaughtered the Celestials," reduced the Nova Corps to a single survivor (Richard Rider), and destroyed Xandar [T3] Marvel Database, "Annihilation Wave (Earth-616)," https://marvel.fandom.com/wiki/Annihilation_Wave_(Earth-616). Annihilation "is one of the most important events in Marvel history, and it eventually led to the formation of the Guardians of the Galaxy" [T3] CBR, "Annihilation: What Happened in Marvel's Cosmic War?," https://www.cbr.com/annihilation-marvel-crossover-event-guide/. It is the rebuilding of the Marvel cosmic line — DnA's Guardians, Nova, and the Cancerverse stories of 2008–2013 all flow from it.

**Civil War (2006–07)**, by Mark Millar and Steve McNiven, splits the hero community over the Superhuman Registration Act after the New Warriors trigger a civilian massacre in Stamford. Cap leads the resistance; Iron Man leads enforcement [T3] Wikipedia, "Civil War (comics)," https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civil_War_(comics). Millar framed the event as forcing Marvel's heroes "to grow up," replacing the "wild west fantasy these guys have been having, where they put on a mask and fight whoever they like" [T3] Wikipedia, "Civil War (comics)," https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civil_War_(comics). The reshape lasts: SHRA + the Fifty States Initiative become the institutional shape of Marvel's hero community for three years until Siege.

**World War Hulk (2007)** is the payoff to *Planet Hulk* (2006): the Illuminati exiled Hulk to space, his ship crashed on Sakaar, and after a brief reign as Sakaar's emperor Hulk returns to Earth to break each Illuminati member [T3] Wikipedia, "World War Hulk," https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_Hulk. Lasting impact is mostly atmospheric, but the Illuminati structure itself (Iron Man, Mr. Fantastic, Black Bolt, Namor, Doctor Strange, Professor X) becomes a recurring institutional node — most importantly resurrected for Hickman's *Avengers* run that culminates in Secret Wars 2015.

**Secret Invasion (2008)**, by Bendis and Leinil Yu, reveals that Skrulls have been replacing key heroes for years. Promoted with the tag "Who do you trust?" — Bendis stated the Skrulls' motivation came directly from Annihilation: their empire was destroyed, and they believed Earth "is religiously and rightfully theirs" [T3] Wikipedia, "Secret Invasion," https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secret_Invasion. When Norman Osborn shoots the Skrull queen on live TV, "Dark Reign" begins — Osborn takes over national security, founds H.A.M.M.E.R. as a SHIELD replacement, and assembles the Dark Avengers [T3] Wikipedia, "Dark Reign (comics)," https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dark_Reign_(comics).

**Siege (2010)** ends Dark Reign. Loki manipulates Osborn into a full-scale assault on Asgard (then floating above Broxton, Oklahoma) [T3] Wikipedia, "Siege (comics)," https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siege_(comics). Osborn's madness becomes public; the U.S. government dismantles HAMMER, repeals the SHRA, dissolves the Initiative, and appoints Steve Rogers as the new top cop. CBR's retrospective: "The Avengers have reassembled, and the Heroic Age begins" [T3] CBR, "Avengers: How Asgard's Siege Kickstarted Marvel's Heroic Age," https://www.cbr.com/avengers-siege-led-to-marvels-heroic-age/. The seven-year Bendis arc (Disassembled → House of M → Civil War → Secret Invasion → Dark Reign → Siege) closes here; it is arguably the longest sustained continuity arc in Marvel publishing history.

**Avengers vs. X-Men (AvX, 2012)** is the full reset of the post-House-of-M mutant status quo. The Phoenix Force returns to Earth, divides into the Phoenix Five (Cyclops, Emma, Namor, Colossus, Magik), and Hope Summers eventually absorbs and destroys it [T3] Wikipedia, "Avengers vs. X-Men," https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Avengers_vs._X-Men. With Wanda's help she "destroyed the Phoenix Force with a final blast of energy which caused millions of people around Earth to manifest mutant powers" [T3] Marvel Database, "Avengers vs. X-Men (Event)," https://marvel.fandom.com/wiki/Avengers_vs._X-Men_(Event). The Decimation is reversed; Cyclops becomes a Magneto-aligned revolutionary; Marvel NOW! launches as the post-AvX reset.

## 6. The Hickman Reset: Infinity, Original Sin, Secret Wars 2015

**Infinity (2013)**, by Jonathan Hickman, runs two stories in parallel: the Avengers (off-planet) versus the Builders, a "powerful race of god-like multidimensional beings"; and on Earth, Black Panther, Iron Man, and Black Bolt facing Thanos and his Black Order [T3] Wikipedia, "Infinity (comic book)," https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infinity_(comic_book). Two structural fallouts: the Black Order becomes a permanent fixture (later crucial to the MCU's *Infinity War*), and Black Bolt detonates a Terrigen Bomb, releasing Terrigen Mist into Earth's atmosphere and triggering the rise of the NuHumans — Marvel editorial's failed attempt at a mutant alternative ("Inhumanity") [T3] Wikipedia, "Infinity (comic book)," https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infinity_(comic_book).

**Original Sin (2014)**, by Jason Aaron and Mike Deodato, opens with the murder of Uatu the Watcher [T3] Wikipedia, "Original Sin (comics)," https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Original_Sin_(comics). The investigation reveals Nick Fury has, for decades, been "the watcher on the wall" — secretly killing alien threats before the public could see them [T3] Marvel Database, "Original Sin (Event)," https://marvel.fandom.com/wiki/Original_Sin_(Event). At the close, Fury absorbs Uatu's powers and becomes "The Unseen," chained to the Moon. The structural payoff is the introduction of Nick Fury Jr. as the operational SHIELD director, freeing the original Fury for cosmic stories.

**Secret Wars (2015)**, by Hickman and Esad Ribić, is the largest structural event since 1984. Building on the "Time Runs Out" arc, the Marvel multiverse collapses: every universe's Earth (616, Ultimate/1610, 2099, AoA, 1602, House of M, etc.) is fused into a single Battleworld ruled by God-Emperor Doom, after Doom seizes the Beyonders' power via Molecule Man [T3] Wikipedia, "Secret Wars (2015 comic book)," https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secret_Wars_(2015_comic_book). At the close, "the Marvel Multiverse was completely restored with a few slight alterations, with the most visible of these changes being the incorporation of Miles Morales and Ultimate Reed Richards into the main Marvel Universe" [T3] Wikipedia, "Secret Wars (2015 comic book)," https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secret_Wars_(2015_comic_book). The Battleworld reality is later catalogued as Earth-15513. Structurally: the Ultimate Universe (1610) ends, Miles Morales becomes part of 616, and the All-New All-Different relaunch reorganizes the line.

## 7. Late 2010s: The "Civil War II → Secret Empire" Era

**Civil War II (2016)**, by Bendis and David Marquez, fights the precognitive-Inhuman Ulysses Cain question: do you act on probabilistic visions of the future? Captain Marvel says yes; Iron Man says no. War Machine and She-Hulk are casualties; Ulysses eventually evolves beyond human form and ascends [T3] Wikipedia, "Civil War II," https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civil_War_II. The political reshape is much narrower than CW1's, but it sets up Tony Stark's coma exit and the brief Riri Williams / Doctor Doom replacement era.

**Secret Empire (2017)** revealed Steve Rogers as a Cosmic-Cube-rewritten Hydra agent and is one of Marvel's most controversial events; **Generations (2017)** and **Marvel Legacy (2017)** restored the legacy numbering. None of these reshape continuity at the cosmic/institutional level the way the earlier events do, and the period reads as table-clearing for the Krakoan reboot.

## 8. Symbiote Cosmology and the Krakoan Era (2018–2024)

**King in Black (2020–21)**, by Donny Cates and Ryan Stegman, is the culmination of Cates' multi-year *Venom* arc and 2019's *Absolute Carnage*. Knull, the elder god of the void who created the Klyntar/symbiotes and forged All-Black the Necrosword, finally reaches Earth with his symbiote dragon army [T3] Wikipedia, "Knull," https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Knull. Eddie Brock, channeling the Uni-Power, defeats Knull in *King in Black* #5 [T3] Marvel.com, "Knull (Earth-616)," https://www.marvel.com/articles/comics/knull-god-symbiotes-klyntar-history-explained. Structurally, Knull retroactively becomes the cosmological origin of Marvel's symbiote line — a backwards retcon that makes the black costume from Secret Wars 1984 the seed of a cosmic mythology that didn't exist in 1984.

**Empyre (2020)**, by Al Ewing and Dan Slott, finally resolves the Kree–Skrull War: the two empires unite under Hulkling against the plant-based Cotati. The post-event Kree/Skrull Alliance, with its capital Throneworld II built on Hala's ruins, replaces both prior empires [T3] Wikipedia, "Empyre," https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Empyre.

The dominant publishing project of the 2019–2024 stretch is Hickman's **Krakoan Age**, kicked off by *House of X / Powers of X* (2019). Its events drive the X-Men line:

- **X of Swords (2020)** is the first major Krakoan crossover: Apocalypse, the X-Men, and Otherworld's Omniversal Majestrix Saturnyne are drawn into a 10-champion-per-side tournament between Krakoa and the rediscovered sister-island Arakko. The structural reveal is that Krakoa was once part of a larger landmass, Okkara, split during an ancient invasion from the demonic realm of Amenth [T3] Wikipedia, "X of Swords," https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/X_of_Swords. The event also establishes a critical resurrection-protocol limit: "if someone gets killed in the tournament, that version of them is gone for good" [T3] Marvel Database, "X of Swords," https://marvel.fandom.com/wiki/X_of_Swords.
- **Hellfire Gala (2021–2023)** functions as Krakoa's annual structural pulse — "an official state dinner, the election of a new X-Men team, a celebrity-packed party, and a showcase for mutant high fashion" [T3] Marvel.com, "The Hellfire Gala, Explained," https://www.marvel.com/articles/comics/what-is-the-x-men-hellfire-gala-history-in-the-comics. The 2021 Gala is the structural milestone: Magneto and several Omega-level mutants terraform Mars and rename it Arakko, giving mutantkind a sovereign planet [T3] Wikipedia, "Hellfire Gala," https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hellfire_Gala.
- **A.X.E.: Judgment Day (2022)**, by Kieron Gillen and Valerio Schiti, pits Avengers, X-Men, and Eternals against each other after the Eternals discover that mutants are a species of "Excess Deviation" [T3] Wikipedia, "A.X.E.: Judgment Day," https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A.X.E.:_Judgment_Day. Uranos kills key characters during the event; Magneto destroys his Cerebro back-up so he cannot be resurrected [T3] Marvel.com, "What 'A.X.E.: Judgment Day' Means for the Marvel Universe," https://www.marvel.com/articles/comics/axe-judgment-day-fallout-repercussions-avengers-x-men-eternals. Mutant resurrection becomes public knowledge — a massive shift in how baseline humanity perceives Krakoa.
- **Fall of X (2023–2024)** ends the era. Orchis (Nimrod, Omega Sentinel, a Moira MacTaggert revealed to be playing both sides, Feilong) ambushes the third Hellfire Gala in July 2023, killing Jean Grey, Dazzler, Cannonball, Frenzy, Prodigy, Jubilee, and others without resurrection back-up [T3] Marvel.com, "Krakoa No More? Fall of X," https://www.marvel.com/articles/comics/x-men-fall-of-x-trailer-orchis-plan-krakoa-downfall. The era closes in the dual miniseries *Fall of the House of X* and *Rise of the Powers of X* (Jan–May 2024) [T3] Wikipedia, "Fall of X," https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fall_of_X. The relaunch *X-Men: From the Ashes* resets the line outside Krakoa.

## 9. The Doom Era (2024–2026)

**Blood Hunt (2024)**, by Jed MacKay and Pepe Larraz, is a vampire-apocalypse line-wide event: an artificial darkness covers Earth and the Vampire Nation strikes globally, led by the Bloodcoven (Megrim, Cruel, Unusual, the Damascene, Smoke Eater, and Bloodstorm One — a Hydra-grown Dracula clone) [T3] Wikipedia, "Blood Hunt (Marvel Comics)," https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blood_Hunt_(Marvel_Comics). Its real structural function is to set up the next event: at Blood Hunt's close, Doctor Strange transfers the title of Sorcerer Supreme to Doctor Doom in exchange for the magical force needed to end the vampire surge [T3] Marvel.com, "SDCC 2025 - One World Under Doom," https://www.marvel.com/DoomSDCC.

**One World Under Doom (2025–2026)**, by Ryan North and R.B. Silva, is the current line-wide event as of writing. Doom — now Sorcerer Supreme — magically commandeers every broadcast medium on Earth and declares himself Emperor of a "new United Latveria," with Earth's heads of state apparently complying [T3] Wikipedia, "One World Under Doom," https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/One_World_Under_Doom. Marvel publicly framed it as the company's "biggest changes to its status quo since 2008's Dark Reign" [T3] CBR, "One World Under Doom, Explained," https://www.cbr.com/one-world-under-doom-comic-event-history-details/. The event runs nine issues plus tie-ins across *Superior Avengers*, *Amazing Spider-Man*, *Doctor Strange of Asgard*, *Red Hulk*, *Iron Man*, *Fantastic Four*, and *Storm*. Editorially, it is also load-bearing for the MCU's *Avengers: Doomsday* film.

## 10. Structural Patterns Across the Timeline

A few patterns are visible only when the events are laid out chronologically:

1. **The cadence accelerates.** From 1984 to 1996 there is roughly one line-wide event every 1–2 years; from 2004 onward there are usually two parallel tracks (an Earth event and a cosmic event); from 2019 onward the Krakoan era runs its own internal event cadence on top of the company-wide events.
2. **Events are increasingly used as relaunch mechanisms.** Onslaught (1996) → Heroes Reborn; AvX (2012) → Marvel NOW!; Secret Wars (2015) → All-New All-Different; Fall of X (2024) → From the Ashes; One World Under Doom (2025–26) → presumably the next reset. The 1980s pattern of "event resolves, line continues" has been largely replaced.
3. **Cosmologies are reverse-built.** Knull retconned the symbiotes from "weird alien costume" (1984) into "creation of an elder void god." The Builders (2013) retroactively explained earlier cosmic anomalies. Fury's "Unseen" status (2014) added a hidden layer of Marvel's espionage history. The trend is toward backward elaboration: each big event tries to re-explain, not just extend, the cosmology.
4. **Institutional state churns on a 5-year clock.** SHRA (2006) → repealed by Siege (2010) → SHIELD restructured in Original Sin (2014) → SHIELD dissolved post-Secret Empire (2017) → Krakoan sovereignty (2019) → terminated by Fall of X (2024) → replaced by Doom's regime (2025+). No institutional configuration in Marvel survives more than about half a decade.
