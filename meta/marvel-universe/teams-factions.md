# Major Teams & Factions of the Marvel Universe

A structural map of the principal teams and institutions of Earth-616, with notes on MCU (Earth-199999) divergences where the institutional shape differs materially. Focus is on **formation, era-defining arcs, and current/historical status** — not character biographies. Citations follow the brief: priority is Type 2 (official Marvel / Wikipedia-grade reference) primary, Type 3 (fan wikis, podcasts, news sites) secondary.

## 1. The Avengers and Their Roster Eras

The Avengers were created by Stan Lee and Jack Kirby in *The Avengers* #1 (Sept. 1963). The five founders were Thor, Iron Man, Hulk, Ant-Man (Hank Pym), and the Wasp; Captain America was thawed in issue #4 and granted retroactive founder status as Hulk's structural replacement when the latter quit almost immediately [T2] Marvel.com, "Avengers In Comics" overview, https://www.marvel.com/teams-and-groups/avengers/in-comics; [T3] Wikipedia, "List of Avengers members," https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Avengers_members.

Key roster eras, treated as discrete institutional configurations:

- **"Cap's Kooky Quartet" (1965, *Avengers* #16):** the founders rotate out and Captain America leads a team of reformed villains — Hawkeye, Quicksilver, Scarlet Witch — establishing the precedent of Avengers as a *redemption mechanism* as much as a hero team [T3] Wikipedia, "List of Avengers members."
- **West Coast Avengers (1984):** chairman Vision proposed a second branch to absorb roster overflow; Hawkeye and Mockingbird were dispatched to Los Angeles in a four-issue limited series that became a 102-issue ongoing. Original lineup: Hawkeye, Mockingbird, Wonder Man, Tigra, and War Machine (under the Iron Man identity) [T3] Wikipedia, "West Coast Avengers," https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Coast_Avengers; [T2] Marvel.com, "Avengers West Coast," https://www.marvel.com/teams-and-groups/avengers-west-coast.
- **Disassembled (2004) and the New Avengers (2005):** Brian Michael Bendis's *Avengers Disassembled* destroyed the team via a Scarlet Witch psychotic break, and the *New Avengers* #1 (Jan. 2005, with David Finch) reformed it after a Raft prison breakout drew together Captain America, Iron Man, Luke Cage, Spider-Man, Spider-Woman, and Daredevil [T3] Wikipedia, "Avengers Disassembled," https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Avengers_Disassembled; [T3] Wikipedia, "New Avengers," https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Avengers. This issue is the structural pivot of the modern Avengers — it expanded the team from a fixed superhero club to a sprawling franchise of concurrent rosters.
- **Mighty Avengers (2007):** Tony Stark's "official" post-*Civil War* team (Ms. Marvel, Wonder Man, Black Widow, Sentry, Wasp, Ares), running in parallel with the now-fugitive New Avengers.
- **Dark Avengers (2009):** after *Secret Invasion*, Norman Osborn — having killed Skrull queen Veranke — replaced S.H.I.E.L.D. with H.A.M.M.E.R. and assembled a team of villains posing as known heroes (Bullseye as Hawkeye, Daken as Wolverine, Venom as Spider-Man, Moonstone as Ms. Marvel, Noh-Varr as Captain Marvel, plus Ares, Sentry, and Osborn himself as the Iron Patriot). The team appeared in *Dark Avengers* #1 (Jan. 2009) and was dissolved after *Siege* (2010) [T3] Wikipedia, "Dark Avengers," https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dark_Avengers; [T3] CBR, "Dark Reign: How Norman Osborn Took Over the Marvel Universe," https://www.cbr.com/dark-reign-norman-osborn-dark-avengers/.
- **Uncanny Avengers (2012):** Rick Remender's post-*AvX* fusion of Avengers and X-Men — the institutional answer to decades of mutant alienation; Captain America's "Avengers Unity Squad."
- **Current era (2024–2026):** the Avengers under Jed MacKay's run (launched 2023) operate with Captain Marvel as chair alongside Thor, Iron Man, Sam Wilson Captain America, Black Panther, Vision, and Scarlet Witch, occupying Impossible City as a base [T2] Marvel.com, "Avengers In Comics."

## 2. The X-Men and Their Offshoots

The original X-Men (Lee/Kirby, *X-Men* #1, Sept. 1963) framed mutants as the "next stage of human evolution" — a structural premise that has spawned more spin-off teams than any other Marvel franchise.

- **New Mutants (1982):** Claremont and Sienkiewicz/McLeod's teen-trainee successors — Cannonball, Mirage, Wolfsbane, Sunspot, Karma, later Magik, Magma, Cypher, Warlock. Functioned as the X-Men's pedagogical layer and was the seedbed from which X-Force was grown [T3] Wikipedia, "X-Force," https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/X-Force.
- **X-Factor (1986):** the five original X-Men reunited as a public mutant-hunter front that secretly rescued mutants. Later refactored into the federal X-Factor team led by Havok with Polaris, Madrox, Strong Guy, Quicksilver, and Wolfsbane (Peter David, 1991), which became the durable institutional template — a government task force, later a private detective agency in *X-Factor Investigations* [T2] Marvel.com, "X-Factor," https://www.marvel.com/teams-and-groups/x-factor; [T3] Wikipedia, "X-Factor (comics)," https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/X-Factor_(comics).
- **Excalibur (1987):** Claremont/Davis's UK-based team in *Excalibur Special Edition* #1 — Captain Britain, Meggan, Shadowcat, Nightcrawler, and Rachel Summers (Phoenix). Created to handle interdimensional/Otherworld threats and to absorb post-*Mutant Massacre* X-Men fallout [T3] Wikipedia, "Excalibur (comics)," https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Excalibur_(comics).
- **X-Force (1991):** Rob Liefeld's *New Mutants* #100 finale converted the trainee team into a paramilitary strike force under Cable. X-Force has cycled across generations (Pete Wisdom's covert Brit team in 2008, Kyle/Yost's black-ops Wolverine team), and its core identity remains "the X-team that does what the X-Men can't" [T3] Wikipedia, "X-Force."
- **Quiet Council of Krakoa (2019–2024):** the structural climax of mutant institution-building. Designed by Xavier and Magneto in *House of X* #6 (Dec. 2019, Hickman/Larraz), the Council was a 12-seat governing body for the sovereign mutant nation of Krakoa. Seats were divided into four "seasons" (Spring/Summer/Autumn/Winter) and distributed across factional lines: tradition leaders (Xavier, Magneto, Apocalypse), Hellfire Trading Co. (Emma Frost, Sebastian Shaw, Kate Pryde), former X-Men loyalists (Storm, Nightcrawler, Jean Grey), and former extremists (Mystique, Exodus, Mister Sinister). The Council established Krakoan law — first decree: "No mutant may take a human life" — judging Sabretooth as their first case [T2] Marvel.com, "Krakoa's Quiet Council, Explained," https://www.marvel.com/articles/comics/x-men-krakoa-quiet-council-members-explained; [T3] Marvel Database, "Quiet Council of Krakoa (Earth-616)," https://marvel.fandom.com/wiki/Quiet_Council_of_Krakoa_(Earth-616).
- **Krakoan Age phases:** Dawn of X (2019) → Reign of X (2020) → Destiny of X (2022) → Fall of X (2023). The era was concluded by the dual miniseries *Fall of the House of X* and *Rise of the Powers of X* (Jan.–May 2024), with *X-Men* #35 / legacy *Uncanny X-Men* #700 (June 2024) marking the official end [T3] Wikipedia, "Krakoan Age," https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Krakoan_Age; [T2] Marvel.com, "What You Need to Know for *Fall of the House of X* and *Rise of the Powers of X*," https://www.marvel.com/articles/comics/fall-of-the-house-of-x-rise-of-the-powers-of-x-what-you-need-to-know.
- **From the Ashes (2024–):** the post-Krakoa relaunch returns to the dispersed-team model. Three concurrent X-Men books split the franchise: *X-Men* (vol. 7, MacKay/Stegman) under Cyclops in Alaska with Magneto, Beast, Kwannon, Quentin Quire, Juggernaut, Magik, and Temper; *Uncanny X-Men* (vol. 6, Simone/Marquez) with Rogue and Gambit in New Orleans alongside Nightcrawler, Wolverine, Jubilee; *Exceptional X-Men* (Ewing/Carnero) with Kate Pryde mentoring new mutants in Chicago. Spin-off titles include *NYX*, *Phoenix*, *Storm*, *Wolverine*, and a new *X-Factor* with Angel and Havok positioned as mutant influencers [T3] Wikipedia, "X-Men: From the Ashes," https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/X-Men:_From_the_Ashes; [T3] Popverse, "Marvel's X-Men: From the Ashes is here," https://www.thepopverse.com/x-men-marvel-wolverine-from-the-ashes-reboot-2024.

Magneto, killed in *A.X.E.: Judgment Day* (2022), was restored to life in the four-issue *Resurrection of Magneto* (Jan.–Apr. 2024, Ewing/Vecchio) by Storm — a structural setup for his Cyclops-team role in *From the Ashes* [T2] Marvel.com, "Resurrection of Magneto," https://www.marvel.com/comics/series/38654/resurrection_of_magneto_2024_-_present.

## 3. Fantastic Four

*Fantastic Four* #1 (Nov. 1961, Lee/Kirby) is the foundation stone of the Marvel Age. Publisher Martin Goodman commissioned it after noticing DC's *Justice League of America* sales; the result — Reed Richards, Sue Storm, Johnny Storm, Ben Grimm transformed by cosmic rays on an unsanctioned space flight — introduced the realism, family-dynamic friction, and shared-continuity ambition that defined Marvel as distinct from its competitors [T3] Wikipedia, "Fantastic Four (comic book)," https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fantastic_Four_(comic_book); [T2] Marvel.com, "58 Years Ago Today: *Fantastic Four* #1," https://www.marvel.com/articles/comics/58-years-ago-today-fantastic-four-1.

Era-defining arcs: the Galactus Trilogy (*FF* #48–50, 1966); the John Byrne run (1981–86); Jonathan Hickman's *FF* / *Fantastic Four* run (2009–12) which dissolved the team into the youth-focused Future Foundation; Dan Slott's run (2018–22). The team has functioned as Marvel's *exploration / first-contact* institution — closer in form to NASA-with-superpowers than to a hero team.

## 4. Guardians of the Galaxy: Two Distinct Teams

Two structurally separate Guardians teams share a name. Conflating them is one of the most common errors in casual Marvel reference.

- **Original Guardians of the Galaxy (1969):** debuted in *Marvel Super-Heroes* #18 (Jan. 1969, Arnold Drake/Gene Colan), set in the 31st century of Earth-691, an alternate timeline. Founders: Vance Astro (a 20th-century astronaut who reached Alpha Centauri after a millennium in cryosleep), Martinex (Pluto crystalline), Charlie-27 (Jovian soldier), and Yondu (blue-skinned Centaurian archer). The team's mission was resisting the Badoon occupation of the Solar System; their later signature arc was the pursuit of Korvac back to the 20th century where they teamed with the Avengers [T3] Wikipedia, "Guardians of the Galaxy (1969 team)," https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guardians_of_the_Galaxy_(1969_team).
- **Modern Guardians of the Galaxy (2008):** Dan Abnett and Andy Lanning's relaunch following *Annihilation* (2006) and *Annihilation: Conquest* (2007) repurposed the name for a present-day team led by Star-Lord (Peter Quill) — Rocket Raccoon, Groot, Drax, Gamora, Adam Warlock, Mantis, and the Quasar (Phyla-Vell). This is the team whose composition the MCU adapted; Yondu was reimported from the 1969 team as a thematic bridge [T3] Wikipedia, "Guardians of the Galaxy (2008 team)," https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guardians_of_the_Galaxy_(2008_team).

The 1969 Guardians did encounter the modern Marvel Universe via time travel and remained an intermittent fixture; the 2008 team operationally replaced them as the franchise's flagship.

## 5. Inhumans

Created by Lee/Kirby in *Fantastic Four* #45 (Dec. 1965), with Medusa and Gorgon appearing earlier in *FF* #36 and #44. Origin retconned across decades: 25,000 years ago, Kree scientists experimented on early hominids to engineer a superhuman footsoldier race; the Inhumans diverged, settled in Attilan (originally an island in the North Atlantic, later relocated to the Blue Area of the Moon, then to Earth's surface as New Attilan in Manhattan) [T2] Marvel.com, "Inhumans In Comics," https://www.marvel.com/teams-and-groups/inhumans/in-comics; [T3] Wikipedia, "Inhumans," https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inhumans.

Royal Family core: Black Bolt, Medusa, Karnak, Gorgon, Triton, Crystal, Lockjaw, Maximus. Era-defining arcs: Paul Jenkins/Jae Lee's *Inhumans* (1998–99); *Silent War* (2007); *War of Kings* (2009) where the Inhumans absorbed the Kree Empire; *Infinity* (2013) where Black Bolt detonated a Terrigen bomb, scattering Terrigen mist worldwide and triggering a wave of "NuHumans" — Marvel's editorially-mandated 2013–17 attempt to substitute Inhumans for X-Men in the marketing pipeline (a strategic misfire ultimately reversed). Currently a diminished but recurring institution.

## 6. Defenders

Debuted in *Marvel Feature* #1 (Dec. 1971), with prefiguring crossovers in *Doctor Strange* #183, *Sub-Mariner* #22, and *Incredible Hulk* #126 (1969–70). Original core: Doctor Strange, Hulk, Namor, later Silver Surfer [T3] Wikipedia, "Defenders (comics)," https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Defenders_(comics).

The Defenders are structurally a *non-team* — a term coined in the comic itself: no charter, no headquarters, no fixed roster, mission-based assembly by Doctor Strange. Other long-term members: Valkyrie, Hawkeye, Nighthawk, Gargoyle, Hellcat. The "non-team" idea has been reinterpreted across many revivals (Matt Fraction's *Defenders*, 2011; Al Ewing's cosmological *Defenders* and *Defenders Beyond*, 2021–22). The Netflix MCU "Defenders" (Daredevil/Jessica Jones/Luke Cage/Iron Fist) shares the name but borrows almost none of the comic structure [T2] Marvel.com, "The Defenders In Comics," https://www.marvel.com/teams-and-groups/defenders/in-comics.

## 7. Illuminati and Cabal: The Secret-Government Pair

The **Illuminati** were established via retcon in *New Avengers* #7 (July 2005, Bendis) and elaborated in the *New Avengers: Illuminati* one-shot (May 2006) and five-issue miniseries (Dec. 2006–Nov. 2007, Bendis/Reed/Cheung). Members: Iron Man, Mister Fantastic, Doctor Strange, Black Bolt, Namor, Professor X. The retcon placed their formation just after the Kree-Skrull War (mid-1970s in Marvel time) as a clandestine working group representing the major superhuman power blocs — science, mysticism, mutants, Inhumans, Atlantis, technology [T3] Wikipedia, "Illuminati (comics)," https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Illuminati_(comics); [T2] Marvel.com, "*New Avengers: Illuminati* (2006–2008)," https://www.marvel.com/comics/series/1137/new_avengers:_illuminati_(2006_-_2008).

Era-defining arcs: the Hulk's exile to space (precipitating *Planet Hulk* and *World War Hulk*); the splitting of the Infinity Gems among them; and most importantly Hickman's *New Avengers* (2013–15), where the Illuminati became the institution managing the Incursions of the Multiversal collapse and ultimately destroyed parallel Earths to delay the end of the multiverse — driving directly into *Secret Wars* (2015).

The **Cabal** is the Illuminati's structural shadow. Two distinct Cabals exist:

- **Osborn's Cabal (2008):** formed in *Secret Invasion* #8 (Bendis/Yu) after Osborn killed Skrull queen Veranke; members were Norman Osborn, Namor, Emma Frost, Doctor Doom, Loki, and the Hood. A mutual non-aggression pact: Osborn promised each member latitude to pursue their agenda in exchange for tolerating his public ascendancy [T3] Wikipedia, "Cabal (comics)," https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cabal_(comics); [T2] Marvel.com, "Cabal," https://www.marvel.com/teams-and-groups/cabal.
- **Hickman's Cabal (2014):** during *New Avengers* the Illuminati split when most members refused to destroy other Earths to save 616. Namor recruited Thanos, Black Swan, Maximus, Proxima Midnight, Corvus Glaive, and Terrax to do the work the Illuminati wouldn't — explicitly framed as the dark Illuminati.

## 8. Hellfire Club and Thunderbolts: Villainy as Institution

The **Hellfire Club** debuted in *Uncanny X-Men* #129 (Jan. 1980, Claremont/Byrne/Austin), opening the *Dark Phoenix Saga*. In-universe origins were backdated to mid-18th-century England, modeled on the historical Hellfire Clubs of Sir Francis Dashwood, then expanded into a global elite-influence network. Power resides in the **Inner Circle / Lords Cardinal**, a chess-themed leadership (King, Queen, Bishop, Rook in Black, White, and occasionally Red divisions) presided over by a Lord Imperial. The version that matters to X-Men continuity emerged when Sebastian Shaw and Emma Frost slaughtered the Council of the Chosen and renamed the body the Inner Circle [T2] Marvel.com, "The History of the Hellfire Club," https://www.marvel.com/articles/comics/hellfire-club-history-members-inner-circle-explained.

Notable seats: Shaw (Black King), Frost (White Queen, before defecting to the X-Men), Donald Pierce (White Bishop, then King), Harry Leland (Black Bishop), Selene (Black Queen). In the Krakoan era the Hellfire Trading Company was reconstituted under Frost, Shaw, and Kate Pryde as Krakoa's economic export arm — a striking inversion of the Club's original X-Men-antagonist function [T3] Wikipedia, "Hellfire Club (comics)," https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hellfire_Club_(comics).

The **Thunderbolts** debuted in *The Incredible Hulk* #449 (Jan. 1997) and headlined *Thunderbolts* #1 (Apr. 1997, Busiek/Bagley). The book's twist — revealed on the final page of issue #1 and sealed by Marvel as a secret — was that the Thunderbolts were the **Masters of Evil in disguise**: Baron Helmut Zemo (Citizen V), Moonstone (Meteorite), Fixer (Techno), Goliath (Atlas), Beetle (MACH-1), Screaming Mimi (Songbird). The team was assembled to fill the Avengers' absence after *Onslaught*, win public trust, and seize control of the world. The unintended consequence — several members preferred being heroes — turned the franchise into Marvel's permanent vehicle for villain-redemption arcs [T3] Wikipedia, "Thunderbolts (comics)," https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thunderbolts_(comics); [T2] Marvel.com, "Meet the Masters of Evil," https://www.marvel.com/articles/comics/meet-the-masters-of-evil-earths-mightiest-villains-avengers-rivals-thunderbolts.

The Thunderbolts have iterated as: Hawkeye-led reform team; Norman Osborn's federally-sanctioned squad pre-Dark Reign (Venom, Bullseye, Songbird, Penance, Radioactive Man); Luke Cage's penal-rehab team; Bucky Barnes's covert squad. Currently revived in connection with the *Thunderbolts\** MCU release.

## 9. The State Apparatus: S.H.I.E.L.D., S.W.O.R.D., Hydra, A.I.M.

**S.H.I.E.L.D.** debuted in *Strange Tales* #135 (Aug. 1965, Lee/Kirby) — the same issue that introduced Hydra. Originally "Supreme Headquarters, International Espionage Law-Enforcement Division," renamed several times (the most enduring being "Strategic Hazard Intervention, Espionage Logistics Directorate"). Lee modeled it on *The Man from U.N.C.L.E.*, transforming WWII Sgt. Fury into a James Bond-style superspy in the Cold War present. Kirby designed the Helicarrier and Life Model Decoys in this run. Jim Steranko took over the feature with *Strange Tales* #155 (Apr. 1967), retconning Hydra's origin as Strucker's creation and visually defining the agency's spy-fi aesthetic [T3] Wikipedia, "Nick Fury, Agent of S.H.I.E.L.D.," https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nick_Fury,_Agent_of_S.H.I.E.L.D._(feature); [T3] Wikipedia, "Strange Tales," https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strange_Tales.

S.H.I.E.L.D. has been dissolved and reconstituted multiple times — replaced by H.A.M.M.E.R. under Osborn (2008–10), restored after *Siege*, dissolved again post-*Secret Empire* (2017) when the agency was revealed to be Hydra-corrupted from inception in Nick Spencer's run.

**S.W.O.R.D.** (Sentient World Observation and Response Department) was created by Joss Whedon and John Cassaday in *Astonishing X-Men* vol. 3 #6 (Dec. 2004) as the orbital, extraterrestrial counterpart to S.H.I.E.L.D., headquartered on the Peak space station. Founding director: Abigail Brand, half-human/half-alien. Functionally subordinate to S.H.I.E.L.D. but operationally independent — relations soured after Fury's departure [T2] Marvel.com, "S.W.O.R.D.," https://www.marvel.com/teams-and-groups/sword; [T3] Wikipedia, "S.W.O.R.D.," https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/S.W.O.R.D. In the Krakoan era, S.W.O.R.D. was reformed (2020, Al Ewing) as a mutant-led space agency under Brand, integrating it into mutant statecraft.

**Hydra** debuted in the same *Strange Tales* #135 (1965). The modern organization was founded during WWII by Wolfgang von Strucker, allied with the Red Skull, taking control of the Hand and using Arnim Zola's bioscience. A backdated retcon (notably elaborated in Jonathan Hickman's *S.H.I.E.L.D.*, 2010, and Jason Aaron's *Secret Empire*) traces Hydra's spiritual lineage to the ancient "Brotherhood of the Spear," eastern counterpart of the proto-S.H.I.E.L.D. "Brotherhood of the Shield" [T3] Wikipedia, "Hydra (comics)," https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydra_(comics); [T2] Marvel.com, "Hydra In Comics," https://www.marvel.com/teams-and-groups/hydra/in-comics. *Secret Empire* (2017) saw a Cosmic Cube–altered Captain America install a Hydra government over the United States — the most extreme institutional inversion in Marvel publication history.

**A.I.M.** (Advanced Idea Mechanics) debuted in *Strange Tales* #146 (July 1966, Lee/Kirby). Originally a science branch of Hydra established by Strucker in WWII, it later incorporated publicly as a weapons R&D firm and severed from Hydra after MODOK (transformed George Tarleton) killed the Scientist Supreme and seized control during the chaos following Hydra Island's destruction. A.I.M.'s most consequential creations are the Cosmic Cube, the Super-Adaptoid, and MODOK himself [T3] Wikipedia, "Advanced Idea Mechanics," https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Advanced_Idea_Mechanics; [T2] Marvel.com, "Meet M.O.D.O.K.," https://www.marvel.com/articles/comics/modok-george-tarleton-aim-explained. Under Sunspot's *New Avengers* (2015, Ewing), A.I.M. was rebranded "Avengers Idea Mechanics" — the rare instance of a major Marvel villain organization being permanently captured and reoriented as a hero institution, before subsequent runs reverted it.

## 10. Continuity Notes on the MCU Mappings

The MCU (Earth-199999) flattens these institutional structures heavily. S.H.I.E.L.D. is rendered functionally similar to its 616 form but absorbed Hydra publicly in *Captain America: The Winter Soldier* (2014), pre-empting the comics' *Secret Empire* by three years. The MCU's Avengers have no Reserve, West Coast, or Dark roster system, though *Thunderbolts\** (2025) imports the comics-Thunderbolts redemption framework. S.W.O.R.D. arrived in *WandaVision* (2021) under acting director Tyler Hayward — a structural parallel without Brand. The MCU Inhumans (Inhumans 2017 series, *Agents of S.H.I.E.L.D.*) are largely decoupled from the principal film slate, and the Krakoan era has no MCU presence as of 2026.
