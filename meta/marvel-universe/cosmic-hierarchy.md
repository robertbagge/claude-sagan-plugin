# Marvel Cosmic Hierarchy: Structural Map and Historical Evolution

This map describes the strata of cosmic beings that sit above the super-hero stratum of the Marvel Universe (Earth-616 baseline, with structural cross-references to the broader Multiverse). The focus is structural: who sits where, what they have jurisdiction over, and how the architecture has been retconned across eras — most notably by Jim Starlin (1970s–80s), Mark Gruenwald (early 80s), Tom DeFalco / Steve Englehart (late 80s), Jonathan Hickman (2012–2015), and Al Ewing (2016–2022).

## 1. The Apex: One-Above-All and the Problem of "Top"

At the structural apex sits the **One-Above-All** — described as the being that "brought the multiverse and all life into existence," sitting above the Living Tribunal, Eternity, Infinity, Oblivion, and Death `[T3] Comic Book Co, "Beyond Galactus: Marvel's Cosmic Hierarchy and Abstract Entities Explained," https://comicbookco.com/comics/marvel/beyond-galactus-cosmic-hierarchy-abstract-entities-explained/`. Unlike the other tiers, the One-Above-All has no jurisdictional metaphor (judge, embodiment, agent) — it is treated as pure creative force, often equated meta-textually with Marvel's writers/Jack Kirby `[T3] thedigitalweekly, "One Above All Explained," https://thedigitalweekly.com/one-above-all-explained-marvel-s-ultimate-power-source/`.

Note the naming collision the hierarchy creates: "One-Above-All" is also the title used for the leader of the **Celestials** (a much lower tier — see §4). Marvel.com treats these as distinct entities sharing a name `[T2] Marvel.com, "Living Tribunal" article, https://www.marvel.com/articles/comics/living-tribunal-history-powers-explained`. This collision is the first sign that the "hierarchy" is less a clean ladder than a stack of jurisdictional layers each with its own internal "top."

## 2. The Living Tribunal: Multiversal Judge

Directly below the One-Above-All sits the **Living Tribunal**, a three-faced entity that personifies multiversal law and acts as the One-Above-All's representative `[T2] Marvel.com, "Living Tribunal" article, https://www.marvel.com/articles/comics/living-tribunal-history-powers-explained`. Jurisdiction is explicitly **multiversal** — the Tribunal can erase entire realities that threaten balance, putting it structurally above any abstract entity bound to a single universe.

Its place in the stack collapsed during Jonathan Hickman's "Time Runs Out" arc and the lead-up to Secret Wars (2015): the Beyonders slew the Living Tribunal while ending the Seventh Cosmos `[T3] Marvel Database, "Beyonders" entry, https://marvel.fandom.com/wiki/Beyonders`. After the multiverse was restored at the end of Secret Wars (2015), Al Ewing's *Ultimates 2* (2016–17) made the Tribunal's vacancy a structural problem: Master Order, Lord Chaos, and the In-Betweener forcibly fused into a composite entity, **Logos**, to occupy the Tribunal's empty role `[T2] Wikipedia, "In-Betweener," https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/In-Betweener`. This is the first time the "judge" slot has been treated as a function — something other entities can be promoted (or coerced) into filling — rather than a unique being.

## 3. Beyonders and the Beyonder/Beyonders Distinction

The most consequential retcon in the cosmic hierarchy is the bifurcation of "the Beyonder."

**The original Beyonder** debuted in *Marvel Super Heroes Secret Wars* #1 (May 1984) as an unseen, nigh-omnipotent voice from "beyond" the multiverse, with "power millions of times greater than the entire multiverse combined" `[T2] Wikipedia, "Beyonder," https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beyonder`. The original Secret Wars treated him as a singular omnipotent being.

The character was then sequentially demoted:

1. **Fantastic Four #319 (1988)** by Tom DeFalco retconned the Beyonder into an incomplete Cosmic Cube — a creation, not a creator `[T2] Wikipedia, "Beyonder," https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beyonder`.
2. *New Avengers: Illuminati* (2007) further suggested he was a mutant-Inhuman hybrid `[T3] Marvel Database, "Beyonder (Earth-616)," https://marvel.fandom.com/wiki/Beyonder_(Earth-616)`.
3. **Hickman's New Avengers (Vol. 3) #30 (2015)** delivered the structural retcon: the original Beyonder is recategorized as a "child unit" of an entire **Beyonders** race, with his Cosmic Cube and Kosmos forms reframed as "larval stages" `[T3] Marvel Database, "Beyonder (Earth-616)," https://marvel.fandom.com/wiki/Beyonder_(Earth-616)`.

The **Beyonders** themselves were first name-checked in *Marvel Two-in-One* #63 (May 1980) by Mark Gruenwald and Jerry Bingham `[T2] Wikipedia, "Beyonders," https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beyonders`, but were not given structural weight until Hickman. In his telling, the Beyonders are higher-dimensional entities living *outside* the Multiverse — originally Celestial servitors called "Omegas" in opposition to the First Firmament's "Alpha" `[T3] Marvel Database, "Beyonders," https://marvel.fandom.com/wiki/Beyonders`. During *Time Runs Out* they ran a multiverse-wide experiment that killed the Living Tribunal, every Celestial, and every abstract entity across all realities `[T2] Wikipedia, "Beyonders," https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beyonders`.

Their key structural weakness — that they are "linear beings" unable to act through time — is what allows Doctor Doom (with help from the Molecule Men) to weaponize them and absorb their power, becoming God-Emperor of Battleworld at the end of Hickman's *Secret Wars* (2015) `[T2] Wikipedia, "Beyonders," https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beyonders`. Functionally, post-Hickman the Beyonders are Marvel's new "above the multiverse" tier — slotting between the Tribunal/abstracts (which they killed) and the One-Above-All (which they did not touch).

## 4. The Cosmoses and the First Firmament: A Historical Spine

Hickman and Ewing together rebuilt the *backstory* of the hierarchy around a sequence of universe iterations:

- **First Cosmos / First Firmament**: a single universe, not a multiverse. It created two factions of servants, the **Aspirants** and what would become the **Celestials**. The Celestials rebelled, shattering the First Firmament `[T3] Screen Rant, "Marvel's 8 Cosmos," https://screenrant.com/marvel-multiverse-8-cosmos-gods-galactus-explained/`.
- **Second Cosmos**: the first true multiverse, born from the shattered First Firmament. The Beyonders ("Omegas") originate here as Celestial servitors `[T3] Marvel Database, "Beyonders," https://marvel.fandom.com/wiki/Beyonders`.
- **Sixth Cosmos**: the universe whose dying sentience merged with the explorer **Galan of Taa** to produce Galactus `[T2] Wikipedia, "Galactus," https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galactus` (origin first established by Mark Gruenwald in *Super-Villain Classics* #1, 1983).
- **Seventh Cosmos**: the multiverse where the bulk of Marvel publication took place — Earth-616, Earth-1610, etc. This is the Cosmos that Hickman ended in Secret Wars (2015). Critically, the Seventh Cosmos was personified as twins (**Eternity** and **Infinity**), unlike previous singular Cosmoses `[T3] Screen Rant, "Marvel's 8 Cosmos," https://screenrant.com/marvel-multiverse-8-cosmos-gods-galactus-explained/`.
- **Eighth Cosmos**: the post-2015-Secret-Wars multiverse, restored by Reed Richards using Molecule Man-derived energy.

The First Firmament returned as an active antagonist in Ewing's *Ultimates 2* (2016–17) — an ancient creator-being trying to reabsorb its descendants. Ewing made it imprison Eternity, which directly drove the Ultimates' arc `[T2] Wikipedia, "Eternity (Marvel Comics)," https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eternity_(Marvel_Comics)`.

This historical spine is structurally important: it converts what used to be ahistorical "cosmic facts" (Celestials, Galactus, Eternity) into entities with timestamps and predecessors. Ewing's *Ultimates 2* #6 (2017) is the load-bearing issue that recasts the Celestials as "rebels" against the Aspirants `[T2] Wikipedia, "Celestial (comics)," https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Celestial_(comics)`.

## 5. Celestials: Galactic-Scale Architects, Recently Demoted

The **Celestials** were introduced by Jack Kirby in *The Eternals* #1 (1976) as 2,000-foot-tall armored humanoids who visit developing worlds in periodic "Hosts" — first visit establishes genetic templates (creating **Eternals** and **Deviants**), later Hosts evaluate progress `[T2] Wikipedia, "Celestial (comics)," https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Celestial_(comics)`. Jurisdiction is **galactic to universal** — they act inside the universe, on planets, with sufficient power to compete with Galactus, the Stranger, Odin, and Zeus per the *Infinity Gauntlet* power-tiering `[T2] Wikipedia, "Celestial (comics)," https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Celestial_(comics)`.

The retcons here run in two directions:

- **Backward** (origin): Ewing's *Ultimates 2* #6 (2017) makes them rogue Aspirants of the First Firmament — i.e., *junior* to the cosmic spine described in §4.
- **Forward** (purpose): the Jason Aaron *Avengers* run and *A.X.E.: Judgment Day* (2022) reframe Celestial interest in Earth as strategic — Earth functions as an antibody incubator against the Horde infection — rather than as pure evolutionary curiosity `[T2] Wikipedia, "Celestial (comics)," https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Celestial_(comics)`.

Both retcons demote the Celestials: where they once read as Marvel's gods, they now read as one mid-tier faction inside a much longer war.

## 6. Abstract Entities: The Cosmological Compass

Below the multiversal layer sits the cluster of **abstract entities** with universal jurisdiction. The canonical "cosmological compass" framing — Eternity, Infinity, Death, Oblivion — identifies the four pillars `[T3] Comic Basics, "All 16 Abstract Entities in Marvel Universe, Ranked," https://www.comicbasics.com/all-abstract-entities-in-marvel-ranked/`.

- **Eternity** (debut: *Strange Tales* #138, November 1965, Lee/Ditko) — embodies time and the living sentience of the universe `[T2] Wikipedia, "Eternity (Marvel Comics)," https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eternity_(Marvel_Comics)`.
- **Infinity** — Eternity's twin, embodies space; together they form the space-time continuum `[T2] Wikipedia, "Eternity (Marvel Comics)," https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eternity_(Marvel_Comics)`.
- **Death** — embodies death/decay; structurally opposed to Eternity/Infinity.
- **Oblivion** — progenitor of Death, predates the Big Bang along with the Living Tribunal; ranked stronger than Death but weaker than Eternity/Infinity `[T3] Comic Basics, "All 16 Abstract Entities in Marvel Universe, Ranked," https://www.comicbasics.com/all-abstract-entities-in-marvel-ranked/`.

Crucially: **each universe has its own Infinity, Eternity, Death, Oblivion** — they are universal, not multiversal `[T3] Screen Rant, "Eternity And 9 Other Crucial Cosmic Entities," https://screenrant.com/marvel-comics-crucial-cosmic-entities/`. The Living Tribunal sits above the per-universe set, providing the multiversal layer.

Two more entities sit adjacent at this tier:

- **Master Order and Lord Chaos** (debut: *Marvel Two-in-One Annual* #2, 1977, Starlin) — agents of universal balance `[T2] Wikipedia, "Master Order," https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Master_Order`. They appeared in the Congress of abstract beings during *The Infinity Gauntlet* (1991) `[T2] Wikipedia, "Master Order"`.
- **The In-Betweener** (debut: *Warlock* #9, 1975, Starlin) — created by Order and Chaos as their living avatar, charged with maintaining universal balance `[T2] Marvel.com, "Meet the In-Betweener," https://www.marvel.com/articles/comics/in-betweener-character-explained-cosmic-force-lord-chaos-master-order`. His loyalty drifts to himself, which is the recurring source of plot.

All of these were killed during *Time Runs Out* by the Beyonders, then restored when the multiverse was reborn `[T2] Marvel.com, "A Brief History of Order and Chaos," https://www.marvel.com/articles/comics/ultimates-a-brief-history-of-order-and-chaos`. Post-Secret Wars, Ewing's *Ultimates 2* (2016–17) does the most aggressive reshuffle: Order and Chaos forcibly merge with the In-Betweener to form **Logos** and attempt to take the dead Living Tribunal's job, with Order and Chaos murdering the resurrected Tribunal in *Ultimates 2* #2 to clear the seat `[T2] Wikipedia, "In-Betweener," https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/In-Betweener`. The arc resolves with Black Panther's astral form separating Logos back into its components, but Order and Chaos remain antagonists rather than balance-keepers — a structural inversion of their original role `[T2] Wikipedia, "Master Order"`.

## 7. Galactus: Floating Tier, Multiple Roles

**Galactus** does not fit cleanly inside any single tier; his power level and role have been deliberately mobile across eras.

- **1966 (Lee/Kirby, *Fantastic Four* #48)**: Devourer of Worlds — a god-tier hunger force `[T2] Wikipedia, "Galactus," https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galactus`.
- **1983 (Gruenwald, *Super-Villain Classics* #1)**: backstory revealed — Galan of Taa, survivor of the Sixth Cosmos, merged with that universe's dying sentience.
- **1991 (Starlin, *Infinity Gauntlet*)**: Galactus is one of many "cosmic deities" overruled by Thanos with the Gauntlet — peer to Celestials, Order, Chaos, Stranger.
- **2016 (Ewing, *The Ultimates* vol. 2 #1–6)**: transformed into the **Lifebringer**, restoring life to dead worlds rather than consuming living ones `[T2] Wikipedia, "Galactus"`. Ewing originally planned to revert this for tragedy but kept it because he liked writing the new form `[T2] Wikipedia, "Galactus"`.
- **2018 (Duggan, *Infinity Countdown* #4)**: reverted to Devourer; killed by Thor in 2020; resurrected 2022 `[T2] Wikipedia, "Galactus"`.

Structurally, Galactus's recurring narrative function is "high-power universal entity who can be reframed when needed" — closer to the abstracts than to the Celestials, but explicitly a survivor from a *previous* Cosmos rather than a current pillar.

## 8. Watchers and the Stranger: Observation and Survey Tiers

Below the abstract entities sit the **scientifically-themed** cosmic races — the layer concerned with *observing* and *cataloguing* rather than embodying or governing.

The **Watchers** were established by Lee/Kirby in *Fantastic Four* #13 (1962). After their first interventionist experiment on Prosilicus ended in nuclear genocide, Ikor drafted the strict non-interference oath that defines the species `[T3] Marvel Database, "Uatu (Earth-616)," https://marvel.fandom.com/wiki/Uatu_(Earth-616)`. The most consequential retcon was *Original Sin* #0 (2014), which established that **Ikor was Uatu's father** — making Uatu's chronic oath-breaking a generational rebellion, not an idiosyncrasy `[T3] Marvel Database, "Uatu (Earth-616)"`. Original Sin (2014) also kills Uatu (Nick Fury shoots him, takes his eye), promotes Fury to "the Unseen," and later resurrects Uatu — the first time the Watcher slot has been depicted as transferable `[T3] Marvel Database, "Uatu (Earth-616)"`.

The **Stranger** (debut: *X-Men* #11, May 1965, Lee/Kirby) is treated by Marvel as "comparable to Galactus and the Celestials" — a universal-scale scientist/surveyor who collects specimens onto a personal laboratory planet `[T2] Wikipedia, "Stranger (Marvel Comics)," https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stranger_(Marvel_Comics)`. Crucially, the Stranger has an in-canon habit of impersonating the Beyonder — most explicitly in the *Beyond!* limited series — which has been used by writers as a get-out-of-jail-free card to retcon previous Beyonder appearances as "actually the Stranger" `[T2] Wikipedia, "Stranger (Marvel Comics)"`. This is structurally significant because it makes the boundary between "outside the multiverse" entities and "powerful in-universe entities" deliberately porous.

## 9. Power Tiers and Jurisdictions: Synthesised View

Reading across the eras, the hierarchy stabilises (post-2017) into roughly five jurisdictional bands:

| Band | Jurisdiction | Members | Status post-Secret Wars (2015) |
|------|-------------|---------|--------------------------------|
| 1. Apex | Beyond multiverse, beyond comprehension | One-Above-All | Untouched; meta-textual |
| 2. Outside the multiverse | Trans-multiversal | Beyonders | Killed by Doom in Secret Wars; status uncertain post-Eighth-Cosmos |
| 3. Multiversal judge | All realities | Living Tribunal; (briefly) Logos | Tribunal restored, then re-killed in Ultimates 2; Logos attempted, dissolved |
| 4. Universal pillars | One universe each | Eternity, Infinity, Death, Oblivion, Master Order, Lord Chaos, In-Betweener, First Firmament (legacy) | Restored; First Firmament now active threat |
| 5. Galactic / Universal floaters | Span universe | Celestials, Galactus, Stranger, Watchers | All retconned to be junior to the First Firmament era |

The historical pattern: each major writer-architect (Starlin → Gruenwald → Hickman → Ewing) has added a **higher** tier above the previous "top," and reframed the previous "top" as junior to it. Starlin gave the universe a metaphysical compass (Order/Chaos, In-Betweener, Death). Gruenwald and Englehart established the Beyond/Beyonder layer. Hickman split the Beyonder into Beyonders and made them slay the Living Tribunal. Ewing then promoted the **First Firmament** as a pre-Celestial creator, making everything we previously thought was top-tier (Celestials, Galactus, even Eternity) into descendants of an even older being.

This is the load-bearing structural fact for synthesis with other topics: Marvel's cosmic hierarchy is not a static ladder but a **stack of retroactively-added ceilings**, each generation of writers reframing the previous generation's apex as middle management.
