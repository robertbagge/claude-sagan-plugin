# Major Species & Races of the Marvel Universe

Marvel's biological cosmology is structurally hierarchical. Most "races" of cosmic significance trace upward to a small set of demiurgic agents — primarily the Celestials, secondarily the Kree and the Elder Gods — and the species occupy a layered tech tree of experiments, by-products, weapons, and rivals. The diegetic taxonomy has been retconned repeatedly: what began in the 1960s as discrete origin stories (Lee/Kirby Inhumans, Lee/Everett Atlanteans) was bolted onto a unifying Celestial frame in the 1970s (Kirby's *Eternals*), then re-bolted in the 2000s (Gaiman's *Eternals*) into a more deterministic, machine-like model, and finally re-bolted again in the 2020s (Gillen's *A.X.E.: Judgment Day*) so that mutants are themselves classed as "deviants" relative to the Celestial Prime Directive. This document maps the structurally significant species by lineage rather than alphabet.

## 1. Celestial-Engineered Earth Lineages: Eternals, Deviants, Mutants

The core trifecta of Earth-side species all derive from a single intervention. The First Host of Celestials experimented on early hominids roughly one million years ago, splitting one hundred specimens into the more powerful, less fertile Eternals and another hundred into the more mutable, more fertile Deviants, while leaving the baseline population with a latent genetic potential — the seed of what would become the X-gene [T2] Marvel.com, "The Complete History of the Celestials, the Deviants, and the Eternals". The Second Host returned to discipline the Deviants when they came close to conquering humanity, sinking Lemuria and Atlantis as a result [T2] Marvel.com, "The Complete History of the Celestials, the Deviants, and the Eternals".

This origin was first laid down in Jack Kirby's *The Eternals* (1976) and operated for thirty years as a freestanding mythos. Neil Gaiman and John Romita Jr.'s 2006 *Eternals* miniseries retconned the Eternals from "godlike beings" into "computerised antibodies for the planet" who are programmed and hardwired by the Celestials, with a hard-coded inability to attack their creators that shuts an attacker's body down automatically [T3] TV Tropes, "Eternals (2006)", https://tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/ComicBook/Eternals2006; [T3] Comic Book Herald, "ETERNALS (2006) by Neil Gaiman and John Romita Jr Review!", https://www.comicbookherald.com/eternals-2006-by-neil-gaiman-and-john-romita-jr-review/. Gaiman's run also re-cast Deviants as "Celestial food" in their own self-description [T3] TV Tropes, "Eternals (2006)".

For mutants, the official wiki frames it explicitly: "During the Celestials' First Host on Earth one million years ago, they gave primitive man the X-Factor gene which would one day bear superhuman fruit" [T3] Marvel Database, "Mutant History", https://marvel.fandom.com/wiki/Mutant_History. The "first mutant" identity has been retconned at least three times: Namor (the original Golden Age claim, as a human/Atlantean hybrid), then Apocalypse / En Sabah Nur as the modern claimant born ~5,000 years ago, then Selene the Black Queen, retconned in *X-Force: Necrosha* (2009) to ~15,000 BC, ~12,000 years before Apocalypse [T3] uncannyxmen.net, "SO WHO IS MARVEL'S FIRST MUTANT THEN?", https://uncannyxmen.net/miscellaneous/so-who-is-marvels-first-mutant-then; [T3] Wikipedia, "Apocalypse (character)", https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apocalypse_(character).

The Krakoan Age (Hickman, *House of X / Powers of X*, July–October 2019) restructured mutant biology politically: the Five (Egg, Proteus, Hope Summers, Tempus, Elixir) plus Cerebro psychic backups produced a working resurrection protocol, effectively granting the species collective immortality and reframing them as a sovereign nation rather than a persecuted minority [T2] Marvel.com, "The Secrets of Mutant Resurrection". The 2022 *A.X.E.: Judgment Day* event then turned the Celestial origin story against them: Druig, the Prime Eternal, declared mutantkind "excessively deviant", invoking the Eternals' Prime Directive — a direct retroactive coupling of the X-gene's Celestial origin to the Eternals' anti-Deviant mandate [T3] Wikipedia, "A.X.E.: Judgment Day", https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A.X.E.:_Judgment_Day. The Progenitor Celestial that the Avengers awakened (Avengers Mountain) then judged Earth itself, with Sersi revealing that Eternal resurrection had always quietly stolen human life force [T2] Marvel.com, "What 'A.X.E.: Judgment Day' Means for the Marvel Universe".

In Earth-1610 (Ultimate Marvel), this Celestial frame is dropped entirely. Mutants are a Weapon X by-product: WWII-era Canadian/US super-soldier programs accidentally generated a genome ("Mutant Zero", James Howlett) which, once released, spread globally — making mutants a literally manufactured species rather than an evolutionary one [T3] Marvel Database, "Earth-1610", https://marvel.fandom.com/wiki/Earth-1610. This is the cleanest example of how a continuity reboot rewires species' origins without changing their phenotype.

## 2. Inhumans: A Kree-Made Branch

The Inhumans (Inhomo supremis) sit one tier down — not made by Celestials directly, but by the Kree exploiting the Celestial-implanted X-gene. The Kree science party that came to Earth was attempting to weaponise human latent potential against the Skrulls during the Kree-Skrull War; the Supreme Intelligence shut the project down after foreseeing it would lead to his own destruction [T2] Marvel.com, "Inhumans In Comics"; [T3] Wikipedia, "Inhumans", https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inhumans. The remaining experimental population built the city of Attilan, isolated themselves, and discovered the Terrigen Mist — a vapour from Terrigen Crystals which, when inhaled, triggers Terrigenesis and unlocks the abandoned Kree augments [T3] Marvel Database, "Terrigenesis", https://marvel.fandom.com/wiki/Terrigenesis.

Attilan is itself a structural element: Stan Lee and Jack Kirby placed it variously in the Himalayas, the Atlantic, and most famously on the Blue Area of the Moon, then later above the Hudson, with each move signalling a new editorial era for the species [T2] Marvel.com, "Inhumans In Comics".

The Inhumans were used as a structural counterweight to mutants from 2013 onwards. *Infinity* (2013) detonated a Terrigen Bomb that seeded latent Inhuman descendants ("NuHumans") globally. Then *Death of X* (2016) and *Inhumans vs. X-Men* (2016–17, written by Charles Soule and Jeff Lemire) established that the same Terrigen cloud was toxic to mutants, with Beast discovering only two weeks remained before Earth became uninhabitable for them — the editorial subtext being widely read as a corporate-rights proxy fight given that Fox held the X-Men film rights at the time [T3] Wikipedia, "Inhumans vs. X-Men", https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inhumans_vs._X-Men. The IvX endgame led into *ResurrXion*, restoring the X-Men's editorial primacy and shelving the Inhumans-as-mutants-replacement project.

## 3. The Galactic Triumvirate: Kree, Skrulls, Shi'ar

These three empires are the core galactic political structure of 616 cosmic stories. All have been retconned to share Celestial fingerprints: the Kree-engineered Inhumans, the Skrulls' own Celestial-tampered offshoots (Eternals of the Skrulls and Deviants of the Skrulls — confirmed when Kirby's frame was extended), and the Shi'ar, whose homeworld Aerie was uplifted by Aleph-spawned demiurges Ex Nihilo and Abyss in a Hickman-era retcon [T3] Wikipedia, "Shi'ar", https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shi%27ar.

The **Kree** are blue- (and pink-, a caste division) skinned warriors from Hala. Their politics centre on the Supreme Intelligence, an aggregated consciousness of the empire's greatest minds, ruling from Kree-Lar / Hala. The originating cosmic conflict — the **Kree-Skrull War** — was first dramatised in Roy Thomas's *Avengers* #89–97 (June 1971 – March 1972) with art by Sal Buscema, Neal Adams, and John Buscema, and is widely cited as the first true cosmic crossover and a template for everything since [T3] Wikipedia, "Kree–Skrull War", https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kree%E2%80%93Skrull_War. The retconned origin: Skrull Emperor Dorrek I visited Hala and held a contest between the Kree and the plant-like Cotati for advanced tech, which the Kree resolved by murdering both the Cotati delegates and the Skrull observer, then stealing Skrull tech [T2] Marvel.com, "How the Kree-Skrull War Paved the Road to 'Empyre'".

The **Skrulls** are green-skinned reptilian shapeshifters. Beyond the Kree war, two events are structurally load-bearing. *Secret Invasion* (Bendis, April–December 2008) revealed a long-game replacement of multiple Marvel heroes — Hank Pym, Jarvis, and others — by Skrull infiltrators, with the post-event installation of Norman Osborn's Dark Reign as direct fallout [T3] Wikipedia, "Secret Invasion", https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secret_Invasion. *Annihilation* (2006) had previously eradicated most of the Skrull Empire in under six months as the Annihilation Wave swept through Kree and Skrull space [T3] Marvel Database, "Annihilation Wave (Earth-616)", https://marvel.fandom.com/wiki/Annihilation_Wave_(Earth-616).

*Empyre* (2020, Al Ewing & Dan Slott) finally collapsed the Kree-Skrull binary: the two empires unified under Dorrek VIII (the Young Avenger Hulkling, a Kree-Skrull hybrid) into the Kree/Skrull Alliance, with Throneworld II built on the ruins of Hala — a major structural simplification of the cosmic map after fifty years of bilateral war [T2] Marvel.com, "Crash Course to 'Empyre'"; [T3] Wikipedia, "Empyre", https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Empyre. The villains of Empyre were the Cotati — the third party from the original Kree-Skrull contest, finally promoted from continuity Easter egg to event antagonist.

The **Shi'ar** debuted in Chris Claremont and Dave Cockrum's *Uncanny X-Men* #97 (Feb. 1976) and serve as the X-Men's primary cosmic interface (the Phoenix Saga, Lilandra, the Imperial Guard) [T3] Wikipedia, "Shi'ar", https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shi%27ar. Cold-blooded humanoids of avian descent, they rule from the throneworld Chandilar (Aerie's status is uncertain) and form an aggressively expansionist Imperium whose religion mandates the absorption of other cultures by force [T3] Wikipedia, "Shi'ar"; [T3] uncannyxmen.net, "Shi'ar Imperium", https://uncannyxmen.net/glossary/groups/shiar-imperium. The Shi'ar provide the technological backbone of mutant resurrection itself: Cerebro's psychic-backup upgrade depends on Shi'ar tech [T2] Marvel.com, "The Secrets of Mutant Resurrection".

## 4. Predator Species: Brood, Phalanx, Symbiotes

Where the empires are political, these species are structural antagonists at a biological-horror level — each functioning as a "what if a species was just a contagion" thought experiment.

The **Brood** debuted in Claremont/Cockrum's *Uncanny X-Men* #155 (March 1982). They are insectoid xenoparasites that reproduce by implanting hosts (Cockrum's design intent: "the most horrible looking thing he could think of"), modelled visually on the Xenomorphs from *Alien* (1979) [T3] Wikipedia, "Brood (comics)", https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brood_(comics). Later lore has them captured and weaponised by the Kree against the Shi'ar, slotting them into the empire-vs-empire structure [T3] Multiversal Omnipedia, "Brood (Marvel)", http://moa.omnimulti.com/Brood_(Marvel).

The **Phalanx / Technarchy** are techno-organic. The Phalanx are a hive-minded cybernetic collective that has assimilated 100,000 worlds and slaughtered 200,000 races over 100,000 life cycles; the Technarchy were originally created by the Phalanx as a viral delivery system (the Transmode Virus) [T3] Wikipedia, "Phalanx (comics)", https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phalanx_(comics); [T3] uncannyxmen.net, "PHALANX", https://uncannyxmen.net/alien-races/phalanx. Mutants are a known partial-immunity case — a structural plot device used in *Annihilation: Conquest* (2007) when the Phalanx took over the Kree Empire post-Annihilation and were defeated largely by mutants and Adam Warlock.

The **Symbiotes (Klyntar)** got the most aggressive structural retcon of any Marvel race. From their 1984 *Secret Wars* debut, they were "alien costume" antagonists with no fixed cosmology; Donny Cates's *Venom* (2018–) and *King in Black* (2020) retconned them into a cosmological prime mover. Symbiotes were created by the primordial god **Knull** from the "living abyss" before the universe existed; the Celestials recruited Knull as the original "King in Black", which he rejected, and the symbiotes were later weaponised by him until they rebelled and trapped him inside an artificial planet of bonded symbiotes — Klyntar literally means "cage" in their language [T2] Marvel.com, "Meet Knull, the God of Symbiotes"; [T3] Wikipedia, "Knull", https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Knull. *King in Black* (2020) was the cosmological climax in which Knull finally reached Earth.

## 5. Pseudo-Divine Lineages: Asgardians, Olympians, Atlanteans, Lemurians

These are species which sit at the seam between "alien" and "myth" — Marvel's standard solution being to pin them to the Elder Gods rather than the Celestials.

**Asgardians** (Æsir) are humanoid quasi-immortals from the dimension of Asgard, which contains five other intelligent races — Giants, Dwarves, Elves, Trolls, and Demons — each with their own Realm in the Yggdrasil cosmology [T3] Wikipedia, "Asgardians (Marvel Comics)", https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asgardians_(Marvel_Comics). Main 616 canon makes them descendants of the Elder Gods, the same lineage as all Earth pantheons (Olympians, etc.); alternate-universe stories have variously presented them as Clarke's-Law sufficiently-advanced aliens [T3] Wikipedia, "Asgardians (Marvel Comics)". *War of the Realms* (2019) was the most recent structurally significant Asgardian event.

**Atlanteans** and **Lemurians** are both *Homo mermanus*, an offshoot of *Homo sapiens* that emerged ~10,000 years ago — origin pointedly "lost" — possessing gills and bluish skin [T3] Wikipedia, "Homo mermanus", https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homo_mermanus. The Atlantean kingdom rose ~6,000 BC in the sunken ruins of Pre-Cataclysmic (surface-dwelling, magic-using) Atlantis. The Lemurians broke from Atlantis 5,800–5,300 BC under Set-worshipping schismatics and colonised the Pacific ruins of Deviant Lemuria — the same Lemuria the Celestials had previously sunk [T3] Marvel Database, "Lemurians (Homo mermanus)", https://marvel.fandom.com/wiki/Lemurians_(Homo_mermanus). The Serpent Crown turned Lemurian skin greenish and scaly, marking them as the dark mirror to the Atlanteans [T3] Marvel Database, "Lemurians (Homo mermanus)". Note the structural elegance: the *Homo mermanus* lineage literally lives inside the geographical scars left by the Celestial-Deviant war.

## 6. Cosmic Observers and the Last Survivors: Watchers, Elders of the Universe

Two species occupy the "post-political" tier — beings older or more powerful than the empires, defined by what they no longer do.

The **Watchers** originated on Lumina (originally "the Luminous"). After their first uplift experiment — gifting atomic energy to the Prosilicans, who promptly auto-genocided themselves with nuclear weapons — Ikor drafted a code of ethics requiring strict non-interference and passive observation [T3] Marvel Appendix, "Prosilicans", http://www.marvunapp.com/Appendix/prosilic.htm; [T3] Wikipedia, "Watcher (comics)", https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Watcher_(comics). Each Watcher is assigned a sector; Earth's is Uatu, who maintains a Citadel on the Blue Area of the Moon and is famous for repeatedly violating his oath. The Prosilican incident is structurally important because it grounds the entire "non-intervention" trope — including Doctor Who-style narrative use throughout the *What If…?* anthology.

The **Elders of the Universe** are not a species but a status: each is the last survivor of an otherwise-extinct primordial race who discovered they could live indefinitely as long as they retained the will to do so [T3] Wikipedia, "Elders of the Universe", https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elders_of_the_Universe. The Grandmaster (En Dwi Gast) is one of the oldest, from one of the first sapient species to evolve after the Big Bang; the Collector (Taneleer Tivan), the Champion, the Gardener, the Runner, the Contemplator, and others share this status [T2] Marvel.com, "Grandmaster In Comics". They tried collectively to consume Galactus using the Infinity Gems, failed, and were partially eaten by him in retaliation [T3] Wikipedia, "Elders of the Universe". Functionally, the Elders are the "monomyth NPCs" of the cosmic tier — they exist so that any given cosmic story has a curator/host/judge to invoke without disturbing the political structure of the empires.

## 7. Continuity Map: How the Tree Has Been Re-Rooted

A short structural summary of how the species map has been re-anchored over time:

- **1960s:** Discrete origins. Atlanteans (Everett, *Motion Picture Funnies Weekly* 1939, *Marvel Comics* #1 1939), Inhumans (Lee/Kirby, *Fantastic Four* #45, 1965), Skrulls (*Fantastic Four* #2, 1961), Kree (*Fantastic Four* #65, 1967), Asgardians (*Journey into Mystery* #83, 1962). No unifying frame.
- **1971:** Kree-Skrull War in *Avengers* #89–97 codifies inter-empire structure [T3] Wikipedia, "Kree–Skrull War".
- **1976:** Kirby's *Eternals* introduces Celestials as retroactive engineers of Earth life [T2] Marvel.com, "The Complete History of the Celestials, the Deviants, and the Eternals". Shi'ar debut in *Uncanny X-Men* #97 [T3] Wikipedia, "Shi'ar".
- **1982:** Brood debut, completing the cosmic-horror tier [T3] Wikipedia, "Brood (comics)".
- **2005:** *House of M* "No More Mutants" depopulates mutants to ~198 individuals, restructuring the X-gene from majority-future to endangered-species [T3] Wikipedia, "Decimation (comics)", https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Decimation_(comics).
- **2006:** Gaiman's *Eternals* makes the Eternals/Celestials relationship mechanical and deterministic [T3] TV Tropes, "Eternals (2006)". *Annihilation* destroys most of the Skrull Empire and the Nova Corps [T3] Marvel Database, "Annihilation Wave (Earth-616)".
- **2008:** *Secret Invasion* recasts decades of stories as Skrull infiltration [T3] Wikipedia, "Secret Invasion".
- **2013–2017:** Inhumans elevated as mutant-replacement; *Inhumans vs. X-Men* (2016) ends the experiment [T3] Wikipedia, "Inhumans vs. X-Men".
- **2018–2021:** Cates's *Venom* / *King in Black* retcons symbiotes into a Celestial-era cosmological pillar via Knull [T2] Marvel.com, "Meet Knull, the God of Symbiotes".
- **2019:** Hickman's *House of X / Powers of X* establishes Krakoa, mutant resurrection, and a sovereign-state structure [T2] Marvel.com, "The Secrets of Mutant Resurrection".
- **2020:** *Empyre* unifies Kree and Skrull empires under Hulkling [T3] Wikipedia, "Empyre".
- **2022:** *A.X.E.: Judgment Day* couples the X-gene Celestial origin to Eternal Prime Directive, classifies mutants as "deviant" [T3] Wikipedia, "A.X.E.: Judgment Day".

In the MCU (Earth-199999), the Celestial frame is reused and tightened: in *Eternals* (2021), Arishem reveals that Celestial seeds (e.g. Tiamut) gestate inside host planets, fuelled by sapient population energy, with Eternals as engineered shepherds — reframing the species as fundamentally biotech tools rather than gods [T3] Wikipedia, "Eternals (film)", https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eternals_(film). MCU mutants, as of *Deadpool & Wolverine* (2024), have an as-yet-unspecified origin distinct from this Celestial seeding.

The throughline across all retcons: Marvel's species map is *vertical*. It tends, over time, to push every interesting biology either upward toward the Celestials (mutants, Eternals, Deviants, Inhumans-via-Kree, Knull-via-Celestial-rejection) or laterally into the Elder Gods / abstract entities. The trend since 2018 (Cates, Hickman, Gillen) has been to make this verticality *explicit* and to use it as event fuel — Judgment Day, King in Black, Empyre, Krakoa.
