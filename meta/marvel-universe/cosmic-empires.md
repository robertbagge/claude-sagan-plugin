# Cosmic Empires & Galactic Geopolitics (Marvel-616, structural map)

This topic maps Marvel's interstellar polities as a *structural* system: home worlds, ruling architectures, and the long-arc consolidation pattern that runs from the original 1971 *Kree–Skrull War* through 2020's *Empyre* and its dissolution. Characters appear only as nodes (rulers, regents, ambassadors); the focus is the geometry of power.

The overarching pattern is consolidation by catastrophe: every two-to-three decades, a publishing-era cosmic crossover detonates one or more empires, then re-aggregates the survivors into a higher-order coalition. Operation: Galactic Storm (1992) collapses Kree autonomy into a Shi'ar protectorate; Annihilation (2006) ends the Skrull throneworld system as a continuous polity; Annihilation: Conquest (2007) does the same to the post-Galactic-Storm Kree; Infinity (2013) institutionalises the survivors as a Galactic Council; Empyre (2020) fuses Kree and Skrull under a single hybrid throne. Each event also re-tunes the Shi'ar Imperium, which functions as the system's stable counterweight.

## 1. The Kree Stellar Empire — Hala, Pama, and the Supreme Intelligence

The Kree are an "alien race... native to the planet Hala in the Large Magellanic Cloud" running "one of the oldest and most expansive empires" in the Marvel cosmos, militaristic and obsessed with genetic perfection [T3] Wikipedia, "Kree", https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kree. Their constitutional fiction is the Supreme Intelligence (Supremor): "an artificial intelligence that rules the alien race known as the Kree", first appearing in *Fantastic Four* #65 (Aug 1967) by Lee/Kirby [T3] Wikipedia, "Supreme Intelligence", https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supreme_Intelligence. The Supremor is "a vast cybernetic/organic computer system that contains the disembodied, preserved brains of the greatest statesmen and philosophers in Kree history" — a literalised fusion of state and archive, built originally on Kree-Lar to design a Cosmic Cube [T3] Wikipedia, "Supreme Intelligence", https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supreme_Intelligence.

Two structural characteristics matter:

1. **Stagnation as constitutional crisis.** The Supremor "reveals that the Kree are at an evolutionary dead-end and that it intends to jump-start the Kree's evolution"; in Operation: Galactic Storm (1992) it deliberately "detonates a Nega-Bomb to influence the Kree's genetic potential, only to kill billions of Kree" [T3] Wikipedia, "Supreme Intelligence", https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supreme_Intelligence; [T3] Wikipedia, "Operation: Galactic Storm", https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation:_Galactic_Storm.
2. **Annexation rather than independence.** After Galactic Storm, "the Shi'ar [...] annex the devastated Kree Empire, with Lilandra's sister Deathbird becoming viceroy of the Kree territories" [T3] Wikipedia, "Operation: Galactic Storm", https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation:_Galactic_Storm. The Kree Empire from 1992 onward is no longer a peer polity but a vassal/successor entity until *Annihilation: Conquest*.

The Kree home system itself is destroyed twice in the post-2000 era. In *The Black Vortex* (2015), "Mister Knife unleashed the full firepower of his flying fortress on Hala, resulting in the planet's destruction... the Supreme Intelligence stay[ed] behind and [was] destroyed with Hala" [T3] Wikipedia, "The Black Vortex", https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Black_Vortex. *Empyre* later builds a new capital, **Throneworld II**, "into the sacred ruins of Hala", which is renamed **New Hala** after the Alliance dissolves [T3] Marvel Database, "Throneworld II", https://marvel.fandom.com/wiki/Throneworld_II.

## 2. The Skrull Empire — Skrullos, Tarnax IV, and the diaspora

The Skrull constitutional history is a chain of capital relocations under existential pressure. The original homeworld was Skrullos; "roughly 100,000 years ago the Skrull capital was relocated to Tarnax IV", later renamed Throneworld, in the Andromeda Galaxy [T3] Marvel Database, "Tarnax IV", https://marvel.fandom.com/wiki/Tarnax_IV. The structural form is hereditary monarchy (Emperor Dorrek dynasty, then Empress R'Klll, Princess Anelle), with a rigid caste of warlord houses below.

The first decapitation strike comes from Galactus, not from a rival empire: "In *Fantastic Four* (1961) #257, Galactus destroyed Tarnax IV after having been led there by his herald, Frankie Raye, AKA Nova... Galactus consumed Tarnax IV, killing seven billion Skrulls, including R'klll and Princess Anelle" [T3] Marvel Database, "Tarnax IV", https://marvel.fandom.com/wiki/Tarnax_IV. From 1984 onward the Skrull Empire is a polity without a centre — "with the destruction of a central government, rebellions and chaos spread throughout the Andromeda galaxy" [T3] Marvel Database, "Tarnax IV", https://marvel.fandom.com/wiki/Tarnax_IV.

The second decapitation, twenty years later in publishing time, is total. In *Annihilation* (2006), the Annihilation Wave's planet-rendering weapon (the Harvester of Sorrows) means "it took them less than six months to eradicate what was left of the Skrull Empire" [T3] Villains Wiki, "Annihilation Wave", https://villains.fandom.com/wiki/Annihilation_Wave. This sets up *Secret Invasion* (2008): writer Brian Michael Bendis stated "the motivation for the invasion is the destruction of the Skrull Empire in the 2007 'Annihilation' storyline" [T3] Wikipedia, "Secret Invasion", https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secret_Invasion. Queen Veranke leads the diaspora's last coordinated military project; after her death "the last remnants of the Skrull armada were destroyed... the Skrulls were in disarray with many either imprisoned or dead while others were forced to flee" [T3] Wikipedia, "Secret Invasion", https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secret_Invasion. From 2008 to 2020 the Skrulls function as warlord factions rather than an empire — the Super-Skrull Kl'rt eventually consolidates them in *Infinity* and again on the road to *Empyre* [T3] Marvel Database, "Galactic Council (Earth-616)", https://marvel.fandom.com/wiki/Galactic_Council_(Earth-616).

## 3. The Shi'ar Imperium — Chandilar, Aerie, and the Imperial Guard

The Shi'ar are "cold-blooded humanoids of avian descent" whose imperium is "a vast collection of alien species, cultures, and worlds situated close to the Skrull and Kree empires" [T3] Wikipedia, "Shi'ar", https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shi'ar. Their power architecture has two distinctive features:

- **A throneworld-of-state model.** "The central base of power was located on the 'throneworld' Chandilar, while the Shi'ar homeworld was called Aerie." Chandilar is "an artificial planet, which the Shi'ar moved to from their original homeworld" [T3] Wikipedia, "Shi'ar", https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shi'ar. Marvel structurally distinguishes between the *capital of state* and the *species homeworld* — a design echoed by the Skrull Skrullos→Tarnax IV move and by the post-*Empyre* New Hala/Throneworld II split.
- **A nominal high council with executive override.** "The empire is nominally ruled over by a high council, which has representatives from a large majority of the alien races... However, in practice, the head of the council (the Majestor or Majestrix) exercises strong executive control and can institute policy virtually by decree" [T3] Marvel Database, "Shi'ar Empire", https://marvel.fandom.com/wiki/Shi'ar_Empire.

Enforcement runs through the **Imperial Guard**, formed historically "to stop Rook'shir, a Shi'ar who became the host of the Phoenix Force" and first appearing in *Uncanny X-Men* #107 (Oct 1977) by Claremont/Cockrum as a deliberate pastiche of DC's Legion of Super-Heroes [T3] Wikipedia, "Imperial Guard (Marvel Comics)", https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imperial_Guard_(Marvel_Comics). The Guard is structured in three tiers: "an elite corps of the Guard, dubbed Superguardians, protect the ruling emperor (majestor) or empress (majestrix)... the Borderers, aid the governors of conquered worlds in enforcing Shi'ar law... The ranks of the Guard are fed by the Imperial Guard Training Academy or Subguardians" [T3] Wikipedia, "Imperial Guard (Marvel Comics)", https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imperial_Guard_(Marvel_Comics). The Praetor (Gladiator/Kallark for most of modern continuity) commands the Superguardians.

The succession arc is the empire's longest dramatic axis: D'Ken (mad emperor, comatose after the M'Kraan crystal incident) → Lilandra Neramani (X-Men ally) → Deathbird (intermittent usurpation) → Vulcan (post-coup emperor during the Shi'ar/Kree conflicts of *War of Kings*) → Gladiator/Kallark (defects to Lilandra, eventually becomes Majestor after Lilandra's assassination) → Xandra Neramani (Lilandra/Xavier's daughter, current heir) [T3] Wikipedia, "Shi'ar", https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shi'ar; [T3] Marvel.com, "Lilandra Neramani", https://www.marvel.com/characters/lilandra-neramani.

## 4. Spartoi/Spartax, Brood, and Badoon — second-tier polities

Three additional empires sit at the next ring of importance and exhibit very different governance shapes.

**Spartoi/Spartax.** "The Spartoi, also called Spartax after their home planet, are believed to have a common ancestry with the Shi'ar" and run a hereditary monarchy out of Spartax [T3] Marvel Database, "Spartoi", https://marvel.fandom.com/wiki/Spartoi. The continuity here is famously messy: in early appearances "the Spartax Empire... existed in the Greater Magellanic Cloud", but a *Guardians of the Galaxy* (Vol. 3) #0.1 retcon "established that the father of [Earth-616] Star-Lord was J'son and that the Spartax Empire apparently existed in the Milky Way Galaxy", with classical-era Emperor Jason eventually re-assigned to Earth-791 [T3] Marvel Database, "Spartax (Planet)", https://marvel.fandom.com/wiki/Spartax_(Planet). Politically the Spartax matter because J'son sat on the inner Galactic Council during *Infinity* before being deposed by his son Peter Quill, who briefly held and then abandoned the throne [T3] Wikipedia, "J'son (character)", https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J'son_(character).

**Brood.** A non-state, non-monarchical polity — a parasitic hive species first appearing in *Uncanny X-Men* #155 (Mar 1982) by Claremont/Cockrum [T3] Wikipedia, "Brood (comics)", https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brood_(comics). Governance is biological: "The Empress is the ruler of the Brood and contains the species' hive mind... a matriarchal society. This caste based society began with the Empress and continued to the Firstborn, Queen, Dwarf Queen and the Drones" [T3] Marvel Database, "Brood", https://marvel.fandom.com/wiki/Brood. Succession crises produce "nest wars... whereby rival queens battled one another to assume control of the hive in a conflict that lasted several decades" [T3] Marvel Database, "Brood", https://marvel.fandom.com/wiki/Brood. The Brood are "decimated" by the Annihilation Wave and "now on the brink of extinction" — but a Brood Queen is nonetheless a Galactic Council member during *Infinity* [T3] Marvel Database, "Brood", https://marvel.fandom.com/wiki/Brood.

**Badoon.** First appearing in *Silver Surfer* #2 (Oct 1968), the Badoon are "renowned as one of the galaxy's oldest spacefaring empires", originating from Lotiara [T3] Wikipedia, "Badoon", https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Badoon. Uniquely, they have a structural gender schism: "the Brotherhood of Badoon, ruled by a Brother Royal, lived on the planet Moord and founded the Badoon Empire; the Sisterhood of Badoon, ruled by a Queen, resided on the Badoon homeworld of Lotiara" [T3] Marvel Database, "Badoon", https://marvel.fandom.com/wiki/Badoon. The two societies "periodically reunite for reproduction under controlled conditions". At their peak, "at the time of the Annihilation Wave, the Badoon control 37.7% of the Milky Way Galaxy" [T3] Marvel Database, "Badoon", https://marvel.fandom.com/wiki/Badoon.

## 5. The Kree–Skrull treaty geometry — from 1971 to *Empyre*

The original *Kree–Skrull War* (Avengers #89–97, June 1971 – March 1972, Roy Thomas / Sal Buscema / Neal Adams / John Buscema) sets the long-arc treaty geometry between Marvel's two oldest empires [T3] Wikipedia, "Kree–Skrull War", https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kree%E2%80%93Skrull_War. Thomas explicitly framed it as a Cold War / McCarthy / Arab–Israeli analogue, "with the Earth as one of their battlefields" — Earth as Korea/Vietnam, Kree and Skrull as the superpowers [T3] Wikipedia, "Kree–Skrull War", https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kree%E2%80%93Skrull_War. The structural legacy was a permanent, unresolved Cold War footing between the two empires that survived every subsequent retcon.

The 1992 Operation: Galactic Storm storyline (19 parts across Avengers titles, March–May 1992) shifts the geometry from binary to triangular: the Shi'ar declare war on the Kree, the Avengers split three ways over how to intervene, and the Supreme Intelligence orchestrates the Nega-Bomb detonation that kills "billions" of Kree to force genetic evolution [T3] Wikipedia, "Operation: Galactic Storm", https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation:_Galactic_Storm. Outcome: Shi'ar annexation of the Kree, with Deathbird as viceroy. The Kree–Skrull dyad is replaced for fifteen publishing years by a Shi'ar-dominated triangle.

The 2006–2007 Annihilation cycle then resets both successor structures. *Annihilation* destroys the Skrull Empire's last contiguous territory; *Annihilation: Conquest* (2007) sees the techno-organic Phalanx, "using the Space Knights as pawns unwittingly brought to the Kree homeworld of Hala by the former Star-Lord, Peter Quill", "assimilating the majority of the population and encasing the Kree Galaxy in a force field" — Ultron is revealed as the architect [T3] Wikipedia, "Annihilation: Conquest", https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Annihilation:_Conquest. By 2008 both empires are functionally collapsed.

What follows is reconstruction-by-coalition rather than reconstruction-by-restoration.

## 6. The Galactic Council — institutionalisation during *Infinity* (2013)

*Infinity* (Aug–Nov 2013, Hickman) creates Marvel's first stable inner-circle multi-empire institution. The Galactic Council "was an intergalactic organization which was created to deal with important matters in the universe. Represented are the races of eight galaxies (those of known space), and other dimensions (e.g. Asgard and the Negative Zone). It consisted of an inner council that is made up of the leaders of the major empires and an assembly of representatives from thousands of worlds" [T3] Marvel Database, "Galactic Council (Earth-616)", https://marvel.fandom.com/wiki/Galactic_Council_(Earth-616).

The inner council assembled "at the Citadel of the Galactic Council on Nomad for a war council, with council leaders including Gladiator of the Shi'ar Empire, the Supreme Intelligence and Ronan the Accuser of the Kree Empire, and J'son of the Spartax Empire" against the Builders [T3] Marvel Database, "Galactic Council (Earth-616)", https://marvel.fandom.com/wiki/Galactic_Council_(Earth-616). The structural breakthrough is membership of historical antagonists: "Kl'rt the Super-Skrull managed to convince most of the Skrull warlords to unite under him and present himself to the Council for admittance, and historical enemies such as the Brood Queen and Annihilus of the Negative Zone agreed to join forces against the Builders" [T3] Marvel Database, "Galactic Council (Earth-616)", https://marvel.fandom.com/wiki/Galactic_Council_(Earth-616). For the first time the Marvel cosmos has a UN-style standing body that includes a re-consolidated Skrull seat (under Kl'rt), Annihilus' Negative Zone, and the Brood — alongside the long-recognised Kree, Shi'ar, Spartax, and Asgardian thrones.

After *Empyre*, "the Galactic Council convened at a new space station called the Proscenium built specifically to serve as an intergalactic neutral ground in order to review galactic treaties" — the institution survives the Kree–Skrull merger and acquires neutral physical chambers [T3] Marvel Database, "Galactic Council (Earth-616)", https://marvel.fandom.com/wiki/Galactic_Council_(Earth-616).

## 7. *Empyre* (2020) and the Kree/Skrull Alliance

*Empyre* (July 2020, Al Ewing & Dan Slott) collapses the original 1971 dyad into a single hybrid throne [T3] Wikipedia, "Empyre", https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Empyre. The political logic: the Cotati (the plant-based ancient enemy of both Kree and Skrull) move to exterminate animal life, forcing the two empires into common defence. "The two empires decided to set their differences aside and united into one benevolent civilization under the leadership of Skrull-Kree Hybrid Emperor Hulkling" — Teddy Altman, crowned Dorrek VIII, "the first royal hybrid in a thousand of years, and the son of the Kree champion Mar-Vell and the Skrull princess Anelle" [T3] Marvel.com, "The Skrull and Kree Unite", https://www.marvel.com/articles/comics/the-skrull-and-kree-unite-in-road-to-empyre-the-kree-skrull-war-1.

Hulkling marries Wiccan (Billy Kaplan), making him "Prince-Consort and Court Wizard" of the Alliance — the first openly queer royal union to head a Marvel cosmic empire [T3] Wikipedia, "Empyre", https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Empyre. The new capital, Throneworld II, is built "into the sacred ruins of Hala", deliberately collapsing the two empires' geographies as well as their thrones [T3] Marvel Database, "Throneworld II", https://marvel.fandom.com/wiki/Throneworld_II.

The Alliance is structurally short-lived. "The Alliance came to an abrupt and violent end with the betrayal by the Skrulls, who came to the belief that their alliance went against their very nature, and they never got over the contempt they held for the Kree." Before that, "the Alliance became a force of good in the universe as it helped in the invasions of Knull, Dormammu, and the Reckoning" — meaning the unified state functioned as the principal cosmic responder during the King in Black, Death of Doctor Strange, and Reckoning War crossovers [T3] Marvel Database, "Kree/Skrull Alliance (Earth-616)", https://marvel.fandom.com/wiki/Kree/Skrull_Alliance_(Earth-616). After dissolution, "the reformed Kree Empire renamed [Throneworld II] to New Hala" — the structural cycle returns to two distinct empires, but with the precedent that fusion is now part of the canon's possibility space [T3] Marvel Database, "Throneworld II", https://marvel.fandom.com/wiki/Throneworld_II.

## 8. The long-arc consolidation pattern (1971 → 2020s)

| Era | Trigger event | Structural state |
|---|---|---|
| 1971–1984 | *Kree–Skrull War* (Avengers #89–97) | Binary Cold War: Kree (Hala/Supremor) vs. Skrull (Tarnax IV/Dorrek dynasty); Shi'ar introduced 1976 as third pole |
| 1984–1992 | *Fantastic Four* #257 — Galactus eats Tarnax IV | Skrull decentralisation; warlord factions; Kree and Shi'ar dominant |
| 1992–2006 | Operation: Galactic Storm; Nega-Bomb | Shi'ar annexation of Kree territories; Deathbird viceroy; triangular geometry collapsed to Shi'ar primacy |
| 2006–2013 | *Annihilation* (2006); *Annihilation: Conquest* (2007); *Secret Invasion* (2008); *War of Kings* (2009) | Both Kree and Skrull empires functionally collapsed; Shi'ar fragments under Vulcan; Inhumans briefly inherit Kree throne |
| 2013–2020 | *Infinity* — Galactic Council formalised at the Citadel/Nomad | Inner-council institutionalised; Skrull seat reconstituted under Kl'rt; Brood and Negative Zone admitted |
| 2020–c. 2022 | *Empyre* — Kree/Skrull Alliance under Hulkling/Dorrek VIII | Single hybrid throne; Throneworld II atop Hala; Galactic Council moves to neutral Proscenium |
| Post-*Reckoning War* | Skrull betrayal dissolves Alliance | Two-empire restoration: New Hala (Kree) and a re-emergent Skrull state; Galactic Council persists |

The structural read: Marvel's interstellar map is *less* multipolar than it looks. From Kirby through Hickman, only three polities — Kree, Skrull, Shi'ar — function as continuous great powers, with Spartax, Brood, Badoon, Annihilus' Negative Zone, and Asgard rotating in as second-rank seats. The recurring narrative gesture is to destroy a great power (Galactus eats Tarnax IV; Nega-Bomb on Hala; Phalanx assimilation; Annihilation Wave on the Skrulls; Slaughter Lords on Hala) and then institutionalise the next layer of cooperation (Galactic Council, then Kree/Skrull Alliance). What looks like an expanding cosmic stage is in practice a fifty-year consolidation arc into fewer, larger, more federated entities.
