# Reality-altering artifacts & MacGuffins

Marvel's universe is structured around a small set of objects that can be picked up by characters but which behave more like cosmological infrastructure than props. Their publication histories reveal Marvel's editorial mechanics more clearly than most character arcs do: artifacts get renamed, destroyed and restored on a roughly decadal cadence, almost always tied to a line-wide event (Thanos Quest 1990, Black September 1995, Hickman's incursions 2013, Marvel Legacy 2017, Infinity Wars 2018). The map below traces the seven artifact families named in the brief — Infinity Gems/Stones, the Ego Gem, Cosmic Cubes (Tesseract/Kobik), the M'Kraan Crystal, the Heart of the Universe, the Ultimate Nullifier, the Wand of Watoomb, the Eye of Agamotto, the Casket of Ancient Winters — across the principal continuities: Earth-616, the MCU (Earth-199999), the Ultraverse, and key structural side-realities.

## The Infinity Gems on Earth-616: from "Soul Gems" to "Infinity Stones"

Marvel's premier MacGuffins began life under a different name. The first gem appeared in *Marvel Premiere* #1 (1972) as a single "Soul Gem" worn by Adam Warlock, and *Captain Marvel* #41–43 (1976) established a set of six Soul Gems — the term "Infinity Gem" did not exist yet [T3] Wikipedia, "Infinity Gems," https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infinity_Gems. The 1990 *Thanos Quest* two-issue series by Jim Starlin is the inflection point: Thanos relieves the Elders of the Universe of the six Soul Gems, renames them the Infinity Gems and fits them to the Infinity Gauntlet [T3] ComicsAlliance, "The History Of The Infinity Stones Explained," https://comicsalliance.com/marvel-infinity-stones/.

Once the Gauntlet is undone in *Infinity Gauntlet* (1991), Adam Warlock is ordered by the Living Tribunal to ensure no single being holds more than one Gem. Warlock convenes the Infinity Watch in *Warlock and the Infinity Watch* #1 (1992) and distributes the gems: Warlock keeps Soul, Gamora takes Time, Pip the Troll takes Space, Drax holds Power, Moondragon holds Mind, and — controversially, in secret — Thanos is given Reality [T3] Marvel Database, "Infinity Watch (Earth-616)," https://marvel.fandom.com/wiki/Infinity_Watch_(Earth-616); [T2] Marvel.com, "Infinity Watch — Members, Enemies, Powers," https://www.marvel.com/teams-and-groups/infinity-watch. Through the entire Marvel Universe Handbook era of the 1990s and 2000s these were referred to with the suffix "Gem," matched to Starlin-era colours (Soul orange, Time green, Mind blue, Power red, Space purple, Reality yellow), differing from the MCU palette later adopted [T3] Marvel Database, "Infinity Stones," https://marvel.fandom.com/wiki/Infinity_Stones.

Hickman's *New Avengers* run breaks the toy. In *New Avengers* #3 (2013), Captain America wields a fully assembled Gauntlet against an Incursion; the strain shatters the gems and renders the Gauntlet inert, eliminating it as an Incursion countermeasure for the rest of *Time Runs Out* and forcing the Illuminati toward Earth-destruction as the only remaining tool [T3] Sequart, "Jonathan Hickman's New Avengers #2: In Secret, They Rule," http://sequart.org/magazine/70512/jonathan-hickman-new-avengers-2/; [T3] CBR, "16 Superheroes (And Villains) Who Used The Infinity Gauntlet," https://www.cbr.com/superheroes-who-used-the-infinity-gauntlet/. The shattering is structurally important: it removes the Infinity Gems from continuity for nearly four years and clears the field for *Secret Wars* (2015) to rewrite Marvel's reality.

Marvel Legacy in 2017 restores the gems, now permanently rebranded **Infinity Stones** with colours retconned to match the MCU (yellow→Mind, blue→Space, red→Power, orange→Soul, green→Time, purple→Reality), making the comics palette align with screen merchandise [T3] Wikipedia, "Infinity Gems," https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infinity_Gems. In *Infinity Countdown* and *Infinity Wars* (2018), the wielder Requiem (an alternate Gamora) collapses half the universe into a "Warp World" inside the Soul Stone; Adam Warlock and the Cosmic Avengers ultimately restore reality while preserving Warp World inside the Soul Gem, and the Stones are afterwards bonded to living "Stone Avatars" rather than redistributed to a Watch [T3] Wikipedia, "Infinity Wars," https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infinity_Wars; [T3] Marvel Database, "Adam Warlock (Earth-616)," https://marvel.fandom.com/wiki/Adam_Warlock_(Earth-616). In 2024 Donny Cates' *Thanos: Death Notes* and follow-on stories introduced a "seventh stone" idea reworked as a **Death Stone**, a separate retcon distinct from the 1990s Ego Gem that explicitly modifies forty-eight years of Marvel lore [T3] ScreenRant, "Thanos Officially Introduces Seventh Infinity Stone, Permanently Changing 48 Years of Marvel Lore," https://screenrant.com/thanos-new-infinity-stone-death-stone-ego-gem/.

## The Ego Gem and the Ultraverse seventh-stone retcon

The first time a "seventh gem" entered Marvel continuity was a 1995 piece of corporate machinery rather than a story idea. Marvel had purchased Malibu Comics and the Ultraverse in late 1994 and tried to drive Ultraverse sales by importing Marvel franchises [T3] Multiversity Comics, "Crossed-Out Crossovers: Black September," http://www.multiversitycomics.com/news-columns/crossed-out-crossovers-black-september/. In the *Rune/Silver Surfer* one-shot (1995) the Ultraverse vampire Rune steals the six Infinity Gems from the Infinity Watch; Loki then walks across the Malibu line collecting them and discovers a seventh gem hidden in the Ultraverse — the **Ego Gem** [T3] CBR, "Loki Fell Into Marvel's Lost Universe With the Infinity Gems," https://www.cbr.com/loki-marvel-ultraverse-infinity-gems/.

The in-fiction retcon: all seven gems were originally aspects of a primordial entity called Nemesis, who suicided rather than exist alone. Six gems carried Nemesis's external-reality powers; the seventh carried its mind, and any wielder of it inside the Ultraverse was overwritten by Nemesis's psyche [T3] Marvunapp, "Nemesis (Infinity Gems, Ultraverse story)," http://www.marvunapp.com/Appendix/nemesise.htm; [T2] Marvel.com, "Nemesis (Infinity Gems Restored)," https://www.marvel.com/characters/nemesis-infinity-gems-restored. In *Avengers/Ultraforce* #1 the Ego Gem possesses Sersi, merges with the other six to reform Nemesis, and is defeated when the Black Knight shatters Nemesis's crown; the Ego Gem's essence is dispersed into the Power, Soul, Mind and Space gems, which acquire autonomous personalities afterwards [T3] CBR, "The Secret History of Marvel's Seventh Infinity Stone," https://www.cbr.com/marvel-avengers-seventh-infinity-stone-ultraverse-nemesis/. Black September (1996) sealed the Ultraverse off from the main Marvel multiverse for legal reasons; the Ego Gem effectively died with Malibu and has not been restored, though it remains the canonical answer to "why are the Power/Soul/Mind/Space gems sentient" in 1990s–2000s continuity.

## MCU Infinity Stones (Earth-199999)

The MCU rebuilt the Stones from scratch with a clean cosmology. The six Stones are explicitly created from six singularities that predated the Big Bang, exposition delivered by the Collector in *Guardians of the Galaxy* (2014) and reinforced in *Avengers: Infinity War* (2018) [T3] Wikipedia, "Infinity Stones," https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infinity_Stones; [T3] MCU Wiki, "Infinity Stones," https://marvelcinematicuniverse.fandom.com/wiki/Infinity_Stones. There are six, not seven; they are called Stones from day one; and each is housed inside a containing artifact rather than left bare:

- **Space Stone — Tesseract.** Introduced in the *Thor* (2011) post-credit scene and centred in *Captain America: The First Avenger* and *The Avengers*; tracked through *Thor: Ragnarok* (Loki secretly pockets it) to *Infinity War*, where Thanos kills Loki for it [T3] The Ringer, "A Comprehensive Guide to the Infinity Stones," https://www.theringer.com/2019/04/22/movies/avengers-endgame-thanos-infinity-stones-guide.
- **Reality Stone — Aether.** *Thor: The Dark World* (2013) reframes the comics' Casket-era Malekith as a forger of an MCU-original liquid form of the Reality Stone, the Aether, used to revert the universe to primordial darkness during Convergence; left with the Collector on Knowhere until Thanos retrieves it [T3] MCU Wiki, "Reality Stone," https://marvelcinematicuniverse.fandom.com/wiki/Reality_Stone.
- **Mind Stone — Loki's Scepter / Vision's forehead.** Given to Loki by Thanos in *The Avengers*, recovered in *Age of Ultron*, embedded in Vision; killed by Wanda then taken anyway by Thanos in *Infinity War* [T3] Wikipedia, "Infinity Stones."
- **Power Stone — Orb.** *Guardians of the Galaxy* (2014); held briefly by the Nova Corps on Xandar; lost when Thanos sacks Xandar off-screen between *Thor: Ragnarok* and *Infinity War* [T3] The Ringer, https://www.theringer.com/2019/04/22/movies/avengers-endgame-thanos-infinity-stones-guide.
- **Time Stone — Eye of Agamotto.** *Doctor Strange* (2016) — see mystic-relics section for the divergence from 616.
- **Soul Stone — Vormir.** Hidden behind a sacrifice mechanic guarded by the Red Skull; *Infinity War*. Black Widow dies for it in the Endgame time heist [T3] Wikipedia, "Infinity Stones."

The Endgame "time heist" introduces a structurally important MCU-only concept: temporally-displaced duplicate Stones that, once removed from a branched timeline, must be returned to prevent universe-ending instability. Loki's escape with the 2012 Tesseract spawns the *Loki* Disney+ continuity and the TVA's confiscation room of dozens of Tesseract variants, broadening the MCU's "Stones are infrastructure" treatment in the Multiverse Saga [T3] MCU Wiki, "Infinity Stones." After Tony Stark's snap in *Endgame* (2019) the six prime Stones are destroyed; only variant Stones in branched timelines persist as of 2026 MCU continuity.

## Cosmic Cubes — Tesseract, Kobik, and the Beyonder connection

The Cosmic Cube is Marvel's other reality-rewrite engine and predates the Infinity Gems by six years: it debuts in *Tales of Suspense* #79 (1966), built on Earth by A.I.M. and stolen by the Red Skull [T3] Wikipedia, "Cosmic Cube," https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cosmic_Cube; [T2] Marvel.com, "Cosmic Cube (Tessaract) History, Owners, & Powers," https://www.marvel.com/items/cosmic-cube. Later writers retconned the design lineage to the Skrulls, who first opened a meta-singularity into the Beyonder Realm and trapped an "X-Element" of that realm inside a containment cube; A.I.M. and the Kree's Supreme Intelligence later built their own Cubes from the same blueprint [T3] CBR, "Gleaming The Cosmic Cube: A History of Marvel's Most Powerful Reality-Altering Artifact," https://www.cbr.com/gleaming-the-cosmic-cube-a-history-of-marvels-most-powerful-reality-altering-artifact/.

The Cube–Beyonder retcon is the most significant cosmological move in the artifact's history. After enough use, a Cube becomes sentient (*Captain America* #143 onward), maturing through stages exemplified by the Shaper of Worlds and Kubik. Once Hickman reframed the Beyonders as a hostile race in *New Avengers* (2013) and the original *Secret Wars* (1984) Beyonder as one of their immature offspring, the Marvel reality-warp ladder gained a unified backstory: sentient Cubes are Beyonder larvae [T3] Wikipedia, "Beyonder/Kosmos," https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kosmos_(Marvel_Comics); [T3] CBR, "Gleaming The Cosmic Cube." The Cube is therefore the primary in-fiction bridge between Earth supervillain plots and the highest tier of multiversal cosmology.

**Kobik** is the most recent named Cube. In *Avengers: Standoff!* (2016), S.H.I.E.L.D. (Maria Hill) fuses fragments from multiple shattered Cubes into a child-form sentient Cube and uses her to brainwash imprisoned villains in the gated community Pleasant Hill [T3] Wikipedia, "Avengers: Standoff!," https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Avengers:_Standoff!; [T2] Marvel.com, "Kobik — Marvel 101," https://www.marvel.com/articles/comics/kobik-marvel-101. One of Kobik's source fragments was a Cube once wielded by the Red Skull's clone, who manipulates her into rewriting Steve Rogers as a lifelong Hydra agent — the trigger for *Captain America: Steve Rogers* #1 (2016) and *Secret Empire* (2017) [T2] Marvel.com, "Secret Empire Exposed: Kobik," https://www.marvel.com/articles/comics/secret-empire-exposed-kobik. Kobik is structurally important because it is the mechanism Marvel used to render a major character (Cap-as-Hydra) reversible without retconning the story away.

The MCU's Tesseract is, importantly, a *Cube*, not the Cube — in MCU continuity it is simply a containment vessel for the Space Stone with no sentience or Beyonder linkage. This is the cleanest example of MCU continuity collapsing two distinct 616 artifact families into one prop.

## Cosmic-scale singletons: M'Kraan Crystal, Heart of the Universe, Ultimate Nullifier

Three artifacts sit above ordinary cosmic-scale items because they are explicitly framed as universe-level infrastructure rather than weapons.

The **M'Kraan Crystal** entered continuity in the Phoenix Saga (*Uncanny X-Men* #107–108, 1977) as a Shi'ar relic — a vast pink crystal containing a city, at the centre of which a neutron galaxy doubles as the nexus point of all matter and antimatter across all realities [T3] X-Men Wiki, "M'kraan Crystal," https://x-men.fandom.com/wiki/M'kraan_Crystal; [T3] uncannyxmen.net, "M'Kraan Crystal," https://uncannyxmen.net/objects/mkraan-crystal. The Crystal exists simultaneously in every reality, so cracking it in any one universe propagates a chain reaction that can unmake the multiverse — the existential threat that drives Jean Grey's first sacrifice. A 2024 *Rise of the Powers of X*-era retcon, drawing on the Shi'ar tractate "The Violet Raptor," made the Crystal explicit: it is the "base code" of reality, with the rules of magic, space and time literally inscribed on it; it is a foundation stone, not a weapon [T3] ScreenRant, "After 48 Years, Marvel Reveals the Lore of X-Men's Most Powerful Artifact," https://screenrant.com/x-men-mkraan-crystal-explained-backstory-powers-explainer/. Shards have been used as plot tools, e.g. powering the Tallus and the Exiles' reality-hopping mechanic, and the Crystal restored Charles Xavier's X-Gene after Wanda Maximoff's M-Day depowering [T3] X-Men Wiki, https://x-men.fandom.com/wiki/M'kraan_Crystal. The Crystal does not appear in MCU continuity.

The **Heart of the Universe** is unique in that it is the only major Marvel reality-warp artifact that is officially non-canonical. It appears in Jim Starlin and Al Milgrom's *Marvel Universe: The End* (2003), where it is described as an energy source discovered a millennium ago by alien explorers who called themselves the Celestial Order [T3] Wikipedia, "Marvel: The End," https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marvel:_The_End. Thanos absorbs it and becomes literally one with the universe — every entity, including the Living Tribunal, becomes a fragment of him — making this the high-water mark of Thanos's onscreen power, dwarfing the Infinity Gauntlet [T3] CBR, "Marvel: 10 Things You Need To Know About The Heart Of The Universe," https://www.cbr.com/marvel-things-need-know-heart-of-universe-trivia/. He discovers a fundamental flaw will doom the universe, sacrifices himself to restart it, and Adam Warlock witnesses the reset. Marvel Executive Editor Tom Brevoort has stated that all Marvel "The End" series, this one included, are not in mainstream continuity, so the Heart functions as Marvel's official ceiling on "what's the maximum reality-warp artifact" without disturbing 616 cosmology [T3] Wikipedia, "Marvel: The End."

The **Ultimate Nullifier** debuted in *Fantastic Four* #50 (May 1966), retrieved by the Human Torch from Galactus's homeworld at the Watcher's direction, threatened by Reed Richards, and made Galactus retreat [T3] Wikipedia, "Ultimate Nullifier," https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ultimate_Nullifier. Originally the only weapon that could frighten Galactus, it has the property that firing it erases its target from existence — and, if the wielder's mind is insufficient, the wielder along with it, in violation of conservation of mass. The 2024 *Fantastic Four* run gives the Nullifier an in-universe origin: it was built by the Watcher Emnu to stop the Prosilican Empire, which had received Watcher technology and was using it to invade other worlds; the resulting first firing destroyed the Prosilicans and nine-tenths of the surrounding universe, leaving the region known as the Barrens [T3] ScreenRant, "Galactus' Ultimate Weapon Is Finally Explained in Huge Marvel Lore Change," https://screenrant.com/galactus-ultimate-nullifier-explained-huge-marvel-lore-change/. The Nullifier is exclusively a 616 artifact; the MCU has not adapted it as of 2026.

## Mystic relics: Wand of Watoomb, Eye of Agamotto, Casket of Ancient Winters

The mystical-relic tier is structurally distinct from the cosmic tier. These items are weapons of mystic individuals rather than universe infrastructure, but each has had a major continuity-altering retcon.

The **Wand of Watoomb** debuts in *Amazing Spider-Man Annual* #2 (1965), the first Spider-Man / Doctor Strange team-up, by Lee and Ditko [T3] Marvel Database, "Wand of Watoomb," https://marvel.fandom.com/wiki/Wand_of_Watoomb; [T3] Marvunapp, "Wand of Watoomb," http://www.marvunapp.com/Appendix5/wandofwatoomb.htm. In-fiction, the wizard Watoomb retired and split his wand between two apprentices — Stephen Strange and Cyrus Black — for a duel in which Strange won and reassembled the wand. It absorbs and stores mystical energy, opens interdimensional apertures, and amplifies spells. The MCU introduced the Wand in *Black Widow* (2021) as one of Strange's recovered relics, and it appears throughout Doctor Strange's onscreen sanctum dressing [T3] MCU Wiki, "Wand of Watoomb," https://marvelcinematicuniverse.fandom.com/wiki/Wand_of_Watoomb.

The **Eye of Agamotto** has the cleanest continuity divergence on this list. In 616, Agamotto was a mortal son of the Elder Goddess Oshtur who became Earth's first Sorcerer Supreme and a member of the Vishanti; the Eye is his creation and has nothing to do with the Infinity Stones [T3] Wikipedia, "Eye of Agamotto," https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eye_of_Agamotto; [T3] Marvel Database, "Eye of Agamotto," https://marvel.fandom.com/wiki/Eye_of_Agamotto. Its powers are scrying (replaying recent events), revealing illusion, opening portals to other dimensions, and projecting an "all-revealing light" repulsive to demons. *Marvel Legacy* (2017) retroactively places Agamotto wearing the Eye in the Stone Age Avengers c. 1,000,000 BC. The MCU, by contrast, makes the Eye an Agamotto-built containment device for the Time Stone — a wholesale fusion of two unrelated 616 artifacts to reduce on-screen complexity [T3] MCU Wiki, "Eye of Agamotto," https://marvelcinematicuniverse.fandom.com/wiki/Eye_of_Agamotto.

The **Casket of Ancient Winters** is a Walt Simonson creation, debuting in *Thor* #346 (August 1984) [T3] Marvel Database, "Casket of Ancient Winters," https://marvel.fandom.com/wiki/Casket_of_Ancient_Winters; [T2] Marvel.com, "Casket of Ancient Winters History, Owners, & Powers," https://www.marvel.com/items/casket-of-ancient-winters. It contains the Fimbulwinter of Ymir, a stored apocalyptic winter; long held by the Frost Giants until Asgardians captured it during the 965 AD battle of Tønsberg, Norway. Malekith the Accused — also a Simonson creation, *Thor* #344 (1984) — used it to wage the Surtur Saga, ultimately destroying the Casket and unleashing endless winter on Earth, an event that frees Surtur from Muspelheim [T3] Wikipedia, "Malekith the Accursed," https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malekith_the_Accursed. The MCU's *Thor* (2011) repurposes the Casket as the principal Frost Giant weapon recovered by Odin and stored in Asgard's vaults, and *Thor: The Dark World* (2013) recasts Malekith around the unrelated Reality Stone instead of the Casket — a deliberate compression of two Simonson stories into one MCU villain [T3] MCU Wiki, "Casket of Ancient Winters," https://marvelcinematicuniverse.fandom.com/wiki/Casket_of_Ancient_Winters.

## Cross-continuity status (2026 snapshot)

| Artifact | Earth-616 status | MCU status | Other major continuities |
|---|---|---|---|
| Infinity Stones | Restored, bonded to living Stone Avatars; "Death Stone" 2024 retcon adds seventh | All six destroyed by Stark snap (2019); variants persist in branched timelines via *Loki* TVA | Ultimate (1610): functional but minor; *What If…?* T'Challa-Thanos uses them |
| Ego Gem | Inert / dispersed; Ultraverse line dormant since Black September | Not adapted | Ultraverse only |
| Cosmic Cube (general) | Multiple in circulation; sentient Cubes = Beyonder larvae | Tesseract = Space Stone vessel only, not sentient | Ultimate Tesseract appears in Ultimate Nullifier–like role |
| Kobik | Active, used to restore aged Steve Rogers | Not adapted | None |
| M'Kraan Crystal | Active, retconned 2024 as "base code of reality" | Not adapted | Exiles continuity references via Tallus shard |
| Heart of the Universe | Officially non-canonical (per Brevoort) | Not adapted | Marvel: The End only |
| Ultimate Nullifier | Active; 2024 origin retcon (Watcher Emnu / Prosilicans) | Not adapted | Ultimate (1610) variant exists |
| Wand of Watoomb | Active, Strange's possession | Sanctum prop; *Black Widow* (2021) cameo | None notable |
| Eye of Agamotto | Active; 616 mystic relic, no Time-Stone link | Destroyed with Time Stone (Infinity War 2018) | None notable |
| Casket of Ancient Winters | Reforged; *War of the Realms* era usage | Asgardian vault; no Reality-Stone connection | None notable |

The pattern is consistent: every flagship Marvel artifact except the explicitly non-canonical Heart of the Universe has been destroyed and restored at least once, with naming changes reliably tracking media pressure (Soul Gem → Infinity Gem → Infinity Stone shadows the comics→film commercial pipeline). The MCU's principal cosmological move was compression — Cosmic Cube and Tesseract become the same prop, Eye of Agamotto absorbs the Time Stone, Malekith absorbs the Casket-of-Ancient-Winters arc into a Reality-Stone arc. The 2010s 616 trend ran the opposite direction: Hickman destroyed everything in 2013, Marvel Legacy in 2017 restored it under MCU branding, and post-2018 Marvel preferred bonding artifacts to people (Stone Avatars, Kobik-as-child) rather than depositing them with abstract custodians.
