# Pantheons & God-Tier Groups in the Marvel Universe

A structural map of Earth's divine hierarchies in Earth-616 (with notes where the MCU diverges), the cosmological "spine" that ties them together, the umbrella **Council of Godheads / Council of Skyfathers**, and how the picture has been retconned across the Lee/Kirby foundation, the Bronze Age expansion, the Walt Simonson and Roy Thomas annual-driven retcons, the Pak/Van Lente *Incredible Hercules / Chaos War* era, Jason Aaron's *Thor: God of Thunder / Mighty Thor / War of the Realms* run, and Al Ewing's *Immortal Thor* (2023–2025), which has done the most aggressive structural surgery on this layer of the universe in two decades.

## 1. The Cosmological Spine: Demiurge, Elder Gods, Demogorge

Almost every Earth-pantheon in 616 is, structurally, downstream of the same metaphysical event. The **Demiurge Primordial** is the sentient life-force of Earth's biosphere; it seeded the planet and gave rise to the **Elder Gods of Earth** (Gaea, Set, Chthon, Oshtur, Hyppus, Tiamat, Gaea-twin Chton-variants, etc.) [T3] Marvel Database, "Elder Gods of Earth" entry, https://marvel.fandom.com/wiki/Elder_Gods_of_Earth ; [T2] marvel.com character page, "Gaea — Powers, Enemies, History," https://www.marvel.com/characters/gaea . When most Elder Gods imitated **Set** in cannibalising their kin and degenerating into demons, Gaea mated with the Demiurge to bear **Atum**, the sun-god who in his "Demogorge the God-Eater" aspect devoured the corrupted Elder Gods; Set, Chthon, and a handful of others escaped into other dimensions [T2] marvel.com, "Atum / Demogorge" character page, https://www.marvel.com/characters/atum ; [T3] Marvel Database, "Demiurge Primordial (Earth-616)," https://marvel.fandom.com/wiki/Demiurge_Primordial_(Earth-616) .

The crucial structural beat is that "Mankind's nascent consciousnesses tapped into the godstuff that Demogorge cast from his body, causing new gods to be born in Man's image," producing every later Earth pantheon — Asgardian, Olympian, Heliopolitan, Vodū, Daevas, Annunaki, Tuatha de Danaan, Amatsu-Kami, etc. [T3] MarvUnApp, "Demogorge" entry, http://www.marvunapp.com/Appendix/atum.htm . This single retcon (drilled in across *Thor* annuals and *Saga of the Sub-Mariner*) is what allows pantheons created decades apart in real-world publication to share a single in-universe origin point and explains why every pantheon has a "skyfather + chthonic shadow" pair (Odin/Surtur, Zeus/Typhon, Ra/Apophis, Vishnu/Yama, etc.).

Al Ewing's *Immortal Thor* (2023–2025) layered an entirely new shelf onto this spine: **Utgard**, "the Land-of-True-Things," is revealed as a parallel exile-realm where five **Elder Gods** — **Toranos** (Utgard-Thor), **Utgard-Loki**, **Kemur**, **Mejed**, and **Nrgl** — fled the Demogorge instead of being eaten, mirroring the canonical Asgardian quintet [T3] Marvel Database, "Toranos (Earth-616)," https://marvel.fandom.com/wiki/Toranos_(Earth-616) ; [T3] CBR, Stephen Schleicher, "Marvel's New Utgard Gods Introduced in Immortal Thor Comics," https://www.cbr.com/immortal-thor-new-utgard-gods-trivia-marvel-comics/ . By making Utgard explicitly older than Asgard and casting the Asgardians as a kind of Demiurge-derived "second draft," Ewing re-frames the entire pantheon layer of the universe as a sequel to a primordial god-war, not its origin.

## 2. Asgard and the Ragnarok Cycle

**Home dimension:** Asgard, one of the Ten (later Nine, then Ten again) Realms strung along **Yggdrasil**, the World Tree; reachable from Midgard via the **Bifrost**. **Skyfather:** Odin All-Father (with rotating regents — Thor, Balder, Freyja, Loki — across the modern run).

The Asgardians are the *engine* of pantheon retcons in Marvel because Thor titles drive almost every cosmological revision. Three retcon waves matter:

- **Walt Simonson era (1983–1987):** establishes Surtur's "Twilight Sword" Ragnarok and the malleability of Asgardian fate; the gods can die and be reborn but the cycle is treated as natural Norse cosmology.
- **Michael Avon Oeming/Daniel Berman & Andrea Di Vito's *Thor: Disassembled / Ragnarok* (2004):** retcons in **Those Who Sit Above in Shadow**, a higher-order pantheon revealed as parasitic puppeteers who *engineered* the Ragnarok cycle to feed on Asgardian rebirth-energy. Thor breaks the cycle by killing them and severing Yggdrasil [T3] Hero and Villain World, "Thor: Disassembled — Ragnarok Now, the Fall of Asgard, and a Cycle Renewed," https://heroandvillainworld.com/thor-disassembled-ragnarok-asgard/ ; [T3] Screen Rant, "Thor Once Saved Asgard from Ragnarok (By Moving to Oklahoma)," https://screenrant.com/thor-asgard-ragnarok-oklahoma-marvel-comics/ . J. Michael Straczynski's 2007 relaunch then brings Asgard physically to Earth, hovering over Broxton, Oklahoma — a structural change (Asgard as Midgard real estate) that lasts roughly through *Siege* (2010) and Aaron's run.
- **Al Ewing's *Immortal Thor* (2023–2025):** reframes Asgard as merely the latest phase of a longer Demiurge cycle, with the previous god-host (the Utgard Elder Gods) returning to challenge Thor's claim. Toranos, the Utgard-Thor, comes for Mjolnir on Gaea's instigation; Thor passes a moral test by giving Toranos "the power to hold the storm back" [T3] Marvel Database, "Toranos (Earth-616)," https://marvel.fandom.com/wiki/Toranos_(Earth-616) . The arc culminates in **Immortal Thor #23–25**, with Thor "alone against the fury of the malevolent Gods of Utgard," Thor's death, and the relaunch as *Mortal Thor* with Thor reborn on Earth as **Sigurd Jarlson**, no memory of his godhood [T3] ComicBook.com, "Marvel's Immortal Thor Comics Get Major 2025 Tease," https://comicbook.com/marvel/news/marvel-immortal-thor-comics-2025-tease-timeslide/ ; [T3] Bleeding Cool, Rich Johnston, "Immortal Thor End With #25, Relaunches Later This Year With Al Ewing," https://bleedingcool.com/comics/immortal-thor-end-with-25-relaunches-later-this-year-with-al-ewing/ ; [T2] marvel.com, "The Story of the Mortal Thor Begins as the Legend of the Immortal Thor Comes to an End," https://www.marvel.com/articles/comics/the-story-of-the-mortal-thor-begins-as-the-legend-of-the-immortal-thor-comes-to-an-end .

A reasonable read of this is that *Immortal Thor* is the third great Asgardian retcon: 1) Simonson naturalising Norse myth, 2) Oeming breaking the cycle, 3) Ewing nesting the cycle inside an even older Elder God war and then ending Thor's Asgardian phase entirely.

## 3. Olympians

**Home dimension:** Olympus, an extradimensional pocket adjacent to Earth, with a nexus atop Mount Olympus in Greece [T3] Wikipedia, "Olympians (Marvel Comics)," https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olympians_(Marvel_Comics) . **Skyfather:** Zeus. **Key members:** Hercules, Ares, Athena, Apollo, Hera, Hermes, Aphrodite, Pluto/Hades, Poseidon, Dionysus.

Introduced by Lee/Kirby in *Journey into Mystery Annual* #1 (1965), where Zeus intervened in a Hercules/Thor brawl [T2] marvel.com, "Get to Know Zeus and the Gods of Olympus," https://www.marvel.com/articles/comics/who-are-zeus-and-gods-of-olympus-in-marvel-comics . A Roy Thomas retcon in *Thor Annual* #5 (1966) establishes a formal **Asgard–Olympus mutual-defence pact** between Odin and Zeus, the first of the inter-pantheon treaties that later become the Council of Godheads [T2] marvel.com, "Get to Know Zeus and the Gods of Olympus," https://www.marvel.com/articles/comics/who-are-zeus-and-gods-of-olympus-in-marvel-comics .

The Olympians have suffered the most catastrophic 21st-century erosion of any pantheon. In Pak/Van Lente's *Chaos War* (2010–2011) the Council Elite's "Celestial Axis" throne room is breached; **Amatsu-Mikaboshi** erupts from the Olympian Skyfather's chest and devastates "the divine realms of all of Earth's pantheons and destroying most of the gods, even the various Godheads themselves" [T3] Wikipedia, "Chaos War," https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chaos_War . Athena dies at Hercules' hands during this arc; many Olympians are restored only later [T2] marvel.com, "Get to Know Zeus and the Gods of Olympus." In Aaron's *Avengers* (2018) Zeus is killed by the Final Host of Dark Celestials and the Olympians fragment; in *Immortal Thor*, Hercules appears as the diminished consort/ally of **Nyx** (a similarly diminished goddess of night), and Thor consults him to learn about "the wheel" Toranos invokes [T3] Nerd Initiative, "The Immortal Thor #8: A Mother's Warning," https://nerdinitiative.com/2024/03/13/the-immortal-thor-8-a-mothers-warning/ .

## 4. Heliopolitans / Ennead (Egyptian Gods)

**Home dimension:** **Celestial Heliopolis** (the "Black Pyramid" / Celestial Heliopolis dimension), with a small palace-world that the gods retreated to after pulling back from mortal affairs. Bordering realms include **Bast's Panther Palace** and **Orun** (the Vodū realm) [T3] MarvUnApp, "Heliopolitans (Egyptian) Gods," http://www.marvunapp.com/Appendix/godsegpt.htm . **Skyfather(s):** Atum-Ra (founder), Osiris (current canonical regent), with Horus as warrior-heir.

**Members of the Ennead** (per official Marvel team page): Osiris, Seth, Thoth, Geb, Nut, Shu, Tefnut, Ammon-Ra, Anubis, Bast, Bes, Horus, Khonshu, Ptah, Neith, Ma'at, Sekhmet, Sobek [T2] marvel.com, "Ennead (Heliopolitans – Egyptian Gods) Members, Enemies, Powers," https://www.marvel.com/teams-and-groups/ennead-heliopolitans .

Two structural notes worth flagging:

1. **Bast** sits on a structural seam: she is canonically a Heliopolitan but functions as the patron deity of Wakanda and is sometimes counted with the Vodū / African pantheons. Christopher Priest's *Black Panther* #21 made Bast and the Panther God explicitly identical, formalising the bridge between the Egyptian and Wakandan religious frames [T3] Wikipedia, "Heliopolitans," https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heliopolitans .
2. **Khonshu** is the Heliopolitan member most active in Earth continuity through *Moon Knight*, especially Jed MacKay's *Vengeance of the Moon Knight* and Jason Aaron's *Avengers* (where Khonshu briefly became the avatar-god of the planet). The MCU's *Moon Knight* (2022) used the **Ennead** as a six-deity "council of avatars" — a smaller, MCU-specific reshuffle of the comics roster [T3] Inverse, Eric Francisco, "Who is Moon Knight's council of gods? Marvel's Ennead, explained," https://www.inverse.com/entertainment/moon-knight-ennead-egyptian-gods-marvel-comics ; [T3] Marvel Cinematic Universe Wiki, "Ennead," https://marvelcinematicuniverse.fandom.com/wiki/Ennead .

The Heliopolitans' recurring narrative role in Thor titles is via **Seth**, the Egyptian death-god, who repeatedly invades Asgard and engineers proxy wars (notably the Asgard-Tuatha conflict via the shedu monster) [T3] Marvel Database, "Leir (Earth-616)," https://marvel.fandom.com/wiki/Leir_(Earth-616) .

## 5. Vodū (Loa) — West African Gods

**Home dimension:** **Orun**, a small pocket dimension adjacent to Earth, accessed via an interdimensional nexus in West Africa [T3] MarvUnApp, "Vodū (African) Gods," http://www.marvunapp.com/Appendix/africg.htm . **Skyfather:** **Buluku** (the all-father, who merged with **Gaea** in her aspects "Nana / Ale / Asase Ya"). Twin offspring: sun-god **Lusa**, moon-goddess **Mahu**. Lusa's children include **Ayaba, Damballah, Eschu, Faa, Gou, Legba, Loco, Maou, Ogun, Sagbata, Shango, Zaca**.

A key structural distinction Marvel draws: the **Loa** are *not* identical to the Vodū but are "lesser elemental spirits who possess more limited, specialized gifts that personify forces of nature," serving the Vodū [T3] MarvUnApp, "Vodū (African) Gods." This two-tier structure (great gods + servant spirits) is unique to the African pantheon in Marvel cosmology and was canonised in Bill Mantlo's *Moon Knight* #6 (Jan 1981) and built out heavily in Brian K. Vaughan's *Doctor Voodoo* and Rick Remender's *Doctor Voodoo: Avenger of the Supernatural* [T3] MightyAvengers.net, "Doctor Voodoo," https://mightyavengers.net/characters/doctor-voodoo/biography . **Buluku** is canonical Council of Godheads representative for the Vodū [T3] Marvel Database, "Buluku (Earth-616)," https://marvel.fandom.com/wiki/Buluku_(Earth-616) .

Bast's Panther Palace bordering Orun is the structural reason the Wakandan religious system can plausibly draw on both Egyptian and West African divine substrates — a point Ta-Nehisi Coates' *Black Panther* run leaned on heavily.

## 6. Daevas (Hindu / Vedic Gods)

**Home dimension:** **Nirvana** (sometimes rendered Svarga / Devaloka in fan references but Marvel's official term is Nirvana) [T3] Marvel Database, "Daevas," https://marvel.fandom.com/wiki/Daevas . **King:** **Indra** (King of Gods); the **Trimurti** of **Brahma**, **Vishnu**, and **Shiva** are the cosmological functionaries (creator/preserver/destroyer). The Trimurti emerged from "Hiranyagarbha the golden womb of Gaea, later known among the Daevas as 'Aditi.'"

Other named Daevas in Marvel continuity: **Ganesh, Kali, Yama, Skanda, Maya, Agni, Surya** [T3] Comic Vine, "Daevas Members," https://comicvine.gamespot.com/daevas/4060-56917/characters/ . Daevas are noted as physically stronger than most other Earth pantheons (often multi-armed); Vishnu has blue skin and four arms, Brahma three faces, Ganesh an elephant head — all preserved per source mythology.

The Daevas' Council of Godheads slot has been variously held by Brahma, Shiva, or Vishnu; in the seminal *Thor* #300 Third-Host meeting they appear as a bloc of three [T3] MarvUnApp, "Council of Godheads," http://www.marvunapp.com/Appendix/godhed.htm (cited via search summary). They are largely passive in modern continuity — neither *Chaos War* nor *Immortal Thor* gives them significant page time, which is itself a structural fact: the Daevas are Marvel's least-damaged major pantheon, which is partially a publishing decision (avoidance of religious sensitivity around active Hindu deities).

## 7. Yazatas (Persian / Zoroastrian)

**Home dimension:** unnamed extradimensional realm; the system is dualistic by structure rather than spatial [T3] MarvUnApp, "Yazatas (Persian/Zoroastrian gods)," http://www.marvunapp.com/Appendix/yazatas.htm . **Skyfather:** **Ahura Mazda**, who emerged from **Zurvan** (personification of infinite time) and is mirrored by his evil counterpart **Ahriman**, who created the **Divs** (demonic anti-gods) to oppose the Yazatas [T3] Marvel Database, "Ahura Mazda (Earth-616)," https://marvel.fandom.com/wiki/Ahura_Mazda_(Earth-616) .

**Members:** Mithra, Atar, Asha, Kshathra Vairya, Haurvatat, Vohu Manah, Ameretat; **Armaiti** is identified with Gaea. A unique structural quirk: "all the powers of the Yazatas are an extension of Ahura Mazda's own power, and if he were incapacitated all of their powers would cease to exist" — making the Yazatas the most centralised pantheon in the Marvel system, in sharp contrast to the federated Olympian/Asgardian setups [T3] Marvel Database, "Yazatas," https://marvel.fandom.com/wiki/Yazatas .

The Yazatas are Council of Godheads members (Atar, Ahura Mazda, and Mithra all have attendance records) but are otherwise minor in modern continuity. The Daevas/Yazatas etymological collision — in Vedic tradition *daeva* are gods, in Zoroastrian tradition *daeva* are demons — is reflected in Marvel by the Yazatas having the *Divs* as their canonical antagonists rather than the Daevas themselves, a tasteful sidestep.

## 8. Annunaki (Mesopotamian / Sumerian / Babylonian)

**Home dimension:** Dilmun (extradimensional realm; not always named in stories) [T3] MarvUnApp, "Annunaki (Mesopotamian Gods)," http://www.marvunapp.com/Appendix/sumerg.htm . **Original skyfather:** **Anu**; replaced by **Marduk** (Anu's grandson, god of judgment & magic) after Marduk led the Annunaki to victory over **Kingu and Tiamat** [T3] Marvel Database, "Marduk (Earth-616)," https://marvel.fandom.com/wiki/Marduk_(Earth-616) ; [T2] marvel.com, "Marduk (Babylonian) Powers, Enemies, History," https://www.marvel.com/characters/marduk-babylonian .

**Members:** Anu, Marduk, Inanna/Ishtar, Ereshkigal, Nergal, Ninlil, Enki, Enlil, Dagon, Aqhat, Papa, Tiamat (defeated/dead), Kingu (defeated/dead). The Annunaki are notable for an early defeat by Asgard: **in 980 A.D., Marduk sought out the Norse gods and battled Thor, who stripped Marduk of his godly heritage and exiled him to Earth** [T3] Marvel Database, "Marduk (Earth-616)." This is one of the few canonical examples of an inter-pantheon territorial war that *resolved decisively* rather than ending in armistice. The Annunaki recover off-panel; both Dagon and Marduk are named Council of Godheads attendees [T3] Marvel Database, "Annunaki (Deities)," https://marvel.fandom.com/wiki/Annunaki_(Deities) .

## 9. Tuatha de Danaan (Celtic Gods)

**Home dimension:** **Tír na nÓg**, a realm within **The Otherworld**, distinct from but contiguous with Asgard, Avalon, and Annwn (the Celtic underworld) [T3] Irish Myths, "A History of the Gods of Irish & Celtic Mythology in the Comics," https://irishmyths.com/2021/04/14/marvel-comics/ . **Skyfather:** **The Dagda (Eochaid Ollathair)**, god of magic. **Key members:** Leir (storm/lightning, warrior-leader), Caber, Lugh, Morrigan, Brigid, Nuada, Manannán mac Lir.

The Tuatha had a long cold war with Asgard over Avalon-Asgard borders; **Seth** (Egyptian) exploited it by unleashing a shedu through both realms, and **Leir** ended up allying with Thor. This established a Tuatha-Asgard détente that has persisted in modern continuity [T3] Marvel Database, "Leir (Earth-616)," https://marvel.fandom.com/wiki/Leir_(Earth-616) ; [T3] MarvUnApp, "Leir," http://www.marvunapp.com/Appendix/leirthor.htm . The Dagda is the Council representative; Tuatha visibility in modern continuity is low, but they appeared in *Chaos War* and were partially decimated alongside the other pantheons.

## 10. Amatsu-Kami (Shinto)

**Home dimension:** **Ama**, a pocket dimension adjacent to Earth; specifically the **Takamagahara** ("Plain of High Heaven") subregion [T3] Marvel Database, "Amatsu-Kami," https://marvel.fandom.com/wiki/Amatsu-Kami ; [T3] MarvUnApp, "Amatsu-Kami (Japanese Gods)," http://www.marvunapp.com/Appendix/godsjapn.htm . **Founders:** **Izanagi** and **Izanami** (the latter identified with Gaea in her Japanese aspect). **Trinity:** **Amaterasu** (sun), **Susanoo** (storm), **Tsukuyomi** (moon).

The Amatsu-Kami are the structurally most consequential pantheon of the 21st century *despite* having the fewest pages, because of one character: **Amatsu-Mikaboshi**, originally the Shinto god of evil/chaos, who was elevated by Pak/Van Lente in *Ares* (2006) and *Incredible Hercules* (2008–2010) into the **Chaos King** — "the living embodiment of the void before time and space began" [T3] Wikipedia, "Chaos War," https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chaos_War . In *Chaos War* (2010–2011), Mikaboshi infiltrates the Council Elite via the Olympian skyfather, ravages every pantheon's home dimension, kills "even the various Godheads themselves," then sheds his Terran form to become a multiversal entity. He's sealed at the end by Amadeus Cho fulfilling Athena's prophecy, but the pantheon structure of the Marvel universe never fully recovers — *Chaos War* is the event that left the divine layer of the Marvel universe in the depopulated state Aaron and Ewing have been working with ever since [T3] Wikipedia, "Amatsu-Mikaboshi (character)," https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amatsu-Mikaboshi_(character) .

## 11. The Council of Godheads / Skyfathers

**Function:** A loose consultative committee of pantheon-leaders convened to handle threats too large for any single pantheon. Also called the **Council Elite** when meeting in their throne room, the **Celestial Axis** [T2] marvel.com, "Council of Godheads," https://www.marvel.com/teams-and-groups/council-of-godheads ; [T3] Wikipedia, "Chaos War."

**Founding event:** Assembled by Odin around 1000 A.D. to coordinate response to the **Celestials' Third Host**. This founding moment is the de facto origin story of the modern Marvel pantheon meta-structure — every god-tier crossover (*Chaos War*, *War of the Realms*, *Immortal Thor*) draws on it as backstory [T3] MarvUnApp, "Council of Godheads," http://www.marvunapp.com/Appendix/godhed.htm .

## 12. The Thor Lens: Ages of Thunder, Aaron, Ewing

The pantheon layer of Marvel cosmology is largely shaped by whoever is currently writing *Thor*. Three runs are structurally load-bearing:

- **Matt Fraction's *Thor: Ages of Thunder* (2008)** — a one-shot trilogy ("Ages of Fire," "Reign of Blood," "Man of War") that re-presents Asgardians as full-on capricious Norse deities prior to modern continuity, foregrounding Thor's arrogance and pre-Donald Blake mythic register [T3] Goodreads, "Thor: Ages of Thunder," https://www.goodreads.com/book/show/6029320-thor ; [T3] CBR, "Hammer Times Past: Fraction & Zircher on 'Thor: Ages of Thunder,'" https://www.cbr.com/hammer-times-past-fraction-zircher-on-thor-ages-of-thunder/ . Structurally minor but tonally crucial: it licenses subsequent writers (Aaron, Ewing) to treat Asgardians as primal forces rather than superheroes-in-helmets.
- **Jason Aaron's *Thor: God of Thunder / Mighty Thor / War of the Realms* (2012–2019)** — introduces **Gorr the God-Butcher** in *God of Thunder* #2 (Jan 2013), an antagonist who across millennia "[goes] from planet to planet, murdering entire pantheons" [T2] marvel.com, "A Look Back at Thor's First Clash with Gorr in 'Thor: God of Thunder,'" https://www.marvel.com/articles/comics/gorr-first-appearance-in-the-comics-thor-god-of-thunder-series-spotlight ; [T3] Wikipedia, "Gorr the God Butcher," https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gorr_the_God_Butcher . The Gorr arc retroactively explains why so many extraterrestrial pantheons (Shi'ar, Zenn-La, Kree, Rigellian) are missing or diminished in the modern Marvel universe — a much cleaner accounting than *Chaos War* alone provides.
- **Al Ewing's *Immortal Thor* (2023–2025)** — the Utgard arc, the Mother Gaea reveal, the Toranos test, Thor's death in #25, and the *Mortal Thor* relaunch with Sigurd Jarlson [T3] Marvel Database, "Immortal Thor Vol 1 23," https://marvel.fandom.com/wiki/Immortal_Thor_Vol_1_23 ; [T2] marvel.com, "The Story of Immortal Thor Comes to an End," https://www.marvel.com/articles/comics/the-story-of-immortal-thor-comes-to-an-end-martin-coccolo-connecting-covers ; [T3] The PullBox, "Marvel Introduces… The Mortal Thor!" https://www.thepullbox.com/2025/07/tmt/ . Ewing's structural innovation is to make *the Demiurge cycle itself* the subject of the run, with Asgardians, Olympians, and Utgard Elder Gods all framed as iterations of the same primordial process.

## 13. Inter-Pantheon Geometry (Quick Reference)

- **Asgard ↔ Olympus**: formal mutual-defence pact since *Thor Annual* #5 (1966).
- **Asgard ↔ Annunaki**: Marduk vs Thor war, 980 A.D., resolved by Marduk's exile.
- **Asgard ↔ Tuatha**: cold war over Avalon, ended by Seth's shedu plot and Leir-Thor alliance.
- **Asgard ↔ Heliopolitans**: chronic conflict via Seth's invasions; ambient peace otherwise.
- **Heliopolitans ↔ Vodū**: contiguous realms (Panther Palace borders Orun); Bast as bridge figure.
- **All pantheons ↔ Amatsu-Kami**: post-*Chaos War* trauma; Mikaboshi as universal antagonist.
- **All pantheons ↔ Utgard Elder Gods**: existential, not political; the Utgard Five claim primacy *over* the entire Council of Godheads structure.
