# Knull / King in Black: The Symbiote Cosmology Retcon

**Topic:** Trace the 2018–21 Cates/Stegman retcon (*Venom* vol. 4 and *King in Black*) that reframed symbiotes as a Celestial-era primordial substance and Knull as a god of the void predating the light. Reconcile with Hickman's First Firmament cosmology, the post-2015 Eighth Cosmos, and prior Klyntar-as-paradise framing. Cover Eddie Brock's elevation to Captain Universe / King in Black successor.

The Knull retcon is one of the most aggressive structural rewrites in 21st-century Marvel cosmology. In roughly three years, Donny Cates and Ryan Stegman took the symbiote — a 1984 Spider-Man costume gimmick — and recast it as a primordial substance older than the current cosmos, weaponised it via a previously unrelated Jason Aaron villain (Gorr), gave Guardians of the Galaxy's Knowhere a new origin, and ended by making Eddie Brock simultaneously a Captain Universe and a god in his own right. The retcon does not actually replace Hickman's First Firmament cosmology — it slots underneath it as a pre-light "abyss" and survives the Seventh-to-Eighth Cosmos transition without comment. This file traces the structural beats.

## 1. The pre-Cates symbiote canon (1984–2017)

The original symbiote origin is a Spider-Man costume change. The black suit appeared concurrently in *The Amazing Spider-Man* #252, *The Spectacular Spider-Man* #90, and *Marvel Team-Up* #141 (May 1984), with its alien nature explained later in *Secret Wars* #8 (Dec 1984) [T3] Wikipedia, "Symbiote (comics)", https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Symbiote_(comics). David Michelinie and Todd McFarlane spun it into Venom in 1988, and Carnage followed in 1992. None of this required a cosmic origin.

The first attempted upgrade came in *Planet of the Symbiotes* (1995, Michelinie / various artists), which framed symbiotes as a hostile invasive species, but stopped short of an explicit cosmogony [T3] Wikipedia, "Symbiote (comics)", https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Symbiote_(comics).

The structurally important pre-Cates retcon is Brian Michael Bendis's *Guardians of the Galaxy* run (2013–2017), which introduced the **Klyntar** as a *species name* and reframed symbiotes as fundamentally benevolent: "the Klyntar were established as a peaceful species of warriors who bonded with various creatures throughout space, though they could be corrupted when bonding with certain creatures" [T3] ComicBook.com, "Marvel Makes Major Retcon to Venom's Origin", https://comicbook.com/marvel/news/marvel-venom-retcon-klyntar-origin/. Bendis's framing established the **Agents of the Cosmos** — symbiote-host pairs serving a hive-mind "voice of the Cosmos" — and treated the planet Klyntar as a paradise homeworld [T3] Marvel Database (Fandom), "Klyntar (Location)", https://marvel.fandom.com/wiki/Klyntar_(Location).

Cates inherited two simultaneous origins — invasive parasite (Michelinie) and benevolent collective (Bendis) — and resolved both with a third.

## 2. The Cates/Stegman retcon: *Venom* vol. 4 (2018)

The retcon is delivered across the opening arc of *Venom* vol. 4 by Donny Cates and Ryan Stegman, launched May 2018 as part of Marvel's "A Fresh Start" initiative [T3] TV Tropes, "Venom (Donny Cates)", https://tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/ComicBook/VenomDonnyCates.

The key issues:

- **Venom (vol. 4) #3 (August 2018)** — Knull's first named full appearance. Cates and Stegman introduce him as the god-of-symbiotes [T2] Wikipedia, "Knull", https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Knull, ¶"Publication history". Marvel's official site frames the cosmology bluntly: "Knull's existence predates the seventh iteration of the Multiverse. The Celestials offered him kingship... Knull rejected this offer and attacked the Celestials for bringing light to his realm of darkness, even beheading one of them with his sword" [T2] Marvel.com, "Meet Knull, the God of Symbiotes" canon entry, https://www.marvel.com/articles/comics/knull-god-symbiotes-klyntar-history-explained.
- **Venom (vol. 4) #4 (July/August 2018)** — Knull narrates his origin to Eddie Brock telepathically. The issue establishes that Knull "plunged his hand into his newly-cast shadow, and pulled out a sword of living darkness — the first symbiote", decapitated a Celestial, and then "used the head to forge All-Black the Necrosword by combining his symbiote sword with extracted cosmic energy from its brain" [T3] ScreenRant, "Why The Venom Symbiotes Were REALLY Created", https://screenrant.com/venom-origin-symbiotes-explained/. Cates confirmed on Twitter that the severed Celestial head is the future Knowhere: "Yep! The severed celestial head in VENOM #4 would someday erode and decay and become Knowhere! Good catch!" [T3] Donny Cates on X, 26 July 2018, https://x.com/doncates/status/1022604840051109888.

The retcon does three structural things at once:

1. **Backdates symbiotes to the cosmogonic prologue.** Symbiotes are no longer a species evolved on a planet; they are a *substance* — Knull's living shadow — older than the current cosmic order.
2. **Pulls Jason Aaron's *Thor: God of Thunder* into the symbiote mythology.** Aaron and Esad Ribic introduced All-Black the Necrosword in *Thor: God of Thunder* #2 (Jan 2013), with its cosmic prehistory shown in *Thor: God of Thunder* #6 (March 2013) where a nameless dark god wields it before Gorr [T3] Wikipedia, "Gorr the God Butcher", https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gorr_the_God_Butcher. Cates retroactively names that nameless figure as Knull: "Knull was first introduced in Venom vol. 4 #3 (Aug 2018), giving a nod to a nameless being appearing in Thor: God of Thunder #6" [T3] Wikipedia, "Knull", https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Knull. The Necrosword is recast as Knull's *firstborn* symbiote — the original All-Black — and Gorr's god-killing weapon becomes the prototype for every subsequent symbiote.
3. **Gives Knowhere a new origin.** The Guardians of the Galaxy's space station, previously the severed head of an unnamed Celestial of unspecified provenance, is now specifically the Celestial Knull beheaded at the dawn of the Seventh Cosmos [T3] CBR, "CONFIRMED: Venom Just Gave GotG's Knowhere an Origin Story", https://www.cbr.com/venom-knowhere-origin-story-guardians-of-the-galaxy/.

## 3. Reconciling the Klyntar paradise: "Klyntar" means "cage"

The Bendis paradise framing isn't deleted — it's recast as a deliberate symbiote lie. Cates's mythology has the symbiote hive rebel against Knull when one of his weaponised dragons (Grendel) is severed from him. Marvel's official summary: the liberated symbiotes "rebelled against their god and trapped him inside an artificial planet formed of billions of symbiotes, which they called Klyntar — 'cage' in their language" [T3] Wikipedia, "Knull", https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Knull.

The Agents of the Cosmos and the "voice of the Cosmos" hive are kept, but reframed: "Ashamed of their dark origins, the symbiotes of Klyntar invented and perpetuated a lie that their species was naturally benevolent, and established a hive mind capable of sensing the 'voice' of the Cosmos and created the Agents of the Cosmos" [T3] ComicBook.com, "Marvel Makes Major Retcon to Venom's Origin", https://comicbook.com/marvel/news/marvel-venom-retcon-klyntar-origin/. This is structurally elegant: every Bendis-era story remains in continuity, but the symbiotes were lying about their nature, and "Klyntar" the homeworld is literally a prison wrapping a chained god.

Grendel — the symbiote dragon Knull remote-piloted — provides the bridge to terrestrial myth. Per Marvel Database, Grendel arrived on Earth in the 6th century CE, was struck down by Thor at Heorot Hall (the *Beowulf* scene), and frozen in a glacier; SHIELD recovered the body in 1965 [T3] Marvel Database (Fandom), "Grendel (Symbiote) (Earth-616)", https://marvel.fandom.com/wiki/Grendel_(Symbiote)_(Earth-616). This connects to Cates's Vietnam-era "Sym-Soldier Program" plotline (Rex Strickland / the Tyrannosaurus symbiote), where US forces were bonded with fragments of the captured Grendel [T3] Marvel Database (Fandom), "Rex Strickland (Earth-616)", https://marvel.fandom.com/wiki/Rex_Strickland_(Earth-616). Beowulf becomes a symbiote story.

## 4. Reconciling with Hickman: where Knull sits in the cosmos cycle

Jonathan Hickman's *Avengers* / *New Avengers* / *Secret Wars* (2012–2015) established that the Marvel multiverse has gone through multiple iterations — the **Cosmos cycle** — beginning with the **First Firmament** (an infinite singularity) and proceeding through Second through Seventh, with Hickman's *Secret Wars* (2015) destroying the Seventh Cosmos and birthing the **Eighth** [T3] Marvel Database (Fandom), "Glossary:Eighth Cosmos", https://marvel.fandom.com/wiki/Glossary:Eighth_Cosmos; [T3] uncannyxmen.net, "Life, the Universe and Everything: A Guide to the Cosmos", https://uncannyxmen.net/continuity/life-the-universe-and-everything-a-guide-to-the-cosmos.

Knull's "void before light" predates *this* cosmos but does not redefine the Cosmos cycle. Marvel's official line is that "Knull's existence predates the seventh iteration of the Multiverse" [T2] Marvel.com, "Meet Knull, the God of Symbiotes", https://www.marvel.com/articles/comics/knull-god-symbiotes-klyntar-history-explained — i.e. he exists in the "Abyss" between the Sixth Cosmos's collapse and the Seventh's creation. Fan-wiki synthesis identifies "Knull's Abyss" as "the existential chasm that was between the destruction of the previous Sixth Cosmos and the creation of the current Seventh/Eighth Cosmos" [T3] Marvel Database (Fandom), "Knull (Earth-616)", https://marvel.fandom.com/wiki/Knull_(Earth-616).

The cleaner cosmological reading places Knull alongside other "proemial darkness" entities (Nyx, Amatsu-Mikaboshi, the Dragon of the Moon) as fragments of the **Anti-All** from the Third Cosmos: "beings of proemial darkness... such as Knull, Nyx, Amatsu-Mikaboshi, or the Dragon of the Moon" plausibly "originated from fragments of the Anti-All" [T3] uncannyxmen.net, "Life, the Universe and Everything: A Guide to the Cosmos", https://uncannyxmen.net/continuity/life-the-universe-and-everything-a-guide-to-the-cosmos. This makes Knull a *Hickman-compatible* primordial: the Lifebringer/Anti-All duality of the Third Cosmos seeds him as a darkness-fragment that survives into the Seventh.

Crucially, the *Secret Wars* (2015) reset to Eighth Cosmos doesn't destroy Knull. He remains imprisoned in Klyntar across the transition; the cosmoses change around him. This is unusual structural carry-over — most cosmic entities Hickman's framework promotes (the Beyonders, the Builders, the Multiversal Eternity) are explicitly tied to a single Cosmos.

## 5. The road to *King in Black*: codices, awakening, and the symbiote dragon army

Cates's run between *Venom* #4 and the *King in Black* event sets up a mechanism for Knull's return. The key concept is the **codex**: every host bonded to a symbiote leaves a residual genetic imprint, and aggregating enough codices can free Knull from Klyntar [T3] CBR, "Absolute Carnage Kicks Off Tomorrow - Here's Your Symbiote Crash Course", https://www.cbr.com/absolute-carnage-what-you-need-to-know-recap/.

- ***Absolute Carnage*** (5 issues, July–Nov 2019, Cates/Stegman) — Cletus Kasady, possessed by Knull, hunts every former symbiote host on Earth to harvest codices. Eddie kills Kasady to save his son Dylan, which (per the trick Knull set) finishes the codex collection. "Knull was finally reawakened after Dark Carnage tricked Eddie into killing him after claiming the remaining codices. Knull promptly destroyed Klyntar, manifesting a suit of draconic armor and coalescing its constituent symbiotes into a fleet of symbiote-dragons" [T3] Wikipedia, "Absolute Carnage", https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Absolute_Carnage.
- ***Venom*** #20–34 and ***Web of Venom*** one-shots (2019–2021) bridge to *King in Black*. *Web of Venom: Empyre's End* connects to the post-Empyre status quo and triggers Knull's invasion fleet [T3] Comic Book Herald, "King in Black Reading Order", https://www.comicbookherald.com/the-complete-marvel-reading-order-guide/king-in-black/.
- Cates's parallel ***Thor*** vol. 6 (2020–2022, with Nic Klein) explicitly threads symbiote/Knull continuity into the Thor mythos via the "God of Hammers" arc, completing the connective tissue back to *Thor: God of Thunder* #6 [T3] ComicsBeat / various, https://bleedingcool.com/comics/donny-cates-and-ryan-stegman-just-discovered-that-knull-is-in-venom-3/.

## 6. *King in Black* (Dec 2020 – April 2021): the event

*King in Black* is a 5-issue main series (Dec 2020 – April 2021) by Cates, Stegman, Frank Martin, and Clayton Cowles, with 30+ tie-ins across Black Cat, Namor, Thunderbolts, Symbiote Spider-Man, Captain America, Immortal Hulk, Savage Avengers, and Return of the Valkyries [T2] Wikipedia, "King in Black", https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King_in_Black; [T3] Comic Book Herald reading order, https://www.comicbookherald.com/the-complete-marvel-reading-order-guide/king-in-black/.

Plot beats:

- *King in Black* #1 — Knull arrives at Earth, surrounds the planet in a symbiote shell that blocks the sun, and immediately rips the Venom symbiote off Eddie Brock. Eddie is left brain-dead.
- *King in Black* #2–3 — The Avengers, X-Men, Fantastic Four, and Thor are routed; vampires, Eternals, and Sentry are deployed. Knull personally kills the Sentry.
- *King in Black* #4 — The Silver Surfer punches a hole in the symbiote shell, allowing the **Enigma Force / Uni-Power** through. The Enigma Force (Captain Universe's animating principle) is reframed as a "God of Light" — the cosmic counterpoint to Knull's God-of-Darkness role. It bonds with Eddie's still-alive consciousness in the symbiote hive and resurrects him as **Captain Universe** [T3] CBR, "King in Black: Marvel's God of Light Gives a Major Hero a Massive Power Boost", https://www.cbr.com/king-in-black-eddie-brock-captain-universe-venom/.
- *King in Black* #5 (April 2021) — Captain Universe Eddie obliterates Knull, "essentially turning him to dust and dissipating his control over all the symbiotes" [T3] GamesRadar+, "Flash Thompson is resurrected and Eddie Brock becomes a god in Venom King in Black finale", https://www.gamesradar.com/king-in-black-finale-sets-a-new-course-for-eddie-brock-and-venom-while-another-host-returns-spoilers/. The Enigma Force departs; the symbiote hive recognises Eddie as its new god. Eddie ascends, declaring himself the new **King in Black**.

The structural payoff: Eddie is no longer a host. He *is* the symbiote-god — Knull's successor. The Venom symbiote bonds to his son Dylan (as the new street-level Venom), while Eddie operates on a cosmological tier.

## 7. Aftermath: Eddie's reign, *Venom War*, and the Queen in Black

The post-*King in Black* status quo is sustained across Al Ewing and Ram V's *Venom* vol. 5 (2021–2024) and Torunn Grønbekk's *Venom* vol. 6, with Eddie operating as a time-displaced symbiote-god ruling from a future "Venomworld" while Dylan is the present-day Venom [T3] TV Tropes, "Venom (Donny Cates)", https://tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/ComicBook/VenomDonnyCates.

***Venom War*** (2024, Al Ewing / Iban Coello) frames a father-son schism: "the reigning King in Black Eddie Brock and his son Dylan (the current host of Venom)... engage in a war to determine who will be the one true Venom" [T3] Wikipedia, "Venom War", https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Venom_War. The reveal is that an evil future-Eddie ("Meridius") has been manipulating events. The event ends with Eddie no longer King in Black [T3] ComicBook.com, "Venom Is No Longer King in Black in Finale of Marvel's Venom War", https://comicbook.com/marvel/news/venom-king-in-black-no-more-venom-war-marvel-comics-preview/.

Knull, meanwhile, is resurrected post–*Venom War* but weakened, and is captured by Hela, who becomes the **Queen in Black** [T2] Wikipedia, "Knull", https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Knull. A *Queen in Black* event is scheduled for 2026 [T2] Wikipedia, "King in Black", https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King_in_Black. The structural template Cates established — symbiote-god as heritable cosmic title — remains the operating model.

## 8. Structural assessment

What the Cates retcon actually changed in the Marvel structural map:

| Layer | Pre-2018 | Post-2018 |
|---|---|---|
| Symbiote nature | Alien parasite species (Michelinie) / benevolent collective (Bendis) | Living substance of a primordial god, predating the current cosmos |
| Klyntar | Symbiote homeworld + Agents-of-the-Cosmos paradise | Prison-planet ("cage") wrapping the chained Knull; benevolence is a cover story |
| All-Black Necrosword | Cosmic god-killing weapon of unknown origin | Knull's *firstborn* symbiote |
| Knowhere | Severed Celestial head, no specified killer | The Celestial Knull beheaded at the dawn of time |
| Captain Universe | Generic Uni-Power host-of-the-week | The "God of Light," structural counterpart to Knull |
| Cosmos cycle | Hickman's First Firmament → ... → Eighth | Unchanged at the top level; Knull slotted in as a Sixth-to-Seventh "Abyss" entity, possibly Anti-All-derived |

The retcon is *additive* rather than destructive. Hickman's cosmology is left intact; Bendis's Klyntar is left in continuity but reinterpreted; Aaron's Necrosword keeps every line of dialogue and gains a referent for "the dark god." Bendis-era and Aaron-era stories don't need editing — only re-reading. That's why the retcon stuck. It also gave the symbiote line a single coherent vertical axis (god → first symbiote → Necrosword → dragons → modern symbiotes → codices) where before it had only horizontal continuity patches.

The cost is theological inflation: post–*King in Black*, the symbiote lore now operates at the same scale as Galactus, the Phoenix, and the Living Tribunal. Eddie Brock is not a street-level Spider-Man villain anymore. Whether that's a feature or a bug is a question for the next decade of editors — and for the *Queen in Black* event Marvel has on the schedule for 2026.
