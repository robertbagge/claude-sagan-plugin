# Marvel Multiverse Structure & Cosmology

A structural map of how Marvel's published cosmology has been organised, retconned, and replaced — from the Counter-Earth conceit of the early 1970s through Hickman's Eighth Cosmos and the MCU's "Multiverse Saga" Battleworld endgame.

## 1. Hierarchy: Universe → Multiverse → Megaverse → Omniverse

Marvel's contemporary in-universe taxonomy is layered. Wikipedia's structural overview, drawn from the *Official Handbook of the Marvel Universe* (OHOTMU) entries, distinguishes five tiers: a **universe** is a single dimension (e.g. Earth-616); a **reality** bundles a universe with its associated dimensions (Asgard, Dark Dimension, Negative Zone); a **multiverse** is the collection of alternate universes that share a common nature and cosmic hierarchy; a **megaverse** is the collection of multiverses that need not share natures; and the **omniverse** is everything — including fan fictions, unpublished works, and rival publishers' realities [T2] *Multiverse (Marvel Comics)*, Wikipedia, "Definitions" section.

The Marvel Database (fan-maintained but anchored to OHOTMU entries) is explicit that "Megaverse" is a relatively recent terminological insertion: it "hasn't appeared outside of the Handbooks" and was introduced by the 21st-century edition of OHOTMU as a name for a grouping larger than the Multiverse but smaller than the Omniverse, with the explicit caveat that the definition is "fluid" because future stories may dissolve the boundary [T3] Marvel Database, "Omniverse" entry, https://marvel.fandom.com/wiki/Omniverse.

Practically, working writers conflate Multiverse and Omniverse routinely. Al Ewing — one of the principal architects of late-2010s/2020s Marvel cosmology — has openly conceded the slippage [T3] Marvel Database, "Omniverse" entry, https://marvel.fandom.com/wiki/Omniverse. The hierarchy is therefore best read as a *handbook* taxonomy that in-story usage pressures into looser shape.

## 2. The Earth-XXXXX Designation System

The numeric reality system was not a publisher decree — it was a Marvel UK accident that calcified into canon. Dave Thorpe, then writer of *Captain Britain* (Vol. 2), invented "Earth-616" while building the "Crooked World" of the Jaspers' Warp arc. Thorpe explained in a 2019 interview that he subtracted 50 ("a nice round number") from 666, the Number of the Beast, because a direct 666 reference would have been too obvious [T2] *Earth-616*, Wikipedia, "Origin of the designation" section. The label first appeared in print in *The Daredevils* #7 (Marvel UK, July 1983), in the story "Rough Justice" credited to Alan Moore and Alan Davis [T2] *Earth-616*, Wikipedia.

When Moore took over from Thorpe, he reassigned the digits: the Crooked World became Earth-238, and 616 was repurposed as the home reality of Brian Braddock — i.e. the mainstream Marvel continuity. From there, Mark Gruenwald — the editorial steward of OHOTMU — extended the convention into a system, retroactively numbering alternate realities so that a stable shorthand could be used in handbooks and crossovers [T2] *Multiverse (Marvel Comics)*, Wikipedia.

The numeric grammar that emerged is partially logical, partially playful, and partially arbitrary:
- **Earth-616** — the prime comic continuity since 1961 [T3] Marvel.com, "This Week in Marvel Universes: Earth-616," https://www.marvel.com/articles/comics/this-week-in-marvel-universes-earth-616.
- **Earth-1610** — the Ultimate Universe (2000–2015), digits rearranged from 616 with an added zero to mark its derivative-but-distinct status [T2] *Multiverse (Marvel Comics)*, Wikipedia.
- **Earth-199999** — the Marvel Cinematic Universe, formally designated in *Official Handbook of the Marvel Universe A to Z* #5 [T3] Marvel Database, "Earth-199999," https://marvel.fandom.com/wiki/Earth-199999.
- **Earth-65** — Spider-Gwen's home, numbered for 1965 (Gwen Stacy's first appearance, *Amazing Spider-Man* #31) [T3] Marvel Database, "Earth-65," https://marvel.fandom.com/wiki/Earth-65.
- **Earth-928** — the original *2099* line; later harmonised with Earth-2099 as a future timeline of 616 [T3] Marvel Database, "Earth-2099," https://marvel.fandom.com/wiki/Earth-2099.
- **Earth-001** — home of the Web of Life and Destiny and the Master Weaver, the structural hub of the Spider-Verse [T3] Marvel Database, "Web of Life and Destiny," https://marvel.fandom.com/wiki/Web_of_Life_and_Destiny.

A key 2022 retcon: in *Doctor Strange in the Multiverse of Madness*, Christine Palmer of Earth-838 tells Strange that the Illuminati call his reality "Earth-616," realigning the MCU with comics-canonical numbering and effectively demoting the older Earth-199999 designation [T2] *Earth-616*, Wikipedia, "MCU's Reframing." Marvel Studios' *The Marvel Cinematic Universe — An Official Timeline* subsequently uses "Universe-616" for the MCU [T3] Marvel Database, "Earth-199999."

## 3. Planes of Reality: Mortal, Divine, Abstract

A single Marvel "reality" is not flat. Each universe is layered: the mortal/material plane (Earth, the cosmos, alien civilisations), the divine plane (pantheons and "godly" dimensions like Asgard, Olympus, the Heliopolitans, the Sky-Father pantheons), and the abstract plane occupied by personifications of universal concepts.

The abstracts — Eternity, Infinity, Death, Master Order, Lord Chaos, and the Living Tribunal — are conceptual cosmic beings without fixed physical form. Per the Marvel Database, they are "born" with the Big Bang of each cosmos, and the One-Above-All is their creator [T3] Marvel Database, "Abstract Entities," https://marvel.fandom.com/wiki/Abstract_Entities.

Above the abstracts sits a two-tier multiversal authority:
- **The Living Tribunal** — three-faced personification of multiversal law, representing equity, vengeance, and necessity. Operates as the highest practical authority within the Multiverse, acting on behalf of its superior [T2] Marvel.com, "Meet the Living Tribunal, the Multiversal Arbiter of Cosmic Balance," https://www.marvel.com/articles/comics/living-tribunal-history-powers-explained.
- **The One-Above-All** — described as "the highest creative force in known existence and the source of all there is, was, and ever will be," more powerful than all its creations combined [T3] Marvel Database, "One Above All (Multiverse)," https://marvel.fandom.com/wiki/One_Above_All_(Multiverse).

Layered around and outside the universe proper: the **Superflow** (the conceptual space between universes, navigated by the Builders); **Exo-Space** (the border realm just outside Eternity's field of existence); and the **House of Ideas / Heaven** within "the Mystery," where the One-Above-All resides [T3] Marvel Database, "Glossary:Cosmic Beings," https://marvel.fandom.com/wiki/Glossary:Cosmic_Beings.

## 4. Key Dimensional Layers

Beyond the principal Earth and its outer space, every reality has parallel and pocket dimensions tightly bound to it. The structurally important ones recur across decades of stories:

- **The Negative Zone** — an antimatter universe in contraction rather than expansion, parallel to but distinct from 616's space-time. Reachable through dimensional rifts (Reed Richards' portal, the Distortion Area surrounding it). Functions as a cosmic prison and threshold realm [T3] Top-Strongest Wikia, "Marvel Cosmology," https://topstrongest.fandom.com/wiki/Marvel_Cosmology.
- **The Microverse** — explicitly "a collection of parallel dimensions rather than universes that exist within universes," accessible through vibrational attunement (i.e. shrinking via Pym Particles or related mechanisms). The Microverse is the structural ancestor of the MCU's Quantum Realm [T3] Top-Strongest Wikia, "Marvel Cosmology"; [T3] *Features of the Marvel Universe*, Wikipedia.
- **The Mojoverse** — a singular dimension ruled by Mojo, populated by the Spineless Ones and addicted to gladiator-television. Notably described as "completely one of a kind, with no parallel or alternate reality counterparts," which makes it a structural outlier — most dimensions exist in multiversal multiplicity, but the Mojoverse is asserted to be a single instance [T3] Top-Strongest Wikia, "Marvel Cosmology."
- **The Astral Plane** — a dimension occupying equivalent space to the material world but composed of "ectoplasm," where consciousnesses and life energies become discernible. The principal terrain for psychic combat (X-Men telepaths) and mystic out-of-body operations (Doctor Strange) [T3] Top-Strongest Wikia, "Marvel Cosmology."
- **The Dark Dimension, the Mirror Dimension, the Quantum Realm** — the Doctor Strange and Ant-Man-adjacent dimensional infrastructure, especially elevated to load-bearing cosmology in the MCU [T3] ScreenRant, "7 Marvel Dimensions The MCU Can Explore After The Quantum Realm," https://screenrant.com/marvel-alternate-dimensions-mcu-explore-after-quantum-realm/.

The Spider-Verse adds a structural overlay across realities: the **Web of Life and Destiny**, "a three-dimensional construct in five-dimensional space which acts as a model of the entire Multiverse and enables travel between realities," located on Earth-001 and maintained by the Master Weaver. It binds Spider-Totems across all realities into a single connective lattice [T3] Marvel Database, "Web of Life and Destiny," https://marvel.fandom.com/wiki/Web_of_Life_and_Destiny.

## 5. Historical Evolution: From Counter-Earth to a Numbered Multiverse

### 5.1 Counter-Earth (1972) — pre-multiverse parallel reality

Before "Multiverse" was Marvel's organising frame, the company experimented with single-instance parallel realities. Counter-Earth, introduced in *Marvel Premiere* #1 (1972), was created by the High Evolutionary using (at minimum) some of the Infinity Gems as part of "Project Alpha" [T3] Marvel.com, "Welcome to Counter-Earth, the High Evolutionary's Twisted World," https://www.marvel.com/articles/comics/counter-earth-high-evolutionary-world-explained. Critically, Counter-Earth orbits the same sun as the prime Earth but on the opposite side, hidden by being placed "a micro-second out of dimensional sync" [T3] Marvel.com, "Counter-Earth." This is a *pre-multiverse* solution: rather than a separate numbered reality, it's a hidden extra Earth tucked into the same universe.

### 5.2 Captain Britain Corps & the formalised Multiverse (early 1980s)

Thorpe and then Moore's *Captain Britain* run is where Marvel's multiverse becomes a *system*. Earth-616 enters print in 1983; Moore expands the Captain Britain Corps as one Captain Britain per parallel reality, requiring stable nomenclature [T2] *Earth-616*, Wikipedia. From there, Mark Gruenwald systematises the numbering through OHOTMU.

### 5.3 Secret Wars I (1984) — the Beyonder and Battleworld v1

*Marvel Super Heroes Secret Wars* (1984) introduces the Beyonder, who creates a planet "Battleworld" out of fragments of various worlds and abducts Earth heroes and villains to fight on it [T3] Wikipedia, "Beyonder"; [T2] Marvel.com, "Secret Wars and Battleworld Explained," https://www.marvel.com/articles/comics/secret-wars-and-battleworld-explained. Cosmologically, the Beyonder is initially framed as the most powerful being in the multiverse, "with power millions of times greater than the entire multiverse combined" [T3] Wikipedia, "Beyonder."

This framing held for roughly thirty years — until Hickman retconned it.

### 5.4 Heroes Reborn (1996) — pocket-universe as editorial reset

After *Onslaught* (1996), Marvel exiled the Avengers and Fantastic Four to a separate pocket universe, in-story attributed to Franklin Richards' reality-warping powers. Out-of-story it was an editorial outsourcing experiment: Jim Lee's WildStorm produced *Fantastic Four* and *Iron Man*; Rob Liefeld's Extreme Studios produced *Captain America* and *Avengers* [T2] Wikipedia, "Heroes Reborn (1996 comic)." After one year, the pocket universe was collapsed and the heroes returned to 616 via "Heroes Return," again with Franklin's intervention; the returning Tony Stark uniquely retained dual memories from both versions of himself [T2] Wikipedia, "Heroes Reborn (1996 comic)." This event installed *Franklin Richards* as a recurring narrative engine for pocket-universe fixes.

### 5.5 Hickman's Incursions and Secret Wars II (2015) — full multiverse collapse

Jonathan Hickman's *Avengers* / *New Avengers* run (2012–2015) introduced **incursions**: collisions between two parallel Earths that destroy both their universes if not stopped. The first incursion is discovered by Black Panther [T2] Wikipedia, "Secret Wars (2015 comic book)"; [T3] Nerdist, "What Are 'Multiverse Incursions' in Marvel Comics Lore?" https://nerdist.com/article/incursions-explained-marvel-comics-doctor-strange-multiverse-of-madness/.

The cause is retconned upward: an entire race of **Beyonders** — not a single one — are revealed as the multiverse's cosmic custodians, and the original *Secret Wars* Beyonder is recast as a vastly less significant offshoot [T3] Wikipedia, "Beyonder"; [T3] Comic Book Club, "6 Ways The Beyonders Influenced The Marvel Universe."

The 2015 *Secret Wars* climaxes with Earth-616 and Earth-1610 (the Ultimate Universe) as the final two surviving realities; their incursion destroys both. Doctor Doom, with Doctor Strange and Molecule Man, confronts the Beyonders and forges **Battleworld** v2 — a patchwork micro-reality stitched from salvaged fragments of dead realities, each ruled as a domain under God Emperor Doom [T2] Wikipedia, "Secret Wars (2015 comic book)"; [T3] Marvel Database, "Secret Wars (2015 Event)." Reed Richards eventually wrests reality-warping power from Doom, restores 616, and recreates the multiverse.

### 5.6 Hickman cosmology: Seventh and Eighth Cosmos

Hickman's framework retconned the entire history of existence into a sequence of **Cosmoses**. The Seventh Cosmos was the original Marvel multiverse from 1938–2015, embodied uniquely by *two* personifications (Eternity and Infinity) rather than one. After the Beyonder-driven collapse, a salvaged Battleworld served as the interim. The Eighth Cosmos is the post-2015 multiverse — and is structurally distinctive because, as a *continuation* rather than a fresh start, it inherits memory from the Seventh, with Eternity returning as sole embodiment while Infinity departs to the Far Shore [T3] Marvel Database, "Glossary:Eighth Cosmos," https://marvel.fandom.com/wiki/Glossary:Eighth_Cosmos; [T3] ScreenRant, "Marvel's 8 Cosmos, the Gods Who Created Galactus and the Origin of the Multiverse," https://screenrant.com/marvel-multiverse-8-cosmos-gods-galactus-explained/.

### 5.7 All-New All-Different (late 2015) — soft reboot

Post-*Secret Wars*, Marvel relaunched the line as **All-New All-Different Marvel** — explicitly framed as a "fresh jumping-on point" without a hard continuity reboot [T3] Marvel Database, "Secret Wars (2015 Event)." The structural change: Earth-1610's Miles Morales and a handful of other Ultimate-line characters are folded into 616, removing the wall between the two principal continuities. This is a deliberately *softer* approach than DC's Crisis-style reboots — 616 history is preserved but augmented.

## 6. The Current Multiverse Saga (MCU, 2021– ) and Incursion Model

The MCU adopted Hickman's incursion vocabulary wholesale for its *Multiverse Saga* (Phases 4–6). *Loki* (2021) introduced the Sacred Timeline framework and "He Who Remains"; *Doctor Strange in the Multiverse of Madness* (2022) imported "incursion" as the canonical name for two-universe collisions; and Phase 5 (*Quantumania*, *The Marvels*, *Loki* S2) used Kang variants as the originally planned multiversal antagonist [T3] Variety, "Marvel Studios' Phases 5 and 6: Everything We Learned at Comic-Con About the Multiverse Saga," https://variety.com/2022/film/news/marvel-studios-phase-5-phase-6-multiverse-saga-comic-con-1235323893/amp; [T3] ScreenRant, "How The MCU Has Set Up Doctor Doom As The Main Villain Since Phase 4," https://screenrant.com/mcu-doctor-doom-villain-setup-kang-replacement-explained/.

Following the cancellation of Jonathan Majors, Marvel pivoted: Kang is sidelined and Doctor Doom takes the role of central antagonist for *Avengers: Doomsday* and *Avengers: Secret Wars*, with Robert Downey Jr. cast in the part. The publicly previewed endgame is structurally a remix of Hickman: Doom collapses the MCU multiverse into a single Battleworld stitched from fragments of destroyed realities, after which the saga concludes [T3] Popverse, "Marvel Studios swapping out Doctor Doom for Kang offers the chance to jettison the Multiverse Saga," https://www.thepopverse.com/movies-marvel-multiverse-saga-doctor-doom-kang-mcu-marvel-matters; [T3] The Geek Twins, "The Ultimate Guide to Marvel's Phase 6," https://thegeektwins.com/2025/11/the-ultimate-guide-to-marvels-phase-6.html.

Kevin Feige has explicitly framed the conclusion as a "reset, not a reboot," echoing the All-New All-Different soft-reboot logic of 2015 [T3] ComicBook.com, "Marvel's Kevin Feige Says Avengers: Secret Wars Will 'Reset,' Not Reboot, the MCU," https://comicbook.com/movies/news/marvel-avengers-secret-wars-reset-mcu-reboot-kevin-feige/. The Spider-Verse films work in parallel: Earth-1610 is the home of the *Into/Across the Spider-Verse* Miles Morales, and *Across the Spider-Verse* (2023) treats Earth-616 as the live-action MCU's reality on monitor screens, harmonising film canon with the Doctor Strange 2 retcon [T3] Marvel Database, "Earth-1610," https://marvel.fandom.com/wiki/Earth-1610.

## 7. Structural Pattern: Three Generations of Multiverse Logic

Read across sixty years, three structural eras emerge:

1. **Pre-system parallels (1961–1982).** Single-instance "parallel" Earths (Counter-Earth) and unstable "What If" framings. No numeric grammar. Cosmology is theological/abstract (Eternity, Galactus, Death) but not multiversal in any systematic sense.

2. **Numbered Multiverse (1983–2014).** Earth-616 enters print; Gruenwald formalises numbering in OHOTMU; the Beyonder, Captain Britain Corps, and Exiles legitimise multiversal travel as a recurring story engine. The "Megaverse" tier is added in the 21st-century OHOTMU as a hedge for cross-publisher and beyond-multiverse stories [T3] Marvel Database, "Omniverse."

3. **Cosmos cycles + Incursion model (2015–present).** Hickman's Cosmos sequence retroactively makes the multiverse one phase in a longer cosmic cycle; incursions become the canonical destruction mechanism; Battleworld becomes the canonical reset chamber. The MCU adopts this entire stack for its 2021–2026 saga, with Doom rather than Kang as the eventual architect.

The throughline is editorial: every major restructuring of the cosmology has doubled as a soft reboot tool. Heroes Reborn used a pocket universe to outsource four titles; *Secret Wars* (2015) used incursions to merge Ultimate into 616; the MCU's *Avengers: Secret Wars* is publicly framed as a "reset" using the same vocabulary. Marvel's multiverse, structurally, is as much a continuity-management apparatus as a fictional cosmology.
