# Earth-616 Geography: Sovereign Nations, Hidden Civilizations, and Altered Geography

A historical-structural map of Marvel's primary continuity (Earth-616) treats the globe as a palimpsest: every "real" continent has had fictional nations layered over it, hidden civilizations tucked beneath it, and entire landmasses periodically destroyed, relocated, or terraformed onto neighboring planets. This file inventories the structurally important locations, their physical placement, sovereignty, and the major arcs that have moved them.

The Marvel Earth is explicitly defined as "Earth-616 ... a reality that has all the features of our reality including the same countries, personalities, and historical events," with its fictional polities and hidden civilizations layered onto an otherwise real geography [T3] Marvel Database, "Earth" entry, https://marvel.fandom.com/wiki/Earth.

## 1. European Fictional States: The Doom Belt and Its Neighbors

A cluster of small fictional states sits across Eastern and Central Europe, anchored by Latveria.

**Latveria** is "officially the Kingdom of Latveria, a small country within Eastern Europe with Doomstadt as its capital and largest city," and it is "depicted as a small, isolated European country supposedly located in the Banat region, ... surrounded by the Carpathian Mountains and borders fictional Symkaria to the south" [T2] Wikipedia (drawing on Marvel Comics Atlas and OHOTMU), "Latveria," https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latveria. Sovereignty is concentrated in Victor von Doom, who calls his regime an "enforced monarchy"; the state "is consistently depicted as a global superpower on-par with or even surpassing any nation on Earth, including the United States, and rivalled only by the likes of Wakanda" [T2] Wikipedia, "Latveria". Structurally, Latveria's role across arcs has been the standing antagonist polity for both the Fantastic Four and the wider Avengers (Secret Wars, Emperor Doom, Doomwar, the 2099 Doom presidency). Doom's repeated coups, exiles, and returns make Latveria one of the most-cycled regimes in the Marvel map.

**Symkaria** sits directly south of Latveria, "officially Symkrija Kiralysog (the Kingdom of Symkaria), a small eastern European country within the Balkans. Its capital and largest city is Aniana," and it "has maintained close ties to the nation of Latveria" [T3] Marvel Database, "Symkaria," https://marvel.fandom.com/wiki/Symkaria. Symkaria's structural role is as the home and operating base of Silver Sable's mercenary outfit (Wild Pack / Silver Sable International), and as a recurring buffer/foil against Latverian expansion.

**Sokovia** is the third leg of the Eastern European trio. In MCU canon (Earth-199999) "Sokovia was a former landlocked country in Central-Southeastern Europe, bordered by Slovakia and the Czech Republic. Its capital city was Novi Grad. In 2015, its capital city was destroyed by Ultron, ... Sokovia then ceased to exist as a nation, with its territory absorbed by its bordering countries" [T3] Marvel Cinematic Universe Wiki, "Sokovia," https://marvelcinematicuniverse.fandom.com/wiki/Sokovia. In 616, Sokovia is a comparatively minor entity; its structural import is mostly downstream of the MCU's Age of Ultron reverberations.

**Transia** rounds out the Balkan cluster, "a country in the Balkans and the birthplace of Spider-Woman, Quicksilver, and the Scarlet Witch," and the home of Mount Wundagore [T3] Screen Rant, "Most Important Fictional Marvel Comics Countries," https://screenrant.com/most-important-fictional-marvel-comics-countries/.

**Carbombya** is sometimes confused with the Marvel set but actually originates in *The Transformers*: "Carbombya is a fictional state from The Transformers, with the full title 'Socialist Democratic Federated Republic of Carbombya,' [serving] as a stand-in for ... Libya" [T3] TFWiki, "Socialist Democratic Federated Republic of Carbombya," https://tfwiki.net/wiki/Socialist_Democratic_Federated_Republic_of_Carbombya. It is excluded from Earth-616 canon for that reason.

## 2. African Fictional Nations: Wakanda and Genosha

**Wakanda** is the structural anchor of the Marvel African map. Its capital is "Birnin Zana ... a highly urbanized city with the Wakandan Maglev Train being used for transport, and where the Golden Tribe's royal palace, the Citadel, rested" [T3] Marvel Database, "Birnin Zana," https://marvel.fandom.com/wiki/Birnin_Zana. The state has been deliberately isolationist for most of its history: "Since its early tribal beginnings, Wakanda has been extremely isolationist. They did not invade or provoke any other nations, but if another entered their territory they would retaliate with deadly force. They remained this way for thousands of years, until the modern era when they decided to join the global community" [T3] Marvel Database, "Wakanda," https://marvel.fandom.com/wiki/Wakanda.

Wakanda's monopoly on Vibranium (the Great Mound is the only deposit on Earth, with Antarctic Vibranium in the Savage Land as a separate, weaker isotope) has driven major arcs: *Doomwar* (2010, Doom seizes the Vibranium reserve), the Atlantean flooding of Wakanda during *Avengers vs. X-Men*, and the Necropolis storyline. The Necropolis is "the Wakandan City of the Dead, where previous Black Panthers are buried"; T'Challa "was recently made King of the Dead by Bast, granting him the strength, knowledge and experience of every Black Panther that ever lived" [T3] Marvel Database, "Necropolis," https://marvel.fandom.com/wiki/Necropolis. The official *Black Panther: Wakanda Atlas* (2022) standardized the city map (Birnin Zana, Birnin T'Chaka, Birnin Azzaria, Birnin S'Yan, Necropolis) [T2] Marvel.com, "Definitive Guide to Wakanda," https://www.marvel.com/articles/culture-lifestyle/definitive-guide-to-wakanda-marvel-black-panther-atlas-evan-narcisse-interview.

**Genosha** is the African tragedy node. It is "a small crescent-shaped island located to the northeast of Madagascar, on the eastern coast of Africa in the Indian Ocean" [T3] Marvel Database, "Genosha," https://marvel.fandom.com/wiki/Genosha. Across continuity Genosha shifts roles three times: first an apartheid state weaponizing depowered "mutates" (Claremont/Silvestri, 1988); then briefly a mutant homeland under Magneto post-*Eve of Destruction*; then the site of the Genoshan Massacre, where "the Genoshan Massacre resulted in the death of 16 million people that was half the world's Mutant population" via Cassandra Nova's wild Sentinels in *New X-Men* (2001) [T3] Marvel Database, "Genosha". After M-Day (2005) it became a memorial site, with "a monument to Magneto out of the remains of the Mega-Sentinel that had slaughtered the Genoshan population" [T3] Marvel Database, "Genosha". By the Krakoan Age, Genosha was largely supplanted as the mutant geographic center.

## 3. The Mutant Nations: Krakoa, Arakko, and the Move to Mars

The 2019–2024 Krakoan Age is the largest geographic retcon of the modern era and merits its own section.

**Krakoa** itself was originally introduced as a sentient living mutant island antagonist in *Giant-Size X-Men #1* (1975); Hickman's *House of X / Powers of X* (2019) recast it as the sovereign mutant homeland. The Krakoan Age established Krakoa as a sentient living-island nation with diplomatic recognition, gateway-portal infrastructure, the Quiet Council, and resurrection economy [T2] Wikipedia, "Krakoan Age," https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Krakoan_Age.

**Arakko** is Krakoa's twin. "Arakko was a twin sister entity of Krakoa, both originally part of Okkara, a sentient island that existed in the South Pacific. Several thousand years prior to the Modern Age, Okkara was attacked by the demons of Amenth, and the demonic god Annihilation split Okkara into two halves; Krakoa and Arakko" [T3] Marvel Database, "Arakko (Earth-616)," https://marvel.fandom.com/wiki/Arakko_(Earth-616). After the *X of Swords* (2020) crossover, Arakko's mutants returned to Earth, and *Planet-Size X-Men #1* (2021) had them terraform Mars. The terraforming was specific: "Magneto drew in a massive iron asteroid from space, which Vulcan and Hope Summers used to create the molten core of Mars. ... Proteus primed the planet for life, and Arakko's Sobunar of the Depths unleashed the oceanic ecosystem within his body to fill the world with water and sea life. ... Storm used her powers to bring these elements together and give the planet a new climate" [T2] Marvel.com, "A History of Mutants on Mars," https://www.marvel.com/articles/comics/x-men-mutants-mars-arakko-explained. The result: "They rechristen Mars as Planet Arakko, the first mutant planet, and declare that it is the capital of the Solar System" [T2] Marvel.com, "A History of Mutants on Mars". Governance was "the Great Ring of Arakko, a 12-member council mostly made up of Omega-level mutants, including Storm and Magneto. ... divided into four sections of three members: Dawn, Day, Dusk, and Night" [T2] Marvel.com, "A History of Mutants on Mars".

**Fall of X (2023–2024)** dismantles the geographic settlement. "The Fall of X ran from August 2023 to June 2024 as the final phase of the Krakoan Age, and X-Men #35 (June 2024), legacy Uncanny X-Men #700, marked the official end of the Krakoan Age" [T3] ComicBook.com, "Marvel Reveals the Final Fate of Krakoa," https://comicbook.com/comics/news/marvel-x-men-krakoa-ending-reboot-explained-comic-final-issue/. The mechanism: "During the third Hellfire Gala (July 2023), Krakoa was attacked by Orchis; however, Mother Righteous had transported Atlantic Krakoa into the White Hot Room, which allowed the surviving mutants to rebuild. ... In X-Men #35, the two halves of Krakoa (Pacific and Atlantic) are rejoined ... Krakoa merges with its other half once again to return to the White Hot Room where its lifespan and vitality will be greatly extended" [T3] ComicBook.com, "Marvel Reveals the Final Fate of Krakoa". As of the From the Ashes era (2024–), Krakoa is no longer a physical sovereign nation on Earth; Arakko/Mars persists, but the diasporic mutant population is back on Earth without a recognized homeland.

## 4. Underwater Civilizations: Atlantis and Lemuria

The undersea map has two structurally important civilizations, both descending from a pre-Cataclysmic surface continent.

**Atlantis** in 616 was originally "a small continent (about the size of present-day Australia), located in the Atlantic Ocean between North America and Europe, which sank beneath the sea approximately 20,000 years ago in the Great Cataclysm. The modern generation of Atlanteans built a new Atlantis that exists roughly 9,000 feet below sea level" [T3] Marvel Database, "Atlantis," https://marvel.fandom.com/wiki/Atlantis. The current population is *Homo mermanus*, a water-breathing race that "colonizes the ruins around 6000 BC under the rule of Kamuu and Zartra" [T2] Marvel.com, "The History of Atlantis," https://www.marvel.com/articles/comics/history-of-atlantis. Sovereignty has long centered on Namor McKenzie ("Imperius Rex"), with the throne contested across arcs (Attuma, Byrrah, Krang). Capital relocations include "Antarctic (a city named for Emperor Thakorr), ... North Atlantic; other Atlantean colonies ... Deluvia (now abandoned), Maritanis (destroyed in atomic tests), New Pangea, and Sharka (near Labrador, Canada)" [T3] Marvel Database, "Atlantis". Atlantis has been destroyed multiple times — most notably during *Avengers vs. X-Men* and again during *Secret Wars* (2015) — and rebuilt.

**Lemuria** is the sister sunken continent. "The Lemurians were of an Atlantean sect worshiping the Elder God Set, that left Atlantis, and founded the kingdom of Lemuria. ... a continent ruled by Deviants which sank under the ocean around the time as Atlantis during the Great Cataclysm" [T3] Marvel Database, "Atlantis (Homo mermanus)," https://marvel.fandom.com/wiki/Atlantis_(Homo_mermanus). Note the bifurcation: there is a Pacific Lemuria ruled by the Deviants (the Eternals/Deviants split, important to the *Eternals* mythology and *Celestials* arcs) and a separate, later Lemurian *Homo mermanus* offshoot ruled by Naga and later Llyra. The two are structurally distinct nodes that share a name.

## 5. Inhuman Geography: Attilan's Many Locations

Attilan is the Inhuman capital, and it is the most-relocated sovereign entity in the Marvel canon. The relocation chronology is:

1. Original site "in the North Atlantic Ocean just off the island continent of Atlantis" [T3] Marvel Database, "Attilan," https://marvel.fandom.com/wiki/Attilan.
2. Relocation to the Himalayas: "The Inhumans later came to Earth and placed the location of Attilan within the Himalayas, but remained hidden from the human race while placing a reflected force field around their hidden city" [T3] Marvel Database, "Attilan".
3. Relocation to the Blue Area of the Moon: "When pollution on Earth became too deadly to the Inhumans, they sought the aid of the Fantastic Four to help relocate their home of Attilan, and with the assistance of Mister Fantastic, the entire city was transported to the Blue Area" [T3] Marvel Database, "Attilan". This is the Kree-built region with breathable atmosphere, key to the *Kree-Skrull War* and *Inhumans vs. X-Men*.
4. Brief residence over Hala (Kree homeworld) after a Skrull attack: "a machine developed by Maximus was utilized to send Attilan into space, and once in space, the Inhumans arrived at Hala, where Attilan then settled hovering above the Kree capital city" [T3] Marvel Database, "Attilan".
5. Return to Earth, hovering over the Hudson River / NYC.
6. Destruction by Black Bolt during *Infinity* (2013): "Black Bolt attacked Thanos with his scream, destroying Attilan in the process" [T3] Marvel Database, "Attilan". This is the destruction that scatters Terrigen Mist across Earth and triggers the *Inhumanity*/NuHumans era.
7. Rebuilt as "New Attilan" off the coast of Manhattan, then later relocated again post-IvX.

This trajectory makes Attilan the structurally clearest case of "movable sovereignty" in the Marvel map.

## 6. Hidden Civilizations and Pocket Dimensions

These are sovereignty-bearing entities that exist outside ordinary Earth geography but anchor major arcs.

**Savage Land** is "a fictional prehistoric land ... a tropical preserve, hidden in Antarctica. It is located at the base of the Palmer Peninsula and surrounded by deep volcanoes" [T2] Wikipedia, "Savage Land," https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Savage_Land. Origin: "The Savage Land was created by the alien Nuwali at the behest of the Beyonders, who sought to observe the process of evolution under relatively controlled conditions. The Nuwali chose a valley in Antarctica surrounded by active volcanoes during the Triassic period and installed advanced technological devices to maintain a tropical climate, then stocked the area with all manner of Earth life including dinosaurs and prehistoric mammals" [T2] Wikipedia, "Savage Land". Sovereignty in the Savage Land is informal; Ka-Zar (Kevin Plunder) is its de facto protector. The Savage Land's strategic importance to Earth-616 derives from its Antarctic Vibranium (a different isotope than Wakandan Vibranium), the Mutate genetic experiments of Magneto and Sauron, and its periodic role as a hideout / escape hatch in X-Men, Avengers, and Spider-Man arcs.

**Pangea** is a sub-region of the Savage Land, also called "Atlantean for 'paradise.'" Per Marvel Database: "When the Atlanteans of pre-Cataclysmic Atlantis arrived in the Savage Land, they decided to make this unspoiled land part of their empire. They found the Nuwali technology that sustains the Savage Land and keeps it as a tropical paradise. Atlantean scientists extended the tropical effect, creating a recreation/commerce center called 'Pangea,' then genetically altered Man-Apes into humanoid versions of birds, monkeys, fish, and other animals" [T3] Marvel Database, "Pangea (Savage Land)," https://marvel.fandom.com/wiki/Pangea_(Savage_Land). Pangea was destroyed in *Avengers #257* by Terminus: "the next issue confirms that while the Savage Land is now mostly gone, Pangea is ALL gone" [T3] CBR, "How Ka-Zar's Pangea Plot Was Resolved," https://www.cbr.com/avengers-ka-zar-savage-land-pangea/. The Savage Land was later restored; Pangea was not.

**K'un-Lun** is "a mystical city located in a pocket dimension and is one of the Seven Capital Cities of Heaven. The gate to K'un-Lun can be accessed from China once every 15 years. The Iron Fist serves as the guardian of the gate to K'un-Lun and the city's protector" [T3] Marvel Database, "K'un-Lun," https://marvel.fandom.com/wiki/K'un-Lun. The expanded geography emerged in Fraction/Brubaker/Aja's *Immortal Iron Fist* (2007), which introduced the full set: "K'un-Lun is one of the Seven Capital Cities of Heaven alongside K'un-Zi, Tiger Island, Peng Lai, Kingdom of Spiders, Z'Gambo, Under City, and Eighth City" [T3] Marvel Database, "Glossary:Seven Capital Cities of Heaven," https://marvel.fandom.com/wiki/Glossary:Seven_Capital_Cities_of_Heaven. Every 88 years they merge into the Heart of Heaven, where each city's Immortal Weapon competes in the Tournament of Champions. This is a textbook example of a major retcon-by-expansion: a single hidden city becomes seven, deepening but not contradicting prior continuity.

## 7. Madripoor: The Crime-State Node

Madripoor is the structurally important "shadow Singapore" of the Marvel map. It is "located in maritime Southeast Asia, and more specifically, ... in the southern portion of the Strait of Malacca between Singapore and Sumatra, Indonesia. Madripoor is a fictional location apparently modeled on Singapore, as they are both wealthy maritime Asian island port nations at a strategic location with a single major city" [T2] Wikipedia, "Madripoor," https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madripoor. Internal geography is bifurcated: "Madripoor is divided between a Hightown and Lowtown, places which are wealthy and crime-stricken respectively" [T3] Marvel Database, "Madripoor," https://marvel.fandom.com/wiki/Madripoor. The Princess Bar in Lowtown is the operational hub, "first appeared in print in September 1988, created by Chris Claremont and John Buscema for Marvel Comics Presents #1," and historically run by Wolverine under the alias "Patch" [T3] Marvel Database, "Princess Bar," https://marvel.fandom.com/wiki/Princess_Bar.

Madripoor's sovereignty is nominally a constitutional state, but in practice it is run by criminal syndicates. The Prince/Princess role has been held by Roughhouse, Tyger Tiger, Viper (Madame Hydra), and during the Krakoan Age, the Marauders / Homines Verendi factions struggled over Lowtown — "the Marauders bought a bar in Lowtown as part of their plan to prevent the Homines Verendi from razing the place and kicking out the locals" [T3] Marvel Database, "Princess Bar". Madripoor's structural function is as the no-extradition free port that hosts crossover crime arcs (Wolverine solo, Falcon, Patch, X-Force).

## 8. Altered New York: The Real-City Layer

Earth-616 also has heavy layering on real cities, dominated by New York. "Avengers Mansion is located in the Upper East Side on Fifth Avenue ... 721 (or 890) Fifth Avenue" [T3] Marvel Database, "Avengers Mansion," https://marvel.fandom.com/wiki/Avengers_Mansion. The Baxter Building (Fantastic Four) sits on Madison Avenue. "Hell's Kitchen, also known as Clinton and Midtown West, is a neighborhood located in the borough of Manhattan, New York City, ... One of Hell's Kitchen's most famed residents is Matt Murdock, better known by his vigilante alias, Daredevil" [T3] Marvel Database, "Hell's Kitchen," https://marvel.fandom.com/wiki/Hell's_Kitchen. Post-2020, the FF's headquarters shifted to "4 Yancy Street ... an entire building owned by Ben Grimm, located in the Lower East Side ... Mister Fantastic folded additional dimensions inside the building, making its interior square footage roughly twice of that of the Baxter Building while on the outside its appearance remained unchanged" [T3] Marvel Database, "4 Yancy Street," https://marvel.fandom.com/wiki/4_Yancy_Street.

Two events repeatedly reshape NYC's Marvel map: the *Civil War II* / *Secret Empire* damage cycle, and superhuman-disaster zones (the *Inferno* demonic incursion of 1989, the Brooklyn destruction in *World War Hulk*). Manhattan also became "Battleworld's New York" during *Secret Wars* (2015) and was reset.

## 9. Structural Patterns Across the Geography

Several structural patterns recur in the Earth-616 map:

- **Movable sovereignty.** Attilan, Krakoa, and Atlantis have all relocated multiple times. The "nation" is a polity, not a fixed location.
- **Twin / split entities.** Krakoa↔Arakko (split from Okkara), Atlantis↔Lemuria (Cataclysm fork), Pacific-Krakoa↔Atlantic-Krakoa during Fall of X. Marvel often uses bifurcation as a retcon engine.
- **Destruction-and-replacement.** Genosha (massacred 2001), Pangea (destroyed *Avengers #257*), Attilan (destroyed *Infinity* 2013), Krakoa (Fall of X 2024). In each case, a successor or memorial node replaces the lost geography.
- **Hidden-then-revealed.** Wakanda's "isolationist" status, the Savage Land's Triassic preserve, and K'un-Lun's once-every-15-years gate all encode the same trope: an off-grid polity that surfaces during crossover events.
- **Resource-defined geography.** Wakandan Vibranium, Antarctic Vibranium, Madripoorian crime-finance, Atlantean trench resources, and Krakoan pharmaceuticals all anchor the political-economic map of Earth-616.
