# Major Marvel Continuities — Structural Map

This file catalogs the structurally important Marvel continuities and how they sit relative to one another in the multiversal map. The focus is structural, not biographical: when each continuity was founded, what defines it against the "main" Earth-616 line, what its current status is (active / dormant / destroyed / merged / rebuilt), and which other realities it generates or interacts with.

A core point underlies the whole map: the very idea that Marvel realities have "Earth-#####" coordinates is itself a retrofit, originating in Captain Britain at Marvel UK in 1983 and only later becoming the unifying schema imposed across Marvel's prior multiverse stories (What If?, 2099, Age of Apocalypse, Heroes Reborn, etc.) [T3] Marvel Database, "Earth-616" entry, https://marvel.fandom.com/wiki/Earth-616. Earth designations are therefore archaeological as much as cartographic — they document how Marvel has come to *organize* its publishing history, not just where stories happen.

## 1. Earth-616 — The "Prime" Marvel Universe

Earth-616 is the in-house numerical designation for the mainline Marvel Comics continuity launched in 1961 with *Fantastic Four* #1, but the *number* is far younger than the universe it labels. The designation was first put into print in "Rough Justice," a Captain Britain story by Alan Moore and Alan Davis in *The Daredevils* #7, July 1983 (Marvel UK) [T3] Wikipedia, "Earth-616," https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Earth-616. Alan Davis later said previous Captain Britain writer Dave Thorpe had coined "616" as part of the strip's internal lore; Thorpe in 2019 confirmed to Bleeding Cool that the number was "666 minus 50," with the Number-of-the-Beast joke originally intended for the Crooked World of *Jaspers' Warp*, not the mainline Marvel reality [T3] Bleeding Cool, "What Does Marvel 616 Actually Mean?", https://bleedingcool.com/comics/what-does-marvel-616-actually-mean-we-explain-again/. Once Moore wrote it into Saturnyne's mouth in *Daredevils* #7, "616" stuck to the prime line and was retroactively absorbed into Marvel's Captain Britain Corps → Multiverse cosmology [T3] Marvel.com, "The Origin Story of Earth-616 As Told by Its Creator," https://www.marvel.com/articles/comics/the-origin-story-of-earth-616-as-told-by-its-creator-writer-david-thorpe.

Structurally, Earth-616 is the reality OHOTMU and the *Marvel Encyclopedia* treat as "default" — every alternate Earth is defined by deviation from it [T2] *Marvel Encyclopedia* (DK), "Multiverse" entry. Status: **active and prime**, but with two important asterisks. It was annihilated alongside Earth-1610 in the final incursion that opened *Secret Wars* (2015), and was rebuilt by Reed Richards using the Beyonders' power transferred via Molecule Man and Doctor Doom. Marvel's editorial framing post-2015 is that the rebuilt prime universe is *still* Earth-616 (i.e., the designation survived the death of its referent), and that Miles Morales and a handful of other Ultimate refugees are now natives of this rebuilt 616 [T3] Marvel.com, "The Official Marvel Guide to Every 'Secret Wars'," https://www.marvel.com/articles/comics/secret-wars-official-marvel-guide.

## 2. Earth-1610 — The Original Ultimate Universe (and Its 2024 Successor, Earth-6160)

The Ultimate line launched in 2000 with *Ultimate Spider-Man* #1 (Bendis / Bagley) as a deliberate jumping-on imprint for new readers, structurally separated from the dense Earth-616 backstory. Marvel later assigned its in-house number Earth-1610 [T3] Wikipedia, "Ultimate Marvel," https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ultimate_Marvel. Defining differences from 616: a younger Peter Parker (2000s teenager, not 1962 graduate), an Ultimates rather than Avengers framing, an explicitly post-9/11 Nick Fury modeled on Samuel L. Jackson, mutants as bioweapon programs rather than evolutionary phenomena, and a tighter "this happened on Tuesday" continuity timeline [T3] TV Tropes, "Ultimate Marvel," https://tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/ComicBook/UltimateMarvel.

The Ultimate Universe took heavy structural damage in *Cataclysm* (2013), in which Earth-616's Galactus crossed over and consumed parts of it [T3] Dexerto, "Ultimate Marvel Comics explained," https://www.dexerto.com/comics/ultimate-marvel-comics-explained-what-happened-to-the-original-earth-1610-2477949/. Earth-1610 was then destroyed outright in the final incursion of Hickman's *New Avengers* run, depicted in *Secret Wars* (2015) #1, when 1610 and 616 collided as the last two universes of Marvel's collapsing multiverse [T3] Wikipedia, "Secret Wars (2015 comic book)," https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secret_Wars_(2015_comic_book). Status of original Earth-1610: **destroyed (2015)**; a small set of survivors — Miles Morales, the Maker (a corrupted Ultimate Reed Richards), and a handful of others — were transplanted into the rebuilt 616 [T3] Marvel.com, "The Official Marvel Guide to Every 'Secret Wars'," https://www.marvel.com/articles/comics/secret-wars-official-marvel-guide.

In 2023–24, Jonathan Hickman and Bryan Hitch's *Ultimate Invasion* depicted the Maker constructing a *new* Ultimate Earth in his image, with a radically altered geopolitical history. This world is **not** Earth-1610 but the new in-house designation **Earth-6160**, launching with *Ultimate Spider-Man* #1 in January 2024 (Hickman / Checchetto) and an imprint slate including *Ultimate Black Panther*, *Ultimate X-Men*, *Ultimates*, etc. [T3] Comic Book Herald, "The New Marvel Ultimate Universe 2.0 (2024 to 2026)," https://www.comicbookherald.com/the-complete-marvel-reading-order-guide/ultimate-marvel-universe-reading-order/new-marvel-ultimate-universe-2/. Marvel has publicly committed to a **fixed end date** in April 2026 — i.e., the Ultimate 2.0 imprint was designed from the start as a finite, self-contained continuity with a programmed conclusion, an unusual structural decision for a major-line imprint [T3] Wikipedia, "Ultimate Universe," https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ultimate_Universe.

So the structural picture is: Earth-1610 (2000–2015, destroyed) and Earth-6160 (2024–2026, finite-by-design) are *separate* realities sharing an imprint name and a single bridging character (the Maker).

## 3. Earth-199999 / "Earth-616" — The Marvel Cinematic Universe

The MCU's prime reality was first labeled in a comics handbook: *Official Handbook of the Marvel Universe A-Z, Volume 5* (hardcover, 2008), which assigned the live-action film universe the designation Earth-199999 [T2] *Official Handbook of the Marvel Universe A-Z* HC Vol. 5 (2008), MCU entry; cited in [T3] Wikipedia, "Multiverse (Marvel Cinematic Universe)," https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multiverse_(Marvel_Cinematic_Universe). The number was reused intra-diegetically in *Young Avengers* Vol. 2 #8 (Loki's multiversal passport stamp) and on screen in *Spider-Man: Across the Spider-Verse* (2023) [T3] Marvel Database, "Earth-199999," https://marvel.fandom.com/wiki/Earth-199999.

The on-screen MCU itself, however, has retconned this label. In *Doctor Strange in the Multiverse of Madness* (2022), America Chavez and Stephen Strange explicitly designate the MCU prime reality "Earth-616" — aligning it with the comics' prime universe rather than the handbook's 199999 [T3] Wikipedia, "Multiverse (Marvel Cinematic Universe)," https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multiverse_(Marvel_Cinematic_Universe). Marvel Studios producer Nate Moore had been using "616" internally since at least November 2021. The result is a designation collision: comics-side handbooks still use 199999 for the MCU; on-screen MCU calls itself 616. Both are in active circulation depending on which canon is being cited [T3] Screen Rant, "Earth 616 or 199999? The MCU's Universe Number Finally Has An Official Answer," https://screenrant.com/earth-616-or-19999-the-mcus-universe-number-finally-has-an-official-answer/.

The MCU's multiverse is *organized* by the Sacred Timeline / TVA framework introduced in *Loki* season 1 (2021): a single curated "spine" (the Sacred Timeline) plus pruned branches, until Sylvie's killing of He Who Remains shatters the system and produces an open multiverse [T3] Wikipedia, "Multiverse (Marvel Cinematic Universe)," https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multiverse_(Marvel_Cinematic_Universe). Notable adjacent realities the MCU has officially absorbed via designation:

- **Earth-838** — the Illuminati universe in *Multiverse of Madness* (2022) [T3] same Wikipedia entry.
- **Earth-10005** — the 20th Century Fox X-Men films, retroactively designated by Marvel Comics; brought into MCU canon by *Deadpool & Wolverine* (2024), where the universe is depicted as deteriorating after Wolverine's death in *Logan* [T3] Marvel Cinematic Universe Wiki, "Earth-10005," https://marvelcinematicuniverse.fandom.com/wiki/Earth-10005.
- **Earth-96283** — Sam Raimi's Spider-Man trilogy, designated by Marvel Comics; canonized into MCU continuity by *Spider-Man: No Way Home* (2021) [T3] Marvel Database, "Earth-96283," https://marvel.fandom.com/wiki/Earth-96283.
- **Earth-120703** — Marc Webb's *Amazing Spider-Man* duology, also pulled into the MCU via *No Way Home* [T3] Wikipedia, "Multiverse (Marvel Cinematic Universe)," https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multiverse_(Marvel_Cinematic_Universe).
- **Earth-828** — the Fantastic Four reality of *The Fantastic Four: First Steps* (2025); the number derives from Jack Kirby's birthday (28 August) [T3] same Wikipedia entry.

Status: **active and central**, with the Multiverse Saga structurally treating multiverse traffic as the primary plot engine post-*Endgame*.

## 4. Earth-2149 — Marvel Zombies

Mark Millar introduced Earth-2149 in *Ultimate Fantastic Four* #21–23 ("Crossover," 2005) as a one-off backdrop, where a "Hunger" plague had turned the entire heroic population into intelligent, cannibalistic undead [T3] Wikipedia, "Marvel Zombies," https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marvel_Zombies. Marvel then commissioned Robert Kirkman (then writing *Walking Dead* at Image) and Sean Phillips to do *Marvel Zombies* (2005–06) as a five-issue miniseries set on the same Earth, which became a sustained sub-franchise [T3] Wikipedia, "Marvel Zombies (comic book)," https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marvel_Zombies_(comic_book).

Defining differences from 616: nearly all heroes are infected; the planet's biosphere is consumed; what remains is a bleak survival/horror frame. Structurally, Earth-2149 is interesting because it became a *recurring node* in Spider-Verse-style multiverse stories rather than a one-off — Marvel later spun off Earth-91126 (an Army of Darkness/zombies crossover reality) and used 2149 as a stable multiversal hazard for incursion plots [T3] Marvel Database, "Zombies (Earth-2149)," https://marvel.fandom.com/wiki/Zombies_(Earth-2149). The 2023 MCU animated *Marvel Zombies* series on Disney+ extends the concept into the cinematic multiverse [T3] Comic Book Club, "'Marvel Zombies': Digging up the History," https://comicbookclublive.com/2025/09/23/marvel-zombies-history-comics-disney-plus-show/. Status: **active recurring node** in 616-side multiverse storytelling.

## 5. Earth-65 — Spider-Gwen / Ghost-Spider

Earth-65 was created with a single character in mind: Gwen Stacy as Spider-Woman, in *Edge of Spider-Verse* #2 (Sept. 2014), by Jason Latour and Robbi Rodriguez, as part of Dan Slott's *Spider-Verse* event [T3] Wikipedia, "Spider-Woman (Gwen Stacy)," https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spider-Woman_(Gwen_Stacy). Defining differences from 616: Gwen, not Peter, is bitten by the radioactive spider; Peter Parker dies as the Lizard; Matt Murdock is Kingpin's lawyer and unambiguously a villain; Frank Castle is a clean-cut NYPD detective; the world's pop culture is shifted (Mary Janes are a band) [T3] Marvel.com, "Welcome to Earth-65, Ghost-Spider's Homeworld," https://www.marvel.com/articles/comics/ghost-spider-earth-65-homeworld-explained-differences-similarities.

Structurally, Earth-65 is a key case of a single-character reality that **outgrew its origin**: the *Spider-Gwen* (later *Ghost-Spider*) ongoing launched in 2015; Gwen earned the "Ghost-Spider" alias after Spider-Geddon; she was eventually folded into the rebuilt 616 as a regular cast member [T3] Comicbook.com, "Spider-Gwen Moves to Marvel 616," https://comicbook.com/comics/news/marvel-spider-gwen-the-ghost-spider-gwen-stacy-earth-65-616/. Earth-65 is also a cornerstone of the Spider-Verse cosmology that Sony's *Into/Across the Spider-Verse* films (Earth-65 → Gwen → multiverse hub) built on. Status: **active**, with both the home reality and 616-resident incarnations maintained in parallel.

## 6. Earth-928 — Marvel 2099

Marvel 2099 launched in 1992 as a future-facing imprint, with *Spider-Man 2099* #1 by Peter David and Rick Leonardi as the flagship [T3] Wikipedia, "Spider-Man 2099," https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spider-Man_2099. Setting: a cyberpunk North America in the year 2099, run by megacorporations (Alchemax foremost), with the original Heroic Age a faded religion. Defining differences from 616: corporate-feudal social order, the Public Eye private police force, a "Thor" who is genuinely Thor versus a legacy hero, and a "Hulk" descended from the Banner line. Originally framed as **a possible future of Earth-616**, then retconned into its own designation Earth-928 [T3] M2099 Wiki, "Earth-928," https://m2099.fandom.com/wiki/Earth-928.

Structurally, the imprint had a bumpy life: Peter David resigned *Spider-Man 2099* with #44 (early 1996) in solidarity with his editor; the 2099 line collapsed shortly after into *2099: World of Tomorrow* and the *Apocalypse / Doom 2099* finale [T3] Wikipedia, "Marvel 2099," https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marvel_2099. Modern reuse (Slott's *Spider-Verse*, *Spider-Geddon Handbook*, the 2019 *2099* relaunch) has been internally inconsistent — *Marvel Database* notes that several details after the original David run "do not match" later 2099 stories, but Marvel still treats them as the same Earth-928 [T3] Marvel Database, "Earth-928," https://marvel.fandom.com/wiki/Earth-928. Status: **dormant most years, periodically reactivated** as 616's official far-future.

## 7. Earth-92131 — The 1990s Marvel Animated Universe and *X-Men '97*

Earth-92131 collects the loosely shared continuity of Marvel's 1990s Saturday-morning cartoons: *X-Men: The Animated Series* (1992–97), *Spider-Man: The Animated Series* (1994–98), *Iron Man* (1994–96), *Fantastic Four* (1994–96), *The Incredible Hulk* (1996–97), and *Silver Surfer* (1998) [T3] Marvel Database, "Earth-92131," https://marvel.fandom.com/wiki/Earth-92131. The X-Men and Spider-Man cartoons in particular cross over (the *Secret Wars* 2-parter on Spider-Man Animated explicitly pulls X-Men characters from the same world).

In 2024 Marvel Studios Animation revived this continuity directly with *X-Men '97*, which is canonical to Earth-92131 — i.e., a *direct sequel* to the 1992 cartoon, **not** part of the MCU (Earth-199999/616) [T3] Wikipedia, "X-Men '97," https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/X-Men_'97. Earlier comics intended as a 92131 continuation — *X-Men '92* and *X-Men '92* (Vol. 2), 2015–17 — were displaced when '97 was greenlit and are now assigned the new designation Earth-15730 [T3] Marvel Database, "Earth-92131," https://marvel.fandom.com/wiki/Earth-92131. Defining differences from 616: a flatter mutant-rights-allegory framing, a different roster baseline (Storm, Wolverine, Cyclops, Jean, Rogue, Beast, Gambit, Jubilee), and a stable status quo at Xavier's mansion that movies/comics rarely sustain. Status: **active**, with *X-Men '97* season 2 in production.

## 8. Heroes Reborn (1996) and "Heroes Reborn" (2021) — Pocket Universes as Editorial Reset Tools

The original 1996–97 *Heroes Reborn* was an editorial reset: at the end of *Onslaught* (1996), the Avengers, Fantastic Four, and several other heroes "died" stopping the Onslaught entity, then were transplanted by Franklin Richards into a pocket universe (initially called Counter-Earth, later catalogued as part of "Dimension F" and various Earth designations) where Image Comics founders (Jim Lee, Rob Liefeld) restarted *Avengers* and *Fantastic Four* from #1 with retold origins [T3] Wikipedia, "Heroes Reborn (1996 comic)," https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heroes_Reborn_(1996_comic). Structurally this is the cleanest "pocket universe as soft reboot of a sub-imprint" Marvel has ever published — it ran one year, then *Heroes Return* folded the heroes back into 616 [T3] Marvel Database, "Heroes Reborn (1996 Event)," https://marvel.fandom.com/wiki/Heroes_Reborn_(1996_Event).

In 2021, Marvel reused the brand for a wholly different conceit: Jason Aaron's *Heroes Reborn* event, in which Phil Coulson sells his soul to Mephisto for the Pandemonium Cube and reshapes 616 into a reality where the Avengers never assembled and the Squadron Supreme are Earth's premier heroes. This reality is designated **Earth-21798** and exists as an in-fiction warp of 616 rather than a parallel pocket [T3] Marvel Database, "Earth-21798," https://marvel.fandom.com/wiki/Earth-21798. Status of both: **dormant / closed events**, but they remain useful catalog points for how Marvel uses pocket-universe and reality-warp framings as editorial tools.

## 9. *What If?* — The Branch-Point Engine That Generates the Multiverse

*What If?* Vol. 1 (1977–84) under Roy Thomas's editorship established the core grammar Marvel still uses: a Watcher (Uatu) narrates from outside time, picks a single divergence point in 616 history, and walks the consequences forward [T3] Wikipedia, "What If (comics)," https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/What_If_(comics). Most original-run issues had no explicit Earth-number; OHOTMU and the *Marvel Encyclopedia* later assigned designations retroactively. Examples from the catalog: Earth-772 ("What if Spider-Man had joined the Fantastic Four?"), Earth-774 ("…Bruce Banner's mind in the Hulk?"), Earth-7712 ("…Fantastic Four had different powers?") [T3] Wikipedia, "List of What If issues," https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_What_If_issues; Marvel Encyclopedia entries.

Structurally, *What If?* is what licenses the entire Earth-#### scheme: any divergence creates an alternate Earth, every alternate Earth is a What-If with editorial weight added. Some What-If branches calcified into recurring realities — most importantly **Earth-295 (Age of Apocalypse)**, which began as Legion's accidental killing of his father in the past (1995's *X-Men: Alpha*) and was originally a four-month line-wide replacement of all X-titles before being filed as a permanent alternate [T3] Wikipedia, "Age of Apocalypse," https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Earth_295. Another is **Earth-9997 (Earth X)**, the 30-years-future Alex Ross / Jim Krueger trilogy (1999–2002), notable because OHOTMU explicitly flags its "revelations" as in-universe-suspect because they were narrated by a corrupt Uatu and Mephisto — i.e., Marvel built the metafictional escape hatch directly into the handbook entry [T3] Marvel Database, "Earth-9997," https://marvel.fandom.com/wiki/Earth-9997.

The Disney+ animated *What If…?* (2021–24) imports this model into the MCU, with The Watcher narrating branches off the Sacred Timeline; structurally it is the MCU equivalent of *What If?* Vol. 1 [T3] Wikipedia, "Multiverse (Marvel Cinematic Universe)," https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multiverse_(Marvel_Cinematic_Universe).

## 10. The 2015 Reset and the Current Multiversal Map

The single most structurally important event for this catalog is *Secret Wars* (2015) by Jonathan Hickman and Esad Ribić. The setup, built across Hickman's *Avengers* / *New Avengers* run starting 2012, was that the Marvel multiverse was contracting via "incursions" — collisions of two parallel Earths whose only resolution was the destruction of one or both [T3] Nerdist, "What Are 'Multiverse Incursions' in Marvel Comics Lore?", https://nerdist.com/article/incursions-explained-marvel-comics-doctor-strange-multiverse-of-madness/. The incursion engine was driven by the Beyonders. The final incursion in *Secret Wars* #1 was Earth-616 vs Earth-1610; both annihilated; Doctor Doom (with Doctor Strange and Molecule Man) seized the Beyonders' power and stitched a patchwork **Battleworld** out of surviving fragments, including domains drawn from *Age of Apocalypse*, *Marvel Zombies*, *2099*, *Old Man Logan*, *House of M*, and various What-Ifs [T3] Marvel Database, "Secret Wars (2015 Event)," https://marvel.fandom.com/wiki/Secret_Wars_(2015_Event); [T3] Marvel.com, "Secret Wars and Battleworld Explained," https://www.marvel.com/articles/comics/secret-wars-and-battleworld-explained.

Battleworld resolved when the Beyonders' power was transferred to Reed Richards, who rebuilt the multiverse. Editorial framing: the rebuilt prime universe is *still* Earth-616 (continuity preserved), while the rebuilt multiverse is functionally similar to the pre-collapse one but with **deliberate carry-forward changes** — Miles Morales, the Maker, a few other Earth-1610 natives, and revised Inhumans / Old Man Logan elements all now reside on 616 [T3] Marvel.com, "The Official Marvel Guide to Every 'Secret Wars'," https://www.marvel.com/articles/comics/secret-wars-official-marvel-guide. This is the structural reason the post-2015 line could publish *Spider-Men II*, *Champions*, the new *Ultimates*, etc., without a hard reboot: the incursion event provided cover for a soft reboot disguised as restoration.

The current (2026) map therefore looks roughly like this:

- **Prime axis:** Earth-616 (rebuilt 2015) ↔ Earth-6160 (the Maker's new Ultimate world, 2024–April 2026, finite-by-design).
- **Cinematic axis:** Earth-199999 / "MCU 616" plus a growing constellation of officially-canonized adjacent realities (10005, 96283, 120703, 838, 828, etc.), structured by the post-*Loki* free multiverse rather than the original Sacred Timeline.
- **Animated axis:** Earth-92131 reactivated by *X-Men '97*; Earth-15730 holding the displaced *X-Men '92* comics; *What If…?* MCU branches accumulating.
- **Recurring nodes:** Earth-2149 (Zombies), Earth-65 (Ghost-Spider), Earth-928 (2099), Earth-295 (Age of Apocalypse), Earth-9997 (Earth X) — none currently host an ongoing line, all available as multiverse hazards or guest realities.
- **Closed pockets:** original 1996 Heroes Reborn pocket (folded back); Earth-21798 (2021 Heroes Reborn warp, restored); original Earth-1610 (destroyed 2015, partially evacuated to 616).

The throughline across all of this is that Marvel uses *both* "the multiverse expanded" (incursions, Spider-Verse, MCU multiverse traffic) *and* "we ran the multiverse through a reset" (*Secret Wars* 2015) as structural tools. Earth-#### designations are the catalog system that lets the same brand re-run the same alternate-reality conceit — Heroes Reborn, Ultimate, Zombies, 2099 — every decade or so without committing to a single canonical map.
